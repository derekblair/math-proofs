\section{Abstract}
%======================================================================

We prove a scoped CRT escape theorem: for a finite family of
progressions with $8\mid A_i$, suppose every residue $B_i$ either is
nonsquare modulo some odd prime divisor of $A_i$, or is not congruent
to $1$ modulo $8$. Then infinitely many primes in each class
$r\in\{1,121,169,289,361,529\}\pmod{840}$ avoid all the progressions.
This theorem is formalized as \texttt{es\_crt\_escape}, using Mathlib's
proved general Dirichlet theorem and no custom axioms.

The intended Erd\H{o}s--Straus application concerns identities
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:identityform}
  \frac{4}{A_it+B_i} = \sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{F_{ij}(t)},
  \qquad F_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}[t],
\end{equation}
as rational-function identities, with positive leading coefficients and
$A_i>0$. The implication from such
integer-polynomial identities to the required local obstructions is
an external mathematical input, not proved or assumed as a Lean axiom
here. Consequently the full polynomial-identity barrier is not claimed
as a formalized theorem. The reported $21{,}296$-family census, greedy
coverage values, and witness prime $77{,}598{,}049$ are retained as
external computational data, separate from the kernel-checked theorem.

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================

The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asserts
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:es}
  \frac{4}{n} = \frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}+\frac{1}{z},
  \qquad n \geq 2,\ x,y,z \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}.
\end{equation}
Modulo $840$, the identities of Mordell, Yamamoto, and Rosati dispose of
every residue class except the six square classes
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:squareclasses}
  r \in \{1,121,169,289,361,529\} \pmod{840},
\end{equation}
the invertible quadratic residue classes modulo $840$. A long-running
program has attacked
these classes with \emph{finite covering systems}: finitely many
polynomial identities \eqref{eq:identityform}, each covering an arithmetic
progression, whose union would cover a whole class. The reported
computational attempt, over $21{,}296$ discovered families, covers
$73.5$--$84.9\%$ of each class (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} explains why a finite family satisfying the
explicit local obstructions cannot cover a full square class.
Applying that conclusion to every integer-polynomial identity requires
the separate cited obstruction discussed below. It is not discharged
by the present Lean formalization. Figure~\ref{fig:construction}
outlines the checked construction, while Figure~\ref{fig:witness}
records the externally computed example.

\begin{assumption}[External polynomial-identity input]
  \label{ass:lemma}
  The polynomial-identity application invokes the cited integer-polynomial
  obstruction recorded in Assumption~\ref{lem:schinzel}
  (Schinzel 1956; Mordell 1967~\cite{schinzel1956,mordell1967}).
  Its exact bibliographic statement and hypotheses have not been verified
  here. It is not an axiom in the corrected Lean module.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Scope]
  \label{ass:scope}
  The checked theorem starts with explicit local obstructions and
  $8\mid A_i$. It neither supplies them from~\eqref{eq:identityform}
  nor formalizes a reduction from arbitrary progression moduli to
  moduli divisible by eight. Any polynomial-identity barrier derived
  from it remains conditional on those steps. Per-prime infinite
  constructions are outside this finite-family statement.
\end{assumption}

\section{Methods}
%======================================================================
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Family discovery}

Identity families were discovered by exhaustive search in Mordell's
splitting-lemma framework (numeric lane: $(a,b)$ with $a+b=mh$; symbolic
lane: $(d,n)$ or $(d,x)$), at bounds $\mathrm{CMAX}=64$,
$\mathrm{ABMAX}=2500$, $h \in \{1,\dots,5\}$ (supplied datum;
\url{erdos_straus_r16_discover.py}). The run produced
$\mathbf{21{,}296}$ families ($20{,}976$ numeric $+$ $320$ symbolic) with
$0$ identity failures on $20$-sample exact checks, of which
$\mathbf{6{,}388}$ are prime-covering progressions (supplied data;
\url{erdos_straus_r16_families.json}). Of the $6{,}388$ progressions,
$5{,}965$ satisfy $\gcd(A_i,B_i)=1$. The original computation labels
the remaining $423$ as containing no primes; that data-dependent claim
is not checked here. Noncoprimality alone only shows that at most the
prime divisors of the common gcd could occur.

\subsection{Greedy covering computation}

For each hard cell, a greedy set-cover over residue classes was computed
with free-family closure (moduli dividing the current $M$ cost nothing)
plus a blowup step scored by gain$/\log(\text{blowup})$
(\url{erdos_straus_r16_cover2.py}). Coverage was decided exactly by
residue counting modulo $M = \mathrm{lcm}(35, \text{family moduli})$; in
every cell $M = 3233230 = 2\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 11\cdot 13\cdot 17\cdot 19$.
An independent brute-force recount of uncovered residues mod $M$ for all
six cells matched the incremental computation exactly (e.g.\ cell $529$:
$13{,}962$ uncovered, coverage $0.848860$).

\subsection{Barrier construction}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} starts with the supplied
obstruction for each progression. For an odd-prime obstruction, choose
a nonzero square residue incompatible with $B_i$ and compatible with
the target class modulo $840$. For a 2-adic obstruction, the target
class already avoids $B_i$ modulo $8$. Chinese remaindering combines
the odd-prime conditions with the target class, and the general
Dirichlet theorem supplies infinitely many primes in the resulting
coprime residue class. None of these steps infers an obstruction from
a polynomial identity.

\subsection{Formalization note}

The checked theorem is \texttt{es\_crt\_escape} in
\texttt{Mathproofs/ESBarrier.lean}, using Lean~4.34.1 and
Mathlib~v4.34.1 (pinned commit \texttt{d13f23b}). Its hypotheses are
exactly the per-progression local obstructions and divisibility by eight
stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier}. The targeted build succeeds with
no sorrys; \texttt{\#print axioms es\_crt\_escape} returns only
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

The general Dirichlet theorem is already proved in this pinned Mathlib
as \texttt{Nat.infinite\_setOfPred\_prime\_and\_modEq}, in
\texttt{Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/PrimesInAP.lean}. It requires a
nonzero modulus, which the CRT construction supplies. The former custom
axioms and the unrestricted rational-polynomial \texttt{es\_barrier}
statement were removed after the October 23 review: a zero-modulus
instance made the former Dirichlet axiom inconsistent, and allowing
arbitrary rational denominator polynomials made the former obstruction
axiom false. The present module makes no polynomial-identity implication.
The committed PDF predates these corrections; see \texttt{BUILD.md}.

\subsection{The scoped CRT escape theorem}

\begin{assumption}[Cited integer-polynomial obstruction; unformalized]
  \label{lem:schinzel}
  The intended application cites the claim that an identity of the
  integer-polynomial form~\eqref{eq:identityform}, with positive leading
  coefficients, $A_i>0$, and coprime $A_i,B_i$, forces $B_i$ to be nonsquare
  modulo $A_i$. The references and exact scope remain to be verified.
  This cited mathematical input, and its required local consequence,
  are distinct from the Lean theorem below.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[CRT escape; PROVED]
  \label{thm:barrier}
  Let $(A_i,B_i)$ be finitely many pairs of natural numbers, with
  $8\mid A_i$ for every $i$. Suppose for each $i$ that either
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item some odd prime $q_i\mid A_i$ has $B_i$ nonsquare modulo $q_i$; or
    \item $B_i\not\equiv1\pmod8$.
  \end{enumerate}
  For each $r\in\{1,121,169,289,361,529\}$, infinitely many primes
  satisfy $p\equiv r\pmod{840}$ and $p\not\equiv B_i\pmod{A_i}$ for
  every $i$. In particular they lie outside every $A_i\mathbb{N}+B_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Each listed $r$ is an integer square coprime to $840$, and
  $r\equiv1\pmod8$. At each distinct odd witness prime $q$, choose
  $c_q=r\bmod q$ if $q\nmid r$, and $c_q=1$ otherwise. These are
  nonzero squares, so they differ from every nonsquare $B_i$ witnessed
  by that prime. If $q\mid840$, coprimality gives $q\nmid r$, hence
  compatibility with $r\pmod{840}$; if $q\mid r$, then $q\nmid840$.
  Chinese remaindering therefore supplies a residue $c$ modulo a
  positive common multiple $M$ of $840$ and the witness primes, with
  $c\equiv r\pmod{840}$, $c\equiv c_q\pmod q$, and $\gcd(c,M)=1$.
  Every 2-adic obstruction is already avoided by $c\equiv1\pmod8$.
  By Dirichlet's theorem infinitely many primes are $c\pmod M$.
  They avoid every progression: congruence to $B_i$ modulo $A_i$
  would contradict the associated odd-prime obstruction, or, since
  $8\mid A_i$, the 2-adic obstruction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reported numerical obstruction data}

The supplied computation describes $6{,}388$ prime-covering progressions
with $A_i=24m'$ and $B_i=24k_0+1\bmod A_i$. It reports a symbol value
$-1$ in all $5{,}965$ coprime cases and $+1$ in zero cases, calling this
symbol $(B_i/A_i)$ a Jacobi symbol. Since $A_i$ is even, the ordinary
Jacobi symbol does not have this denominator; the implementation's
extension or odd-part convention has not been checked here. The data
also report an odd prime $q_i\mid A_i$ with $(B_i/q_i)=-1$ for every
admissible family. Neither the data nor the cited general implication
are certified by the Lean module.

\subsection{Concrete witness prime}
\label{sec:witness}

The external computation reports the following application of the CRT
construction to its $30$-identity greedy system for the $529$ cell.
Its family data and obstruction certificates are not instantiated in
Lean here. The reported barrier class is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:barrierclass}
  p \equiv 529 \pmod{38798760},
\end{equation}
whose first prime is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:witness}
  p = 77{,}598{,}049 \quad\text{(deterministic Miller--Rabin)},
\end{equation}
with $p \equiv 529 \pmod{840}$, lying in $\mathbf{0}$ of the $30$
identity progressions (supplied data;
\url{erdos_straus_r16_barrier.json}). Figure~\ref{fig:witness}
illustrates the escape.

\section{Discussion}
%======================================================================

Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} excludes complete coverage by finite
families carrying the stated local obstructions. Extending this
conclusion to every integer-polynomial identity system still requires
the cited obstruction and any modulus-normalization argument; these
are not supplied by the formalization. The greedy data illustrate the
intended application but do not discharge its general mathematical input.

The theorem does not address per-prime infinite constructions
(Assumption~\ref{ass:scope}). The reported greedy set-cover is heuristic,
so the $73.5$--$84.9\%$ figures are achieved coverage values, not proved
maxima. The discovery census and the stated witness prime are external
computations, not additional conclusions of \texttt{es\_crt\_escape}.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================

The formalized result is CRT escape from explicit odd-prime or 2-adic
obstructions for progressions with $8\mid A_i$. It proves infinitely
many escaping primes in every listed square class modulo $840$, using
Mathlib's general Dirichlet theorem and standard axioms only. The
polynomial-identity-to-obstruction implication remains an unformalized
cited input. The reported family census, greedy coverage values, and
witness prime are preserved as computational context rather than
kernel-certified consequences.
