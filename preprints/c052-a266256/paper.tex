\begin{abstract}
Let $\ca : \N \times \Z \to \{0, 1\}$ denote the bi-infinite evolution of Wolfram's Elementary Cellular Automaton Rule~11 ($11 = 00001011_2$) starting from a single ON cell at the origin, $\ca(0, j) = \mathbf{1}_{j=0}$, and let $a(n) = \seq{A266256}(n)$ count the ON cells inside the active light-cone window $[-n, n]$ at generation $n \ge 0$. In December~2015 and April~2019, Colin Barker posted a three-part conjecture bundle on OEIS~\seq{A266256}: the closed form $a(n) = \bigl(-2((-1)^n - 1)n + 3(-1)^n + 1\bigr)/2$ for all $n > 0$, the four-step linear recurrence $a(n) = 2a(n-2) - a(n-4)$ for all $n > 4$, and the rational ordinary generating function $\sum_{n \ge 0} a(n)\,X^n = (1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4)/\bigl((1 - X)^2(1 + X)^2\bigr)$.

We prove Barker's full conjecture bundle unconditionally together with the bit-index truth-table identity, the explicit initial value $a(0) = 1$, and exact sharpness of both domain guards ($n = 0$ and $n = 4$). Unlike the defect-annihilating rules Rule~7 (\seq{A266220}/\seq{A266222}) and Rule~23 (\seq{A266437}/\seq{A266439}), whose single-seed defects vanish by $n = 2$, Rule~11 breaks left-right symmetry via the Boolean normal form $\ruleTT(l, c, r) = (\neg l) \land (\neg c \lor r)$ and sustains a \emph{persistent two-cell glider defect} at $\{n - 1, n\}$ moving rightward at speed~$1$ against a period-$2$ alternating exterior on all of $\Z$: for every $n \ge 1$ and $j \in \Z$, $\ca(n, j) = \operatorname{xor}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{2 \nmid n},\, \mathbf{1}_{j \in \{n-1, n\}}\bigr)$. Intersecting this global invariant with $[-n, n]$ yields the two-element ON pair $\{2m - 1, 2m\}$ ($a(2m) = 2$ for $m \ge 1$) and the contiguous ON interval $[-(2m+1), 2m - 1]$ ($a(2m + 1) = 4m + 1$ for $m \ge 0$), from which the closed form, four-step recurrence, and cleared-unit-denominator identity in $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$ follow directly. We further prove that the isolated-zero pattern $(1, 0, 1)$ never occurs in $\ca$, establishing exact space-time identity between Rules~11 and~43 (noted on \seq{A266253}), contrast Rule~11 with Rule~47 (\seq{A266662}), construct an $O(n^2)$ causal-cone mirror verifying $a(0), \dots, a(12)$ by pure kernel reduction, and formalize both light-cone and fixed-box zero-clamping traps. Every theorem is machine-checked in Lean~4 (\texttt{v4.34.1}, Mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7}) with zero \texttt{sorry}s, allowlist-only axioms, independent \texttt{leanchecker} kernel replay, and zero computational trust escapes.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Wolfram Rule 11 and OEIS \texorpdfstring{\seq{A266256}}{A266256}}

\subsection{Convention Audit and Formalization Traps}
\label{sec:conventions}

A first-hand audit of Robert Price's Mathematica program (\texttt{\%t}), offset (\texttt{\%O A266256 0,3}), and b-file \texttt{b266256.txt} ($n = 0, \dots, 999$)~\cite{oeis266256} establishes five conventions that must be preserved verbatim in any faithful specification:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Evolve-on-$\Z$-first semantics:} Price's program invokes \texttt{CellularAutomaton[11, \{\{1\},0\}, rows-1, \{All,All\}]} before slicing \texttt{Take[ca[[k]], \{rows-k+1, rows+k-1\}]}. Because $\ruleTT(0,0,0) = 1$ and $\ruleTT(1,1,1) = 0$, the uniform exterior $|j| > n$ flips $0 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 0 \mapsto \cdots$ on every step. Both natural zero-clamping shortcuts fail immediately:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Trap~A (light-cone zero-clamping):} clamping $|j| > m$ to $0$ between steps feeds $(0,0,1) \mapsto 1$ into $j = -2$ at step $n = 2$, turning row~$2$ from \texttt{00011} ($a(2) = 2$) into \texttt{10011} (count~$3$).
\item \emph{Trap~B (fixed-box $[-n, n]$ zero-clamping):} evolving on the fixed box $[-n, n]$ with zero exterior corrupts the bit profile already at $n = 2$ (\texttt{10010} vs.\ \texttt{00011}, where a spurious ON bit at $j = -2$ cancels a lost ON bit at $j = +2$) and fails in count at $n = 3$ (\texttt{0011100}, count $3 \ne 5 = a(3)$).
\end{itemize}
Both traps are formalized in Proposition~\ref{prop:clamping-traps}. Hence $\ca : \N \to \Z \to \mathsf{Bool}$ must evolve on all of $\Z$ first and restrict to $\mathrm{Icc}(-n, n)$ only inside $a(n)$.
\item \textbf{Wolfram bit ordering and chirality:} Bit $4l + 2c + r$ of $11$ is confirmed against \seq{A266253} row~$1 = \mathtt{100}$, \seq{A266254}$(1) = 100$, and \seq{A266255}$(1) = 4$ (reversing to $l + 2c + 4r$ gives mirror Rule~81, \seq{A266982}, with row~$1 = \mathtt{001}$). Both the explicit $8$-branch truth table $\ruleTT$ and its equivalence with $\mathrm{Nat.testBit}\;11\;(4l + 2c + r)$ are pinned verbatim.
\item \textbf{Exact guard boundaries:} Offset is $0$ with $a(0) = 1$ pinned explicitly. The guard $n > 0$ in the closed form is sharp because at $n = 0$ Barker's formula evaluates to $((-2)(0)(0) + 3 + 1)/2 = 2 \ne 1$. The guard $n > 4$ in the four-step recurrence is sharp because at $n = 4$ we have $2a(2) - a(0) = 2(2) - 1 = 3 \ne 2 = a(4)$ (creating the $-X^4$ term in the generating function numerator).
\item \textbf{Non-truncating arithmetic and unit-denominator formal power series:} The closed form clears the division by~$2$ in $\Z$ ($2 a(n) = -2((-1)^n - 1)n + 3(-1)^n + 1$), the recurrence casts $a(\cdot)$ to $\Z$ so $\N$-truncating subtraction cannot mask sign errors, and the generating function is stated in cleared-denominator form in $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$. Because $(1 - X)^2(1 + X)^2$ has constant coefficient $1 \ne 0$, it is a unit in $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:unit-inv}), making the cleared equation algebraically equivalent to the rational quotient.
\item \textbf{Scope boundary (companion sequences):} Companion row-encoding records \seq{A266254} and \seq{A266255}, together with Rule~47 companion \seq{A266662} ($a(n) = \seq{A266256}(n)$ for $n > 1$), are excluded from the pinned \seq{A266256} contract so there are zero cross-file sequence dependencies, while Remark~\ref{rem:rule47} explains structurally why Rule~47 merges with Rule~11 at $n = 2$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Main Theorem}

\begin{definition}[Rule~11, bi-infinite $\Z$-evolution, and $a(n) = \seq{A266256}(n)$]
\label{def:c052}
Define $\ruleTT : \mathsf{Bool}^3 \to \mathsf{Bool}$ by the explicit $8$-branch truth table mapping $(\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false})$, $(\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true})$, and $(\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}, \mathtt{true})$ to $\mathtt{true}$ and the remaining five triples to $\mathtt{false}$. Define the global evolution $\ca : \N \to \Z \to \mathsf{Bool}$ recursively by
\[
\ca(0, j) := \mathrm{decide}(j = 0), \qquad
\ca(n + 1, j) := \ruleTT\bigl(\ca(n, j - 1),\, \ca(n, j),\, \ca(n, j + 1)\bigr).
\]
For every $n \in \N$, define the ON-cell count on the active window $[-n, n] \subset \Z$ by
\[
a(n) := \bigl|\{j \in \Z \cap [-n, n] : \ca(n, j) = \mathtt{true}\}\bigr|.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem --- Resolution of Barker's \seq{A266256} Bundle]
\label{thm:main}
Let $\ruleTT$, $\ca$, and $a$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:c052}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(0)}] \textbf{Bit-index truth table:} For all $l, c, r \in \mathsf{Bool}$,
\[
\ruleTT(l, c, r) = \mathrm{Nat.testBit}\;11\;\bigl((\text{if } l \text{ then } 4 \text{ else } 0) + (\text{if } c \text{ then } 2 \text{ else } 0) + (\text{if } r \text{ then } 1 \text{ else } 0)\bigr).
\]
\item[\textup{(1)}] \textbf{Initial value:} $a(0) = 1$.
\item[\textup{(2)}] \textbf{Closed form:} For every $n \in \N$ with $n > 0$,
\[
2 a(n) = -2\bigl((-1)^n - 1\bigr)n + 3(-1)^n + 1 \qquad \text{in }\Z.
\]
\item[\textup{(3)}] \textbf{Four-step linear recurrence:} For every $n \in \N$ with $n > 4$,
\[
a(n) = 2a(n - 2) - a(n - 4) \qquad \text{in }\Z.
\]
\item[\textup{(4)}] \textbf{Ordinary generating function:} Let $F(X) := \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a(n)\,X^n \in \Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$. Then
\[
(1 - X)^2 (1 + X)^2 \, F(X) = 1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4 \qquad \text{in }\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket,
\]
and since $(1 - X)^2(1 + X)^2$ is a unit in $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$, equivalently $F(X) = (1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4)\cdot \bigl((1 - X)^2(1 + X)^2\bigr)^{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, the guards $n > 0$ in \textup{(2)} and $n > 4$ in \textup{(3)} are both sharp.
\end{theorem}

\section{Persistent Two-Cell Glider and Global \texorpdfstring{$\Z$}{Z}-Invariant}

\begin{lemma}[Bit-index equivalence, Boolean normal form, and chirality]
\label{lem:rule11}
For all $(l, c, r) \in \mathsf{Bool}^3$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] $\ruleTT(l, c, r) = \mathrm{Nat.testBit}\;11\;(4l + 2c + r)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(0)});
\item[\textup{(b)}] $\ruleTT(l, c, r) = (\neg l) \land (\neg c \lor r)$;
\item[\textup{(c)}] $\ruleTT$ breaks left-right reflection symmetry:
\[
\ruleTT(\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}) = \mathtt{true} \ne \mathtt{false} = \ruleTT(\mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false}).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Exhaustive evaluation over all $2^3 = 8$ Boolean triples $(l, c, r)$ verifies (a) and (b) directly against $11 = 00001011_2$: every triple with $l = \mathtt{true}$ outputs $\mathtt{false}$, and among the four triples with $l = \mathtt{false}$, only $(\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false})$ outputs $\mathtt{false}$, yielding $(\neg l) \land (\neg c \lor r)$. Part~(c) compares bit~$1$ ($1$) with bit~$4$ ($0$).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generation $n = 1$ on $\Z$]
\label{prop:ca-one}
For every $j \in \Z$,
\[
\ca(1, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j \ne 0 \land j \ne 1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:c052} and Lemma~\ref{lem:rule11}(b), $\ca(1, j) = (\neg \mathrm{decide}(j - 1 = 0)) \land (\neg \mathrm{decide}(j = 0) \lor \mathrm{decide}(j + 1 = 0))$. Consider three exhaustive cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item $j = 0$: neighborhood is $(\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}) \mapsto \mathtt{false}$, matching $\mathrm{decide}(0 \ne 0 \land 0 \ne 1) = \mathtt{false}$.
\item $j = 1$: neighborhood is $(\mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false}) \mapsto \mathtt{false}$, matching $\mathrm{decide}(1 \ne 0 \land 1 \ne 1) = \mathtt{false}$.
\item $j \notin \{0, 1\}$: both $j - 1 \ne 0$ ($l = \mathtt{false}$) and $j \ne 0$ ($c = \mathtt{false}$), so $(\neg \mathtt{false}) \land (\neg \mathtt{false} \lor r) = \mathtt{true}$ regardless of $r$, matching $\mathrm{decide}(j \ne 0 \land j \ne 1) = \mathtt{true}$. \qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

The heart of Rule~11's dynamics is that the two-cell defect $\{0, 1\}$ created at $n = 1$ neither spreads nor annihilates: on every step it shifts rightward by $+1$ while its polarity and the surrounding uniform background both invert.

\begin{lemma}[Single-step glider transitions on $\Z$]
\label{lem:glider-steps}
Let $n \in \N$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] \emph{Odd-to-even transition:} If $\ca(n, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j \ne n - 1 \land j \ne n)$ for all $j \in \Z$, then
\[
\ca(n + 1, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j = n \lor j = n + 1) \qquad (\forall j \in \Z).
\]
\item[\textup{(b)}] \emph{Even-to-odd transition:} If $\ca(n, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j = n - 1 \lor j = n)$ for all $j \in \Z$, then
\[
\ca(n + 1, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j \ne n \land j \ne n + 1) \qquad (\forall j \in \Z).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $j \in \Z$ and write $(l, c, r) = (\ca(n, j - 1), \ca(n, j), \ca(n, j + 1))$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rule11}(b), $\ca(n + 1, j) = (\neg l) \land (\neg c \lor r)$.

\emph{Part~(a).} Under the hypothesis, $\ca(n, i) = \mathtt{false} \iff i \in \{n - 1, n\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $j = n$, then $(j - 1, j, j + 1) = (n - 1, n, n + 1)$, so $(l, c, r) = (\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}) \mapsto \mathtt{true}$.
\item If $j = n + 1$, then $(j - 1, j, j + 1) = (n, n + 1, n + 2)$, so $(l, c, r) = (\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}, \mathtt{true}) \mapsto \mathtt{true}$.
\item If $j \notin \{n, n + 1\}$, then $j - 1 \notin \{n - 1, n\}$, so $l = \mathtt{true}$ and $(\neg \mathtt{true}) \land (\neg c \lor r) = \mathtt{false}$.
\end{itemize}
Hence $\ca(n + 1, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j = n \lor j = n + 1)$.

\emph{Part~(b).} Under the hypothesis, $\ca(n, i) = \mathtt{true} \iff i \in \{n - 1, n\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $j \in \{n, n + 1\}$, then $j - 1 \in \{n - 1, n\}$, so $l = \mathtt{true}$ and $\ca(n + 1, j) = \mathtt{false}$.
\item If $j = n - 1$, then $(j - 1, j, j + 1) = (n - 2, n - 1, n)$, so $(l, c, r) = (\mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true}, \mathtt{true}) \mapsto \mathtt{true}$.
\item If $j \notin \{n - 1, n, n + 1\}$, then both $j - 1 \notin \{n - 1, n\}$ ($l = \mathtt{false}$) and $j \notin \{n - 1, n\}$ ($c = \mathtt{false}$), so $(\neg \mathtt{false}) \land (\neg \mathtt{false} \lor r) = \mathtt{true}$ regardless of $r$.
\end{itemize}
Hence $\ca(n + 1, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j \ne n \land j \ne n + 1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global glider + alternating-exterior invariant on $\Z$]
\label{thm:global-inv}
For every $m \in \N$ and every $j \in \Z$:
\begin{align}
\ca(2m + 1, j) &= \mathrm{decide}(j \ne 2m \land j \ne 2m + 1), \label{eq:inv-odd}\\
\ca(2m + 2, j) &= \mathrm{decide}(j = 2m + 1 \lor j = 2m + 2). \label{eq:inv-even}
\end{align}
Equivalently, for every $n \in \N$ with $n \ge 1$ and every $j \in \Z$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inv-xor}
\ca(n, j) = \operatorname{xor}\bigl(\mathrm{decide}(\mathrm{Odd}(n)),\, \mathrm{decide}(j = n - 1 \lor j = n)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induct on $m \in \N$ for the coupled statement~\eqref{eq:inv-odd}--\eqref{eq:inv-even}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Base case $m = 0$:} Equation~\eqref{eq:inv-odd} at $m = 0$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:ca-one}, and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:glider-steps}(a) at $n = 1$ yields~\eqref{eq:inv-even} at $m = 0$.
\item \emph{Inductive step $m \mapsto m + 1$:} Assume~\eqref{eq:inv-even} holds at $m$, i.e., $\ca(2m + 2, j) = \mathrm{decide}(j = (2m + 2) - 1 \lor j = 2m + 2)$ for all $j \in \Z$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:glider-steps}(b) at $n = 2m + 2$ gives~\eqref{eq:inv-odd} at $m + 1$ ($2(m + 1) + 1 = 2m + 3$), and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:glider-steps}(a) at $n = 2m + 3$ gives~\eqref{eq:inv-even} at $m + 1$ ($2(m + 1) + 2 = 2m + 4$).
\end{itemize}
Finally, for any $n \ge 1$, splitting on parity gives~\eqref{eq:inv-xor}: if $n = 2m$ ($m \ge 1$), $\mathrm{decide}(\mathrm{Odd}(n)) = \mathtt{false}$ and $\operatorname{xor}(\mathtt{false}, b) = b$, matching~\eqref{eq:inv-even} at $m - 1$; if $n = 2m + 1$, $\mathrm{decide}(\mathrm{Odd}(n)) = \mathtt{true}$ and $\operatorname{xor}(\mathtt{true}, \mathrm{decide}(j = 2m \lor j = 2m + 1)) = \mathrm{decide}(j \ne 2m \land j \ne 2m + 1)$, matching~\eqref{eq:inv-odd}.
\end{proof}

\section{Window Counts, Closed Form, Recurrence, and Guard Sharpness}

\begin{proposition}[Exact parity counts and ON/OFF complement law]
\label{prop:counts}
For every $n \in \N$, the integer interval $[-n, n] \cap \Z$ has cardinality $2n + 1$. Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] $a(0) = 1$.
\item[\textup{(b)}] For every $m \ge 1$, $a(2m) = 2$.
\item[\textup{(c)}] For every $m \ge 0$, $a(2m + 1) = 4m + 1 = 2(2m + 1) - 1$.
\item[\textup{(d)}] Letting $a_{\mathrm{OFF}}(n) := |\{j \in [-n, n] \cap \Z : \ca(n, j) = \mathtt{false}\}|$ (\seq{A266258}), we have $a(n) + a_{\mathrm{OFF}}(n) = 2n + 1$ for every $n \in \N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] At $n = 0$, $[-0, 0] \cap \Z = \{0\}$ and $\ca(0, 0) = \mathtt{true}$, so the filtered set is $\{0\}$ of cardinality~$1$.
\item[\textup{(b)}] Let $m \ge 1$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:global-inv}, $\ca(2m, j) = \mathtt{true} \iff j \in \{2m - 1, 2m\}$. Since $m \ge 1$, we have $-2m \le 2m - 1 < 2m$, so $\{2m - 1, 2m\} \subseteq [-2m, 2m]$. Thus the filtered finset equals the two-element set $\{2m - 1, 2m\}$ with $2m - 1 \ne 2m$, giving $a(2m) = 2$.
\item[\textup{(c)}] Let $m \ge 0$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:global-inv}, for $j \in \Z$,
\[
\bigl(-(2m + 1) \le j \le 2m + 1\bigr) \land (j \ne 2m \land j \ne 2m + 1) \iff -(2m + 1) \le j \le 2m - 1.
\]
Hence the filtered finset equals the contiguous integer interval $[-(2m + 1), 2m - 1] \cap \Z$, whose cardinality is $(2m - 1) + 1 - (-(2m + 1)) = 4m + 1$.
\item[\textup{(d)}] Every $j \in [-n, n] \cap \Z$ satisfies either $\ca(n, j) = \mathtt{true}$ or $\ca(n, j) = \mathtt{false}$ exclusively, partitioning $[-n, n] \cap \Z$ into two disjoint subsets whose cardinalities sum to $|[-n, n] \cap \Z| = 2n + 1$. \qedhere
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

From Proposition~\ref{prop:counts} we immediately record the initial values
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:init-vals}
a(0) = 1, \qquad a(1) = 1, \qquad a(2) = 2, \qquad a(3) = 5, \qquad a(4) = 2.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Closed form and sharpness at $n = 0$]
\label{thm:closed-form}
For every $n \in \N$ with $n > 0$,
\[
2 a(n) = -2\bigl((-1)^n - 1\bigr)n + 3(-1)^n + 1 \qquad \text{in }\Z.
\]
The guard $n > 0$ is sharp: at $n = 0$, $2a(0) = 2 \ne 4 = -2((-1)^0 - 1)\cdot 0 + 3(-1)^0 + 1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $n > 0$ and split on parity.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $n = 2m$ ($m \ge 1$), then $(-1)^{2m} = 1$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:counts}(b) gives $a(2m) = 2$, so
\[
-2\bigl(1 - 1\bigr)(2m) + 3(1) + 1 = 4 = 2 \cdot 2 = 2a(2m).
\]
\item If $n = 2m + 1$ ($m \ge 0$), then $(-1)^{2m+1} = -1$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:counts}(c) gives $a(2m + 1) = 4m + 1$, so
\[
-2\bigl(-1 - 1\bigr)(2m + 1) + 3(-1) + 1 = 4(2m + 1) - 2 = 8m + 2 = 2(4m + 1) = 2a(2m + 1).
\]
\end{itemize}
Sharpness at $n = 0$ follows directly from $a(0) = 1$: $2a(0) = 2 \ne 4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Four-step linear recurrence and sharpness at $n = 4$]
\label{thm:recurrence}
For every $n \in \N$ with $n > 4$,
\[
a(n) = 2a(n - 2) - a(n - 4) \qquad \text{in }\Z.
\]
The guard $n > 4$ is sharp: at $n = 4$, $a(4) = 2 \ne 3 = 2a(2) - a(0)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $n > 4$, we have $n \ge 5$, so $n - 2 \ge 3$ and $n - 4 \ge 1$ are strictly positive and share the parity of~$n$:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $n = 2m$ ($m \ge 3$), then $n - 2 = 2(m - 1)$ and $n - 4 = 2(m - 2)$ with $m, m - 1, m - 2 \ge 1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:counts}(b) gives $a(n) = a(n - 2) = a(n - 4) = 2$, whence $2(2) - 2 = 2 = a(n)$.
\item If $n = 2m + 1$ ($m \ge 2$), then $n - 2 = 2(m - 1) + 1$ and $n - 4 = 2(m - 2) + 1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:counts}(c) gives
\[
2a(n - 2) - a(n - 4) = 2\bigl(4(m - 1) + 1\bigr) - \bigl(4(m - 2) + 1\bigr) = 4m + 1 = a(n).
\]
\end{itemize}
At $n = 4$,~\eqref{eq:init-vals} gives $a(4) = 2$ whereas $2a(2) - a(0) = 2(2) - 1 = 3$.
\end{proof}

\section{Rational Ordinary Generating Function in \texorpdfstring{$\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$}{Q[[X]]}}

\begin{theorem}[Cleared-denominator generating function]
\label{thm:gf}
Let $F(X) := \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a(n)\,X^n \in \Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gf-cleared}
(1 - X)^2 (1 + X)^2 \, F(X) = 1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4 \qquad \text{in }\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the commutative ring $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$, polynomial expansion yields
\[
(1 - X)^2 (1 + X)^2 \, F(X) = (1 - 2X^2 + X^4)\,F(X) = F(X) - 2(X^2 F(X)) + X^4 F(X).
\]
By extensionality of formal power series, it suffices to verify equality of the $n$-th coefficient $[X^n]$ for every $n \in \N$. Using $[X^n](X^d F(X)) = a(n - d)$ if $n \ge d$ and $0$ otherwise, we verify the six cases $n = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ and $n = k + 5$ ($k \ge 0$) directly via~\eqref{eq:init-vals} and Theorem~\ref{thm:recurrence}:
\begin{align*}
n = 0:\quad & a(0) = 1 = [X^0](1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4), \\
n = 1:\quad & a(1) = 1 = [X^1](1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4), \\
n = 2:\quad & a(2) - 2a(0) = 2 - 2(1) = 0 = [X^2](1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4), \\
n = 3:\quad & a(3) - 2a(1) = 5 - 2(1) = 3 = [X^3](1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4), \\
n = 4:\quad & a(4) - 2a(2) + a(0) = 2 - 2(2) + 1 = -1 = [X^4](1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4), \\
n = k + 5:\quad & a(k + 5) - 2a(k + 3) + a(k + 1) = 0 = [X^{k+5}](1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4),
\end{align*}
where the last line follows by casting Theorem~\ref{thm:recurrence} at $n = k + 5 > 4$ from $\Z$ to $\Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unit invertibility and lossless inverse-series equivalence]
\label{cor:unit-inv}
The polynomial factor $D(X) := (1 - X)^2(1 + X)^2 \in \Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$ is a unit in $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$, and
\[
F(X) = (1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4) \cdot \bigl((1 - X)^2(1 + X)^2\bigr)^{-1} \qquad \text{in }\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A formal power series over a field is a unit if and only if its constant coefficient is nonzero. Here $[X^0]D(X) = (1 - 0)^2(1 + 0)^2 = 1 \ne 0$, so $D(X) \cdot D(X)^{-1} = 1$ in $\Q\llbracket X \rrbracket$. Multiplying~\eqref{eq:gf-cleared} on the right by $D(X)^{-1}$ yields $F(X) = (D(X) F(X)) D(X)^{-1} = (1 + X + 3X^3 - X^4)\,D(X)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Combine Lemma~\ref{lem:rule11}(a), Proposition~\ref{prop:counts}(a), Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-form}, Theorem~\ref{thm:recurrence}, Theorem~\ref{thm:gf}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:unit-inv}.
\end{proof}

\section{Structural Extensions: Rule~43 Equivalence, Rule~47 Contrast, and Zero-Clamping Traps}

\subsection{Isolated-Zero Avoidance and the \texorpdfstring{$\{11, 43\}$}{\{11, 43\}} Equivalence Class}

For any Wolfram rule code $c \in \N$, let $\operatorname{rule}_c(l, c_0, r) := \mathrm{Nat.testBit}\;c\;(4l + 2c_0 + r)$ and let $\ca_c : \N \to \Z \to \mathsf{Bool}$ denote its bi-infinite single-seed evolution on $\Z$.

\begin{theorem}[Isolated-zero avoidance and Rule~11 / Rule~43 equivalence]
\label{thm:rule43}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] For every $n \in \N$ and every $j \in \Z$, the isolated-zero neighborhood $(\mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true})$ (bit index~$5$) never occurs in $\ca(n, \cdot)$:
\[
\neg\bigl(\ca(n, j - 1) = \mathtt{true} \land \ca(n, j) = \mathtt{false} \land \ca(n, j + 1) = \mathtt{true}\bigr).
\]
\item[\textup{(b)}] If $c \in \N$ agrees with~$11$ on all seven bits $i \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7\}$, then $\ca_c(n, j) = \ca(n, j)$ for all $n \in \N$ and $j \in \Z$. In particular, both Wolfram Rule~$11$ ($00001011_2$) and Wolfram Rule~$43$ ($00101011_2$) generate bit-for-bit identical space-time diagrams on $\Z$ and identical ON-cell counts $a(n) = \seq{A266256}(n)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (a), check the three regimes of $\ca(n, \cdot)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $n = 0$: $\ca(0, j - 1) = \mathtt{true} = \ca(0, j + 1)$ requires $j - 1 = 0 = j + 1$, impossible.
\item $n = 2m \ge 2$ even: by Theorem~\ref{thm:global-inv}, $\ca(2m, \cdot)$ is $\mathtt{true}$ solely on the adjacent pair $\{2m - 1, 2m\}$, so $j - 1 \in \{2m - 1, 2m\}$ and $j + 1 \in \{2m - 1, 2m\}$ cannot hold simultaneously since $(j + 1) - (j - 1) = 2 > 1$.
\item $n = 2m + 1 \ge 1$ odd: by Theorem~\ref{thm:global-inv}, $\ca(2m + 1, \cdot)$ is $\mathtt{false}$ solely on the adjacent pair $\{2m, 2m + 1\}$. If $\ca(2m + 1, j) = \mathtt{false}$, then $j \in \{2m, 2m + 1\}$, so either $j + 1 = 2m + 1$ ($\ca(n, j + 1) = \mathtt{false}$) or $j - 1 = 2m$ ($\ca(n, j - 1) = \mathtt{false}$).
\end{itemize}
Part~(b) follows by induction on $n \in \N$: at each step $n \mapsto n + 1$, the induction hypothesis gives $\ca_c(n, \cdot) = \ca(n, \cdot)$, and by part~(a) the triple $(\ca(n, j - 1), \ca(n, j), \ca(n, j + 1))$ is never $(\mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}, \mathtt{true})$, so bit~$5$ is never queried and $\operatorname{rule}_c$ agrees with $\ruleTT$. Since $43 = 11 + 2^5$, this proves the equivalence remarked on \seq{A266253}~\cite{oeis266253}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Contrast with defect-annihilating rules and Rule~47 (\seq{A266662})]
\label{rem:rule47}
In contrast to Rule~7 (\seq{A266220}/\seq{A266222}) and Rule~23 (\seq{A266437}/\seq{A266439}), which annihilate the seed defect completely by $n = 2$ and $n = 1$ respectively to produce spatially constant alternating rows on $\Z$, Rule~11 preserves a persistent two-cell glider $\{n - 1, n\}$ for all $n \ge 1$. Meanwhile, Wolfram Rule~47 ($47 = 00101111_2$, \seq{A266662}~\cite{oeis266662}) differs from Rule~11 at bit~$2$ ($010 \mapsto 1$) and bit~$5$ ($101 \mapsto 1$). At $n = 1$, bit~$2$ flips $j = 0$ from $0$ to $1$, yielding window \texttt{110} (a single OFF defect at $j = 1$ with $\seq{A266662}(1) = 2 \ne 1 = a(1)$). At $n = 2$, however, $j = 1$ sees the isolated-zero triple $(1, 0, 1)$, which bit~$5$ of Rule~47 maps to~$1$, while $j = 2$ sees $(0, 1, 1) \mapsto 1$ and all other cells map to~$0$---producing the exact same two-cell glider $\{1, 2\}$ (\texttt{00011}) as Rule~11. Because Rule~47 agrees with Rule~11 on all non-$(1, 0, 1)$ transitions, both evolutions coincide identically for every $n \ge 2$, explaining simultaneously R.~J.\ Mathar's remark $\seq{A266662}(n) = \seq{A266256}(n)$ ($n > 1$) and why Rule~47's sharp guards shift up by~$+1$ ($n > 1$ and $n > 5$) relative to Rule~11 ($n > 0$ and $n > 4$).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Causal-Cone Mirror and Formalization of Both Zero-Clamping Traps}

Direct unmemoized evaluation of $\ca(n, j)$ via Definition~\ref{def:c052} traverses a ternary tree of size $3^n$. To enable fast, pure-kernel spot-checking of $a(n)$ directly against OEIS~\seq{A266256} without any computational trust escape, we define an $O(n^2)$ causal-cone contraction mirror $a_{\mathrm{dp}}(n)$: initialize the length-$(4n + 1)$ Boolean list $[0^{2n}, 1, 0^{2n}]$ representing $\ca(0, j)$ on $[-2n, 2n]$, iterate the sliding-window map $(l, c, r, \dots) \mapsto (\ruleTT(l, c, r), \dots)$ exactly $n$ times (shrinking length by~$2$ per step down to $2n + 1$), and count the $\mathtt{true}$ entries.

\begin{proposition}[Causal-cone mirror and both zero-clamping traps]
\label{prop:clamping-traps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] For every $n \in \N$, $a_{\mathrm{dp}}(n) = a(n)$, and pure kernel reduction confirms
\[
\bigl(a(0), a(1), \dots, a(12)\bigr) = (1, 1, 2, 5, 2, 9, 2, 13, 2, 17, 2, 21, 2),
\]
matching OEIS~\seq{A266256}.
\item[\textup{(b)}] \emph{Trap~A (light-cone zero-clamping):} Let $\ca_{\mathrm{cone}}$ evolve by Rule~11 while clamping every cell outside $[-m, m]$ to $\mathtt{false}$ after step~$m$, and let $a_{\mathrm{cone}}(n)$ count its ON cells on $[-n, n]$. Then $a_{\mathrm{cone}}(2) = 3 \ne 2 = a(2)$.
\item[\textup{(c)}] \emph{Trap~B (fixed-box $[-n, n]$ zero-clamping):} Let $\ca_{\mathrm{box}}(R, \cdot, \cdot)$ evolve by Rule~11 on the fixed box $[-R, R]$ with exterior clamped to $\mathtt{false}$, and let $a_{\mathrm{box}}(n)$ count ON cells of $\ca_{\mathrm{box}}(n, n, \cdot)$ on $[-n, n]$. Then $\ca_{\mathrm{box}}(2, 2, -2) = \mathtt{true} \ne \mathtt{false} = \ca(2, -2)$ (bit profile already corrupted at $n = 2$: \texttt{10010} vs.\ \texttt{00011}) and $a_{\mathrm{box}}(3) = 3 \ne 5 = a(3)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a) follows by induction on the step count $k$: applying $k$ sliding-window steps to $[\ca(m, s), \dots, \ca(m, s + \ell + 2k - 1)]$ yields $[\ca(m + k, s + k), \dots, \ca(m + k, s + k + \ell - 1)]$; setting $m = 0$, $s = -2n$, $\ell = 2n + 1$, and $k = n$ recovers the exact window $[-n, n]$ at generation $n$. Parts~(b) and~(c) follow by direct kernel evaluation: under Trap~A, row~$1$ is \texttt{100} with zero exterior, giving row~$2$ equal to $\ruleTT(0,0,1), \ruleTT(0,1,0), \ruleTT(1,0,0), \ruleTT(0,0,0), \ruleTT(0,0,0) = \mathtt{10011}$ (count~$3$); under Trap~B at $R = 2$, row~$1$ on $[-2, 2]$ is \texttt{11001} with zero exterior, giving row~$2$ equal to \texttt{10010} ($\ca_{\mathrm{box}}(2, 2, -2) = \mathtt{true}$), and at $R = 3$, row~$3$ on $[-3, 3]$ is \texttt{0011100} (count~$3 \ne 5$).
\end{proof}
