## Abstract

A labeled Riordan graph of order $n$ (`OEIS A401493`), introduced by Cheon, Jung, Kitaev, and Mojallal (*Linear Algebra Appl.* **579**, 2019, 89–135), is a simple graph on $\{1, \dots, n\}$ whose strictly lower-triangular adjacency matrix is the $(n - 1) \times (n - 1)$ binary Riordan matrix $(g, f)$ generated by formal power series $g(t), f(t) \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ with $f(0) = 0$: for $1 \le J < I \le n$, vertices $I$ and $J$ are adjacent if and only if $[t^{I - 2}]\bigl(g(t) f(t)^{J - 1}\bigr) = 1$. Cheon et al. showed that Appell-type Riordan graphs $G_n(g, t)$ always have Riordan complements and posed Problem 3: *characterize Riordan graphs whose complements are Riordan*. On October 27, 2026, Arnauld Mesinga Mwafise (`OEIS A401496`, `%F` line 12) introduced the sequence $a(n)$ counting labeled Riordan graphs of order $n$ whose graph complement on the **same** vertex labeling is also a labeled Riordan graph (`1, 2, 4, 12, 28, 92, 220, 732, 1756, 5852, 14044, 46812, 112348, 374492, ...`), and conjectured that
$$a(n) = 8\,a(n - 2) - 4 \qquad \text{for all } n \ge 3,$$
with base values $a(1) = 1$ and $a(2) = 2$.

We resolve the labeled case of Cheon et al.'s Problem 3 and give a self-contained algebraic proof and complete Lean 4 formalization of Mwafise's recurrence for all $n \ge 3$. Viewing graph complementation on $\{1, \dots, n\}$ as translation by the complete-graph Appell pair $(u(t), t)$ where $u(t) = (1 + t)^{-1} = \sum_{d \ge 0} t^d \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$, we first observe that for $n \ge 2$, complementation is a free involution swapping the top-left adjacency bit $g_0 = 0 \leftrightarrow g_0 = 1$. Restricting to $g_0 = 0$ makes $\tilde{g} := u + g$ an invertible unit in $\mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ and yields the **Master Characterization Lemma**: $G_n(g, f)^c$ is a labeled Riordan graph on $\{1, \dots, n\}$ if and only if
$$g(t)\,\bigl(f(t) + t\bigr)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{t^{n - 1}}.$$
Necessity follows in characteristic $2$ from the first three Riordan columns $c = 0, 1, 2$; sufficiency constructs the explicit unit-inverse witness $\tilde{g} = u + g$, $\tilde{f} = (u + g)^{-1}(u t + g f)$ and proves via a three-term linear recurrence $Y_{c + 1} = (u + g)(t + f)Y_c + (u + g)^2 t f Y_{c - 1} + \Delta X^{c - 1}$ that every column discrepancy lies in the principal ideal $\Delta \cdot \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ generated by $\Delta = u g (f + t)^2$. Counting canonical polynomial representatives across valuation strata $r \in \{1, \dots, n - 2\}$ ($k = n - 2 - r$) via Frobenius squaring $(f + t)^2 = \sum_{d \ge 1} h_d t^{2d}$ yields $2^k \cdot 2^{k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor} = 2^{2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}$ solutions in layer $k$, hence
$$a(n) = 2\left(1 + \sum_{k = 0}^{n - 3} 2^{2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}\right) \qquad (n \ge 2),$$
from which peeling $k = 0, 1$ and shifting $k \mapsto k + 2$ closes $a(n) = 8\,a(n - 2) - 4$ with zero finite-horizon search. We also explain why two natural proof attempts fail: the inhomogeneous term $-4$ cannot arise from four fixed Appell families ($a(n) - c = 8(a(n - 2) - c)$ has unique fixed point $c = 4/7 \notin \mathbb{Z}$, and Appell graphs number $2^{n - 1}$), and naive induced-subgraph projection $G_n \mapsto G_{n - 2}$ fails to have uniform $8$-to-$1$ fibers starting at $n = 6$, where every internal layer splits $50/50$ into dead graphs ($0$ extensions) and $16$-extension graphs. All definitions and theorems compile cleanly in Lean 4 without `sorry`, custom axioms, `native_decide`, or `ofReduceBool`.

## 1. Introduction and Primary-Source Context

### 1.1 Binary Riordan Graphs, Cheon et al.'s Problem 3, and Mwafise's Conjecture

In 2019, Cheon, Jung, Kitaev, and Mojallal [1] introduced *Riordan graphs* as a broad family of structured graphs generalizing Pascal graphs, Catalan graphs, and binary Toeplitz graphs via the Riordan group reduced modulo $2$. Given formal power series $g(t), f(t) \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ with constant term $f(0) = 0$, the *labeled Riordan graph* $G_n(g, f)$ of order $n \ge 1$ has vertex set $\{1, \dots, n\}$ and undirected edges $\{I, J\}$ ($1 \le J < I \le n$) defined by
$$A(I, J) = [t^{I - 2}]\bigl(g(t)\,f(t)^{J - 1}\bigr) \in \mathbb{F}_2.$$
Equivalently, the strictly lower-triangular entries below the main diagonal of the adjacency matrix $A$ form the $(n - 1) \times (n - 1)$ binary Riordan matrix $(g, f)$ (`OEIS A401493` `%C` line 10 [2]). Both degenerate series $g = 0$ (producing the edgeless graph $N_n$) and $f = 0$ (producing star-subset graphs whose edges all meet vertex $1$) are included; Cheon et al. [1, Prop. 2.8] proved that the total number of distinct labeled Riordan graphs on $\{1, \dots, n\}$ is
$$L(n) = \frac{4^{n - 1} + 2}{3} = A_{047849}(n - 1) \qquad (1, 2, 6, 22, 86, 342, 1366, \dots).$$

In Section 2.6 (*The complement of a Riordan graph*), Cheon et al. [1, pp. 103–104] proved that every **Appell-type** Riordan graph $G_n(g, t)$ ($f(t) = t$, whose adjacency matrix is Toeplitz) has a Riordan complement on the same vertex labeling, namely
$$\overline{G_n(g, t)} = G_n\bigl(g(t) + (1 + t)^{-1},\; t\bigr),$$
giving $2^{n - 1}$ Appell graphs at order $n$. However, they observed that complements of Riordan graphs need not be Riordan in general—for instance, $\overline{G_4(1 + t^2, t^2)}$ is isomorphic to $G_4(1 + t + t^2, t^3)$ after relabeling vertices, yet is *not* a labeled Riordan graph on the original labeling $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$—and posed **Problem 3** [1, §6, Problem 3]:

> **Problem 3 (Cheon, Jung, Kitaev, and Mojallal, 2019).** *Characterize Riordan graphs whose complements are Riordan in labelled and unlabelled cases.*

In October 2026, Arnauld Mesinga Mwafise [2, 3, 4] published the C++ enumeration package `riordan-graph-enumerator` and contributed three OEIS sequences: `A401493` (unlabeled Riordan graphs), `A401495` (unlabeled Riordan graphs with Riordan complements), and `A401496` (labeled Riordan graphs whose complement **with the same vertex labeling** is also a labeled Riordan graph). Computing $a(n) = A_{401496}(n)$ for $1 \le n \le 14$,
$$(a(n))_{n = 1}^{14} = (1, 2, 4, 12, 28, 92, 220, 732, 1756, 5852, 14044, 46812, 112348, 374492),$$
Mwafise explicitly noted in the software documentation (`docs/COMPUTATIONS.md`, v1.1.0, November 5, 2026 [4]) that the release *"deliberately contains no mathematical proofs"* and posted the primary two-step recurrence conjecture on `OEIS A401496` (`%F` line 12, October 27, 2026; live revision `#9`, November 4, 2026 [3]):

> **Conjecture (Arnauld Mesinga Mwafise, October 27, 2026).** *For every $n \ge 3$,*
> $$a(n) = 8\,a(n - 2) - 4,$$
> *with base values $a(1) = 1$ and $a(2) = 2$.*

*Primary-Source Notes on Companion `%F` Lines.* Live `OEIS A401496` (`#9`) also lists four companion conjectural formulas (`%F` lines 13–16): the first-difference and partial-sum form $a(n + 1) - a(n) = 2^{\lfloor 3(n - 1)/2 \rfloor}$ (`%F` line 13), the offset cross-reference $a(n) = A_{113836}(n - 1) + 1$ (`%F` line 14, defined for $n \ge 2$ since `A113836` has offset `%O 1,2` [5]), parity closed forms (`%F` line 15), and the rational generating function $x(1 - 2x)(1 + 3x)/((1 - x)(1 - 8x^2))$ (`%F` line 16). Notably, in the live OEIS text of `%F` line 15, Markdown emphasis stripping deleted the two asterisks between `10*8^(k-1)` and `3*8^k`, corrupting the literal string to `a(2*k) = (108^(k-1) + 4)/7 and a(2*k+1) = (38^k + 4)/7`, which fails already at $k = 1$ ($5/7 \ne 2$ and $6 \ne 4$), whereas the `*`-restored closed forms $a(2k) = (10 \cdot 8^{k - 1} + 4)/7$ ($k \ge 1$) and $a(2k + 1) = (3 \cdot 8^k + 4)/7$ ($k \ge 0$) follow immediately from `%F` line 12 and $a(1) = 1, a(2) = 2$. Multi-channel prior-art searches across OEIS, arXiv, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Zenodo, and full git-tree scans (`SCOPING-S012.md` §3, `2026-11-15`; extended checks `2026-11-16`) confirmed that neither Mwafise's recurrence nor the labeled complement characterization in Cheon et al.'s Problem 3 had any published proof.

- **Theorem 1.5 (Closed-Layer Formula, Parity, Boundary Classification, & Fixed-Point Refutation — `count_eq_closed_layer_sum`, `odd_complementClosedRiordanCount_iff`, `fin2_both_graphs_complementClosed`, `fin3_boundary_classification`, `fin4_first_non_appell_and_cheon`, `no_integer_fixed_point_shift`).** *For all $n \ge 2$, $a(n) = 2\bigl(1 + \sum_{k = 0}^{n - 3} 2^{2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}\bigr)$ is even ($a(1) = 1$ is the unique odd value for $n \ge 1$); at $n = 4$, $G_4(t, 0)^c = G_4(1 + t^2, t + t^2)$ is the first non-Appell complement-closed pair while Cheon et al.'s $G_4(1 + t^2, t^2)^c$ is non-Riordan on `Fin 4`; and no $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ satisfies $a(n) - c = 8(a(n - 2) - c)$ for all $n \ge 3$.*

## 2. Formal Specification and Why Two Heuristic Approaches Fail

### 2.1 Pinned Lean 4 Definitions (`PIN-S012.md` v1)

Identifying $\{1, \dots, n\}$ order-isomorphically with `Fin n` via $I = i.\mathrm{val} + 1$ and $J = j.\mathrm{val} + 1$ translates $1 \le J < I \le n$ into `j.val < i.val`, with $I - 2 = i.\mathrm{val} - 1$ (which never truncates since `j.val < i.val` implies $1 \le i.\mathrm{val}$) and $J - 1 = j.\mathrm{val}$. The frozen target specification in `SeamC/A401496.lean` reads verbatim:

```lean
def IsLabeledRiordanGraph (n : ℕ) (G : SimpleGraph (Fin n)) : Prop :=
  ∃ g f : PowerSeries (ZMod 2), PowerSeries.coeff 0 f = 0 ∧
    ∀ i j : Fin n, j.val < i.val → (G.Adj i j ↔ PowerSeries.coeff (i.val - 1) (g * f ^ j.val) = 1)

noncomputable def complementClosedRiordanCount (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  Nat.card { G : SimpleGraph (Fin n) // IsLabeledRiordanGraph n G ∧ IsLabeledRiordanGraph n Gᶜ }
```

Four design invariants ensure exact fidelity (`delta_vs_source: NONE`):
1. **Extensional counting by labeled graph:** Multiple formal power series pairs $(g, f)$ produce the same finite graph $G$ on `Fin n` (for instance, $(0, f)$ gives $\perp$ for every $f$, and higher-degree tails $t^d$ with $d \ge n - 1$ are invisible). Counting the subtype `{ G : SimpleGraph (Fin n) // ... }` counts distinct labeled graphs extensionally rather than parameter pairs, while `Nat.card` agrees with `Fintype.card` on finite types without requiring decidability on existential `PowerSeries` quantifiers.
2. **Same-labeling complement `Gᶜ`:** Mathlib's `HasCompl` instance on `SimpleGraph (Fin n)` (`Gᶜ.Adj u v ↔ u ≠ v ∧ ¬ G.Adj u v`) keeps the vertex indices `Fin n` fixed, matching `A401496` `%N` line 8 (*"whose complement, with the same vertex labeling, is also a labeled Riordan graph"*) as opposed to unlabeled complement-isomorphism (`A401495`).
3. **Degenerate series included:** Both $g = 0$ and $f = 0$ lie in `PowerSeries (ZMod 2)` with `coeff 0 f = 0`, matching Cheon et al. [1, Prop. 2.8] and $L(n) = (4^{n - 1} + 2)/3$.
4. **Truncation-free $\mathbb{N}$ subtraction:** Because $a(1) = 1$ and $a(2) = 2$ imply $a(n - 2) \ge 1$ for every $n \ge 3$, $8\,a(n - 2) \ge 8 > 4$, so natural-number subtraction `- 4` never underflows and agrees identically with integer subtraction.

### 2.2 Failure 1: Refutation of the "Plus $4$ Appell Fixed Families" Heuristic

Given an inhomogeneous linear recurrence $a(n) = 8\,a(n - 2) - 4$, a standard combinatorial impulse is to seek an $8$-fold branching rule outside a fixed core of $4$ exceptional ("Appell") families. **Both halves of that heuristic are false** (`SCOPING-S012.md` §2.2(1)):

1. **No integer fixed-point shift (`no_integer_fixed_point_shift`):** If $c$ invariant families persisted unchanged from $n - 2$ to $n$ while the remaining $a(n - 2) - c$ graphs branched $8$-fold, the recurrence would read
   $$a(n) - c = 8\bigl(a(n - 2) - c\bigr) \iff a(n) = 8\,a(n - 2) - 7c.$$
   Matching $-7c = -4$ forces the non-integer rational fixed point $c = 4/7 \notin \mathbb{Z}$ (`4 - c = 8(1 - c)` has no solution in $\mathbb{Z}$).
2. **Appell graphs grow $4\times$ per two-vertex step, not $O(1)$:** Setting $f(t) = t$ leaves $g(t) \bmod t^{n - 1}$ arbitrary ($2^{n - 1}$ distinct labeled Appell graphs at order $n$: $1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, \dots$), multiplying by $4$ under $n - 2 \mapsto n$ and falling strictly short of $a(n)$ for every $n \ge 4$ ($8 < 12$, $16 < 28$, $32 < 92$, $\dots$).
3. **True combinatorial origin of $-4$:** In our closed-layer decomposition $a(n) = 2 + \sum_{k = 0}^{n - 3} 2^{2k + 1 - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}$ ($n \ge 2$), the two singleton boundary graphs $\perp = G_n(0, 0)$ and $\top = G_n(u, t)$ contribute $+2$ at every order rather than $8 \cdot 2 = 16$ (a deficit of $2 - 16 = -14$), while advancing $n - 2 \mapsto n$ births two new bottom valuation layers $k = 0$ ($+2$) and $k = 1$ ($+8$), producing the exact constant $-14 + (2 + 8) = -4$.

### 2.3 Failure 2: Breakdown of Naive $8$-to-$1$ Vertex Deletion $G_n \mapsto G_{n - 2}$ at $n \ge 6$

A second natural strategy is to project complement-closed labeled Riordan graphs of order $n$ onto order $n - 2$ by deleting the last two vertices $\{n - 1, n\}$ (truncating $(n - 1)$-jets modulo $t^{n - 3}$) and counting extension fibers. While the $g_0 = 0$ fiber sizes are $\{6 : 1\}$ at $n = 4$ and $\{6 : 1, 8 : 1\}$ at $n = 5$, **uniform $8$-to-$1$ branching fails starting at $n = 6$** (`SCOPING-S012.md` §2.2(2), re-verified in `verify_s012_lane.py`):

| Order $n$ | $g_0 = 0$ Count at $n - 2$ | $g_0 = 0$ Extension Fiber Histogram ($G_{n - 2} \leftarrow G_n$) | Sum of Fibers $= a(n)/2$ |
| --: | --: | :--- | --: |
| $4$ | $1$ | $\{6 : 1\}$ | $6$ |
| $5$ | $2$ | $\{6 : 1,\; 8 : 1\}$ | $14$ |
| $6$ | $6$ | $\{0 : 2,\; 6 : 1,\; 8 : 1,\; 16 : 2\}$ | $46$ |
| $7$ | $14$ | $\{0 : 6,\; 6 : 1,\; 8 : 1,\; 16 : 6\}$ | $110$ |
| $8$ | $46$ | $\{0 : 22,\; 6 : 1,\; 8 : 1,\; 16 : 22\}$ | $366$ |
| $9$ | $110$ | $\{0 : 54,\; 6 : 1,\; 8 : 1,\; 16 : 54\}$ | $878$ |
| $10$ | $366$ | $\{0 : 182,\; 6 : 1,\; 8 : 1,\; 16 : 182\}$ | $2,926$ |

Theorem 1.4 explains this phenomenon completely. A canonical $g_0 = 0$ graph in layer $k \ge 1$ at order $n - 2$ ($r = n - 4 - k \ge 1$) satisfies $(f + t)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{t^{k + 1}}$, which constrains $h_d = [t^d](f + t) = 0$ for $1 \le d \le \lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ while leaving $h_{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor + 1} \in \mathbb{F}_2$ unconstrained. Extending the same valuation-$r$ graph to order $n$ raises its layer index to $k' = k + 2$, where $\lfloor (k + 2)/2 \rfloor = \lfloor k/2 \rfloor + 1$ **promotes $h_{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor + 1}$ into the constrained range**!
- Half of layer $k$ has $h_{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor + 1} = 1$ (for example, $G_4(t, 0)$ at $n - 2 = 4$, where $k = 1$ and $h_1 = 1$) and therefore admits **$0$ extensions** to order $n = 6$ ("dead" graphs).
- The other half has $h_{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor + 1} = 0$ (for example, $G_4(t, t)$ at $n - 2 = 4$) and extends via $2$ new $g$-bits and $2$ new free $f$-bits in **$4 \times 4 = 16$ ways**.

Although the *average* extension count across layer $k \ge 1$ remains $\frac{1}{2}(0 + 16) = 8$, individual graph fibers are $\{0, 16\}$, making a direct vertex-deletion proof awkward. Counting canonical layers $k \in \{0, \dots, n - 3\}$ directly at fixed order $n$ bypasses vertex-deletion fibers altogether: shifting the summation index $k \mapsto k + 2$ multiplies each layer size $2^{2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}$ cleanly by $2^{4 - 1} = 8$.

## 3. Steps 1–3: Truncated Congruence, Canonical Parameterization, and Appell Involution

Throughout Sections 3 and 4, let $\mathrm{PS} := \mathrm{PowerSeries}\;(\mathrm{ZMod}\;2) = \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$, where $p + p = 0$ (`ps_add_self`), $-p = p$ (`ps_neg`), and $(p + q)^2 = p^2 + q^2$ (`ps_add_sq`).

### 3.1 Truncated Congruence `EqMod`, Frobenius Squaring, and Unit Cancellation

Rather than forming ideal quotients $\mathbb{F}_2[[t]]/(t^m)$, we work directly with coefficient-level congruence modulo $t^m$:
$$\mathrm{EqMod}\;m\;p\;q \;:=\; \forall d < m,\quad [t^d]p = [t^d]q.$$
In characteristic $2$, $\mathrm{EqMod}\;m\;p\;q \iff t^m \mid (p + q)$ (`eqMod_iff_X_pow_dvd`). Writing $p_1 p_2 + q_1 q_2 = (p_1 + q_1)p_2 + q_1(p_2 + q_2)$ immediately establishes that `EqMod m` is a commutative ring congruence closed under addition, multiplication, and powers (`EqMod.add`, `EqMod.mul`, `EqMod.pow`). Three structural lemmas power the elimination arguments:

1. **Frobenius squaring (`coeff_sq_zmod2`):** Decomposing $h(t) = t\,h'(t) + h_0$ via `PowerSeries.eq_X_mul_shift_add_const` and inducting with step $2$ on $m$ gives $h(t)^2 = t^2 h'(t)^2 + h_0$, whence for all $m \in \mathbb{N}$,
   $$[t^m]\bigl(h(t)^2\bigr) = \begin{cases} [t^{m/2}]\,h(t) & \text{if } m \equiv 0 \pmod 2, \\ 0 & \text{if } m \equiv 1 \pmod 2. \end{cases}$$
2. **Unit cancellation (`EqMod.cancel_unit_left`, `EqMod.cancel_unit_zero`):** Whenever $[t^0]\varphi = 1$, $\varphi$ has invertible constant coefficient $1 \in (\mathbb{F}_2)^\times$, so Mathlib's `PowerSeries.invOfUnit φ 1` supplies $\psi \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ with $\psi \varphi = 1$; multiplying congruence modulo $t^m$ by $\psi$ yields $\mathrm{EqMod}\;m\;(\varphi p)\;(\varphi q) \iff \mathrm{EqMod}\;m\;p\;q$.
3. **Monomial stripping (`EqMod.X_pow_mul_zero_iff`):** For all $m, r \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathrm{EqMod}\;m\;(t^r p)\;0 \iff \mathrm{EqMod}\;(m - r)\;p\;0$.

### 3.2 Step 1: Realization and $m$-Jet Column Equivalence (`riordanGraph_eq_iff`)

For any $g, f \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$, define `riordanGraph n g f : SimpleGraph (Fin n)` via `SimpleGraph.fromRel` so that for $0 \le j < i < n$, `(riordanGraph n g f).Adj i j ↔ coeff (i.val - 1) (g * f ^ j.val) = 1`. By symmetry and looplessness (`eq_riordanGraph_of_spec`), $G$ satisfies `IsLabeledRiordanGraph n G` if and only if $G = \mathrm{riordanGraph}\;n\;g\;f$ for some $g, f$ with $f(0) = 0$ (`isLabeledRiordanGraph_iff_exists_eq`).

When $f(0) = 0$, $t \mid f$ implies $t^c \mid g f^c$, so $[t^r](g f^c) = 0$ automatically for $0 \le r < c$ (`coeff_mul_pow_eq_zero_of_lt`). Consequently, setting $m := n - 1$, rows $c \le r < m$ ($i = r + 1 \in \{c + 1, \dots, n - 1\}$) determine the entire $m$-jet of $g f^c$:

**Lemma 3.1 (`riordanGraph_eq_iff`).** *If $f_1(0) = f_2(0) = 0$, then*
$$\mathrm{riordanGraph}\;n\;g_1\;f_1 = \mathrm{riordanGraph}\;n\;g_2\;f_2 \iff \forall c < n - 1,\quad \mathrm{EqMod}\;(n - 1)\;(g_1 f_1^c)\;(g_2 f_2^c).$$

### 3.3 Step 2: Canonical Parameterization (`canonParamEquiv`)

Define the finite parameter type
$$\mathrm{CanonParam}(n) \;:=\; \mathrm{Unit} \;\oplus\; \sum_{k : \mathrm{Fin}(n - 1)} \bigl((\mathrm{Fin}\;k \to \mathbb{F}_2) \times (\mathrm{Fin}\;k \to \mathbb{F}_2)\bigr),$$
mapping `Sum.inl ()` to $(0, 0)$ (the edgeless graph $\perp$) and `Sum.inr ⟨k, (gb, fb)⟩` (representing valuation $r := n - 2 - k \in \{0, \dots, n - 2\}$) to
$$g = t^r \left(1 + \sum_{j = 0}^{k - 1} \mathrm{gb}_j\,t^{j + 1}\right) = t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb}), \qquad f = \sum_{j = 0}^{k - 1} \mathrm{fb}_j\,t^{j + 1} = \mathrm{fOfBits}(\mathrm{fb}).$$

**Theorem 3.2 (`canonParamEquiv`, `three_mul_labeledRiordanCount_eq`).** *The evaluation map `graphOfCanonParam n` is a bijection from `CanonParam n` onto `{ G : SimpleGraph (Fin n) // IsLabeledRiordanGraph n G }`. In particular, $3 \cdot \mathrm{labeledRiordanCount}(n) = 4^{n - 1} + 2$.*

*Proof.*
- **Injectivity (`graphOfCanonParam_injective`):** Suppose two canonical parameters produce equal graphs. By Lemma 3.1, column $c = 0$ equates $g_1 \equiv g_2 \pmod{t^{n - 1}}$. Since $(0, 0)$ has zero $(n - 1)$-jet whereas $t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})$ has first nonzero bit $[t^r]g = 1$ at $r < n - 1$, the stratum (`inl` vs. `inr` and valuation $r = n - 2 - k$) and all $k$ bits of $\mathrm{gb}$ are uniquely determined. When $k \ge 1$ ($n - 1 \ge 2$), column $c = 1$ gives $t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})(f_1 + f_2) \equiv 0 \pmod{t^{n - 1}}$. Stripping $t^r$ (`EqMod.X_pow_mul_zero_iff`) and canceling the unit $\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})$ (`EqMod.cancel_unit_zero`) gives $f_1 \equiv f_2 \pmod{t^{k + 1}}$, fixing $\mathrm{fb}$.
- **Surjectivity (`graphOfCanonParam_surjective`):** Given any $(g, f)$ with $f(0) = 0$, either $[t^d]g = 0$ for all $d < n - 1$—so $g f^c \equiv 0 \pmod{t^{n - 1}}$ and $G = G_n(0, 0)$—or $r := \min\{d < n - 1 : [t^d]g \ne 0\}$ satisfies $[t^r]g = 1$. Setting $k = n - 2 - r$, reading $\mathrm{gb}_j = [t^{r + j + 1}]g$ and $\mathrm{fb}_j = [t^{j + 1}]f$ ($0 \le j < k$) gives $g \equiv t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb}) \pmod{t^{n - 1}}$ and $f \equiv \mathrm{fOfBits}(\mathrm{fb}) \pmod{t^{k + 1}}$. For every $c \ge 1$, $f^c \equiv \mathrm{fOfBits}(\mathrm{fb})^c \pmod{t^{k + 1}}$; multiplying by $t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})$ lifts this congruence to modulo $t^{r + k + 1} = t^{n - 1}$, so Lemma 3.1 yields equality of graphs.
- **Cardinality:** Summing $|\mathrm{CanonParam}(n)| = 1 + \sum_{k = 0}^{n - 2} 2^k \cdot 2^k = 1 + \sum_{k = 0}^{n - 2} 4^k$ gives $3\,L(n) = 4^{n - 1} + 2$ by induction on $n - 1$. $\square$

### 3.4 Step 3: Complementation as Appell Translation and the $g_0 = 0$ Doubling Involution

Let $u(t) := \sum_{d \ge 0} t^d \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ (`uSeries`), which satisfies $(1 + t)\,u(t) = 1$ (`one_add_X_mul_uSeries`) and $[t^r](u(t)\,t^c) = \mathbf{1}_{c \le r}$ (`coeff_uSeries_mul_X_pow`). Because flipping every off-diagonal edge $\{i, j\}$ ($j < i$) adds $1 \pmod 2$ at every entry $0 \le c \le r < n - 1$ while preserving $0$ for $r < c$, we obtain (`compl_eq_riordanGraph_iff`, `compl_isLabeledRiordan_iff`):

**Lemma 3.3 (`compl_isLabeledRiordan_iff`).** *For $f(0) = 0$, `(riordanGraph n g f)ᶜ` is a labeled Riordan graph on `Fin n` if and only if there exist $g', f' \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ with $f'(0) = 0$ such that*
$$\forall c < n - 1,\qquad \mathrm{EqMod}\;(n - 1)\;(g' {f'}^c)\;(u(t)\,t^c + g\,f^c).$$

When $n \ge 2$, the pair $(i, j) = (1, 0)$ exists in `Fin n`, and `(riordanGraph n g f).Adj 1 0` holds if and only if $g_0 = [t^0]g = 1$ (`riordanGraph_not_adj_one_zero_iff`). Because $(G^c)^c = G$ and $G^c.\mathrm{Adj}\;1\;0 \iff \neg G.\mathrm{Adj}\;1\;0$, sending `Sum.inl G ↦ G` and `Sum.inr G ↦ Gᶜ` defines an explicit bijection (`complDoubleEquiv`):
$$\mathrm{CCSetZero}(n) \oplus \mathrm{CCSetZero}(n) \;\simeq\; \mathrm{CCSet}(n) \qquad (n \ge 2),$$
reducing the full enumeration at order $n \ge 2$ to doubling the $g_0 = 0$ slice `CCSetZero n`. (At $n = 1$, `Fin 1` has no off-diagonal vertex pairs, so $\perp^c = \perp$ is fixed and $a(1) = 1$.)

## 4. Steps 4–5: Master Characterization Lemma and Closed-Layer Enumeration

### 4.1 Step 4: Proof of the Master Characterization Lemma (`master_characterization_on_g0_zero`)

Restricting to $g_0 = 0$ eliminates all zero-divisor obstructions on $g' \equiv u + g$: because $[t^0]u = 1$ and $[t^0]g = 0$, the series $g' := u + g$ has constant term $1$, making $g'$ an **invertible unit** in $\mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$!

**Theorem 4.1 (`master_characterization_on_g0_zero`).** *Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $g, f \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$ satisfy $f(0) = 0$ and $g(0) = 0$. Then `(riordanGraph n g f)ᶜ` is a labeled Riordan graph on `Fin n` if and only if*
$$\mathrm{EqMod}\;(n - 1)\;\bigl(g\,(f + t)^2\bigr)\;0.$$

*Proof.* Set $m := n - 1$. By Lemma 3.3, we must show that $g', f'$ with $f'(0) = 0$ solving $g' {f'}^c \equiv u t^c + g f^c \pmod{t^m}$ for all $c < m$ exist if and only if $g(f + t)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{t^m}$.

- **($\Longrightarrow$) Necessity:** If $m \le 2$ ($n \le 3$), $g(0) = 0 \implies t \mid g$ and $f(0) = 0 \implies t \mid (f + t)$, so $t^3 \mid g(f + t)^2$, which vanishes modulo $t^m$ automatically. If $m \ge 3$, columns $c = 0, 1, 2$ all satisfy $c < m$:
  $$g' \equiv u + g, \qquad g' f' \equiv u t + g f, \qquad g' {f'}^2 \equiv u t^2 + g f^2 \pmod{t^m}.$$
  Since $g'(g'{f'}^2) = (g'f')^2$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$, multiplying the $c = 0$ and $c = 2$ congruences and adding the square of the $c = 1$ congruence in characteristic $2$ gives (`delta_identity`)
  $$0 \;\equiv\; (u + g)(u t^2 + g f^2) + (u t + g f)^2 \;=\; u\,g\,(f^2 + t^2) \;=\; u\,g\,(f + t)^2 \pmod{t^m}.$$
  Multiplying modulo $t^m$ by $1 + t = u^{-1}$ (`one_add_X_mul_uSeries`) yields $g(f + t)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{t^m}$.

- **($\Longleftarrow$) Sufficiency via unit inversion and the $3$-term $Y_c$ recurrence:** Assume $g(f + t)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{t^m}$, so $t^m \mid \Delta$ where $\Delta := u\,g\,(f + t)^2$. Set
  $$g' := u + g, \qquad X_{\mathrm{num}} := u t + g f, \qquad f' := (g')^{-1}\,X_{\mathrm{num}},$$
  where $(g')^{-1} := \mathrm{PowerSeries.invOfUnit}\;g'\;1$ satisfies $g'(g')^{-1} = (g')^{-1}g' = 1$ since $[t^0]g' = 1 + 0 = 1$. Because $t \mid t$ and $t \mid f$, we have $t \mid X_{\mathrm{num}}$ and hence $f'(0) = 0$. Column $c = 0$ ($g' \equiv u + g$) holds identically.

  For $c = k + 1 \ge 1$, clear denominators by setting
  $$W_k := (g')^k\bigl(u\,t^{k + 1} + g\,f^{k + 1}\bigr), \qquad Y_k := W_k + X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 1} \in \mathbb{F}_2[[t]],$$
  together with the symmetric elementary coefficients $S := g'(t + f)$ and $P := (g')^2 t f$. Direct ring calculation over $\mathbb{F}_2$ verifies:
  1. $Y_0 = (u t + g f) + (u t + g f) = 0$, and $Y_1 = (u + g)(u t^2 + g f^2) + (u t + g f)^2 = \Delta$ (`delta_identity`);
  2. $W_{k + 2} = S\,W_{k + 1} + P\,W_k$ (since $t$ and $f$ satisfy the characteristic quadratic $z^2 = (t + f)z + t f$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$); and
  3. $X_{\mathrm{num}}^2 = S\,X_{\mathrm{num}} + P + \Delta$ (`xnum_sq_identity`), which multiplied by $X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 1}$ gives $X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 3} = S\,X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 2} + P\,X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 1} + \Delta\,X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 1}$.

  Adding (2) and (3) yields the exact three-term linear recurrence in $\mathbb{F}_2[[t]]$:
  $$Y_{k + 2} = S\,Y_{k + 1} + P\,Y_k + \Delta\,X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 1} \qquad (k \ge 0).$$
  By strong induction on $k$, $\Delta \mid Y_k$ for every $k \ge 0$. Since $t^m \mid \Delta$, we have $t^m \mid Y_k$, i.e., $\mathrm{EqMod}\;m\;W_k\;X_{\mathrm{num}}^{k + 1}$. Multiplying both sides modulo $t^m$ by $((g')^{-1})^k$ cancels $(g')^k$ on the left to give $u\,t^{k + 1} + g\,f^{k + 1}$ and simplifies $((g')^{-1})^k (g' f')^{k + 1} = g' (f')^{k + 1}$ on the right, establishing every column $c = k + 1$. $\square$

*Remark (Full Labeled Solution to Cheon et al.'s Problem 3).* Because $G \mapsto G^c$ is an involution swapping $g_0 = 0$ and $g_0 = 1$ for $n \ge 2$, Theorem 4.1 characterizes **all** complement-closed labeled Riordan graphs of order $n \ge 2$: the $g_0 = 0$ graphs are precisely $G_n(g, f)$ with $g(f + t)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{t^{n - 1}}$, and the $g_0 = 1$ graphs are precisely their explicit complements $G_n\bigl(u + g,\; (u + g)^{-1}(u t + g f)\bigr)$.

### 4.2 Step 5: Exact Layer Count and Recurrence Closure (`count_eq_closed_layer_sum`, `complementClosed_riordan_count_eq`)

Fix $n \ge 2$ and examine a canonical parameter $p \in \mathrm{CanonParam}(n)$ under the conditions $\neg G.\mathrm{Adj}\;1\;0$ ($g_0 = 0$) and $G^c$ labeled Riordan (`canonParam_in_ccSetZero_iff`):
- **Null stratum (`Sum.inl ()`):** $(g, f) = (0, 0)$ has $g_0 = 0$ and $0 \cdot (0 + t)^2 = 0$, contributing **$1$ graph** ($\perp$).
- **Valuation-$r$ strata (`Sum.inr ⟨k, (gb, fb)⟩`):** Here $g = t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})$ with $r = n - 2 - k$. First, $g_0 = 0 \iff r \ge 1 \iff k < n - 2$ (excluding the $r = 0$, $k = n - 2$ layer where $g_0 = 1$). Second, for $0 \le k < n - 2$, stripping $t^r$ and canceling the unit $\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})$ via Section 3.1 transforms Theorem 4.1 into
  $$\mathrm{EqMod}\;(n - 1)\;\bigl(t^r\,\mathrm{vOfBits}(\mathrm{gb})\,(\mathrm{fOfBits}(\mathrm{fb}) + t)^2\bigr)\;0 \;\iff\; \mathrm{EqMod}\;(k + 1)\;\bigl((\mathrm{fOfBits}(\mathrm{fb}) + t)^2\bigr)\;0.$$
  By Frobenius squaring (`coeff_sq_zmod2`), the odd-degree coefficients of $(\mathrm{fOfBits}(\mathrm{fb}) + t)^2$ vanish identically, while the even-degree coefficients $2(j + 1) < k + 1$ ($0 \le j < \lfloor k/2 \rfloor$) read $\mathrm{fb}_j + \mathbf{1}_{j = 0} = 0$ (`fOfBits_add_X_sq_eqMod_zero_iff`). Thus `fb` is valid if and only if
  $$\mathrm{fb}_j = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } j = 0, \\ 0 & \text{if } 1 \le j < \lfloor k/2 \rfloor, \end{cases}$$
  leaving the remaining $k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ coordinates $\mathrm{fb}_{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor}, \dots, \mathrm{fb}_{k - 1}$ completely free (`validFBitsEquiv`).

Combining `validFBitsEquiv` across layers $0 \le k < n - 2$ yields an explicit bijection `validZeroParamEquiv : ValidZeroParam n ≃ CCSetZero n hn`, where layer $k$ has $2^k$ choices for `gb` and $2^{k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}$ choices for the free tail of `fb`. Doubling via `complDoubleEquiv` gives (`count_eq_closed_layer_sum`):
$$a(n) = 2\left(1 + \sum_{k = 0}^{n - 3} 2^{2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}\right) \qquad \text{for all } n \ge 2.$$

Closing the three pinned theorems in `SeamC/A401496.lean` now takes fewer than $60$ lines:
1. **`n = 1` (`complementClosed_riordan_count_one`):** `SimpleGraph (Fin 1)` is a singleton $\{\perp\}$ with $\perp^c = \perp$ and $(0, 0)$ witnessing `IsLabeledRiordanGraph 1 ⊥`, so `Nat.card_unique` gives $a(1) = 1$.
2. **`n = 2` (`complementClosed_riordan_count_two`):** `count_eq_closed_layer_sum` at $n = 2$ has empty range `Finset.range 0`, giving $a(2) = 2(1 + 0) = 2$.
3. **`n ≥ 3` (`complementClosed_riordan_count_eq`):**
   - For $n = 3$, `Finset.range 1` has the single term $k = 0$ ($2^0 = 1$), so $a(3) = 2(1 + 1) = 4 = 8 \cdot 1 - 4 = 8\,a(1) - 4$.
   - For $n = m + 4 \ge 4$, let $f(k) := 2^{2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor}$. Peeling $k = 0$ ($f(0) = 1$) and $k = 1$ ($f(1) = 4$) from $\sum_{k = 0}^{m + 1} f(k)$ via `Finset.sum_range_succ'` and applying $2(k + 2) - \lfloor (k + 2)/2 \rfloor = 3 + (2k - \lfloor k/2 \rfloor)$, i.e. $f(k + 2) = 8\,f(k)$, gives
     $$a(m + 4) = 2\left(1 + 1 + 4 + 8 \sum_{k = 0}^{m - 1} f(k)\right) = 12 + 16 \sum_{k = 0}^{m - 1} f(k) = 8 \cdot 2\left(1 + \sum_{k = 0}^{m - 1} f(k)\right) - 4 = 8\,a(m + 2) - 4. \quad \square$$
