## 2. Step-by-Step Reduction Check (`A267442` $\leftrightarrow$ Rule 126 Middle Column)

**Verdict on Reduction:** **VERIFIED (EXACT).** Mathar's conjecture `A267442(n) = A043545(n)` for all $n > 0$ is **logically equivalent** to the Rule 126 middle-column claim `C_126(n, 0) = A036987(n)` for all $n \ge 0$ (and equivalently for all $n \ge 1$, since $C_{126}(0, 0) = 1 = A036987(0)$ holds by definition).

### 2.1 Exact Definitions and Truth Tables

For an elementary cellular automaton (ECA) with Wolfram rule number $R = \sum_{i=0}^{7} b_i(R) 2^i \in \{0, \dots, 255\}$, the local transition function $f_R : \{0, 1\}^3 \to \{0, 1\}$ is $f_R(p, q, r) = b_{4p + 2q + r}(R)$. Since $126 = (01111110)_2$ and $129 = (10000001)_2 = 255 - 126$:

| $(p, q, r)$ | $111$ (7) | $110$ (6) | $101$ (5) | $100$ (4) | $011$ (3) | $010$ (2) | $001$ (1) | $000$ (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $f_{126}(p,q,r)$ | $0$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $f_{129}(p,q,r)$ | $1$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $1$ |

Thus $f_{126}(p, q, r) = 0 \iff p = q = r$, while $f_{129}(p, q, r) = 1 \iff p = q = r$.

From the single-ON initial configuration (matching `CellularAutomaton[rule, {{1}, 0}, ...]` in `A267442`, `A267417`, `A267366`, and `A071035`),
$$C_R(0, x) = \begin{cases} 1, & x = 0, \\ 0, & x \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}, \end{cases}
\qquad
C_R(n+1, x) = f_R\bigl(C_R(n, x-1),\, C_R(n, x),\, C_R(n, x+1)\bigr) \quad (n \ge 0,\; x \in \mathbb{Z}).$$

### 2.2 Proof that $C_{129}(n, x) = 1 - C_{126}(n, x)$ for all $n \ge 1$ and all $x \in \mathbb{Z}$

Note a crucial subtlety: pure complementation conjugacy $C(129) = 126$, namely
$$f_{129}(1-p, 1-q, 1-r) = 1 - f_{126}(p, q, r) \quad \forall (p, q, r) \in \{0, 1\}^3, \tag{C1}$$
by itself relates $F_{129}^n(c_0)$ to $1 - F_{126}^n(\overline{c_0})$ starting from the **complemented** seed $\overline{c_0}$ (a single `0` in an infinite sea of `1`s), *not* from the single-ON seed $c_0$. The reason the reduction holds from the **same** single-ON seed $c_0$ for all $n \ge 1$ is that $f_{126}$ is *also* symmetric under Simultaneous Input Complementation ($f_{126}(1-p, 1-q, 1-r) = f_{126}(p, q, r)$, since complementing inputs swaps $000 \leftrightarrow 111$ and permutes the six non-uniform triples), so $f_{129}$ is simultaneously the **output negation** of $f_{126}$:
$$f_{129}(p, q, r) = 1 - f_{126}(p, q, r) \quad \forall (p, q, r) \in \{0, 1\}^3. \tag{C0}$$

- **Failure at $n = 0$:** $C_{129}(0, x) = C_{126}(0, x) = \mathbf{1}_{x = 0}$, so $C_{129}(0, x) \ne 1 - C_{126}(0, x)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{Z}$ (in particular $C_{129}(0, 0) = 1 \ne 0 = 1 - C_{126}(0, 0)$).
- **Base case $n = 1$ (via $\text{C0}$):** Since $C_{129}(0, y) = C_{126}(0, y)$ for all $y \in \mathbb{Z}$, identity $(\text{C0})$ gives, for every $x \in \mathbb{Z}$:
  $$C_{129}(1, x) = f_{129}\bigl(C_{126}(0, x-1), C_{126}(0, x), C_{126}(0, x+1)\bigr) = 1 - f_{126}\bigl(C_{126}(0, x-1), C_{126}(0, x), C_{126}(0, x+1)\bigr) = 1 - C_{126}(1, x).$$

  &= f_{129}\bigl(C_{129}(n, x-1),\, C_{129}(n, x),\, C_{129}(n, x+1)\bigr) \\
  &= f_{129}\bigl(1 - C_{126}(n, x-1),\, 1 - C_{126}(n, x),\, 1 - C_{126}(n, x+1)\bigr) \\

## 4. Proof-Strategy Sketch *(Strategy Only — No Proof Attempt)*

### 4.1 Why Direct Linearity and `Doubling.lean` Both Fail on the Single-ON Seed

Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, Rule 126 has algebraic normal form
$$f_{126}(p, q, r) = (p \oplus q) \lor (q \oplus r) = p \oplus q \oplus r \oplus pq \oplus qr \oplus pr.$$
It is **genuinely nonlinear on its single-ON-seed orbit**:
- At every pre-Mersenne time $n = 2^{m+1} - 2$ ($m \ge 1$, i.e., $n = 2, 6, 14, 30, \dots$), the left and right wave-packets reach cells $x = -1$ and $x = +1$ while cell $x = 0$ is `0`, forming the colliding-wave neighborhood $(p, q, r) = (1, 0, 1)$ at the origin. Additive Rule 90 ($p \oplus r$) outputs $1 \oplus 1 = 0$, whereas $f_{126}(1, 0, 1) = 1$ fuses the two inner fronts into a solid block of $2^{m+2} - 1$ ones at step $n + 1 = 2^{m+1} - 1$.
- Likewise, `LocalRule.finRule_two_dbl` (`lividgreen/lean-elementary-cellular-automata`, `ECA/Doubling.lean`) applies **only** to configurations in the image of `dbl` ($j \mapsto x(j/2)$, support size always even). Because the single-ON seed has odd support size $1$ and produces row $2$ equal to `11011` (two `11` pairs separated by a **width-1 odd gap** at $x = 0$), the single-seed orbit **never** lies in `Set.range dbl`.

### 4.2 Recommended Lean Strategy (Route A — Global Closed-Form Binomial-Parity Invariant)

Instead of splitting into era boundaries $[2^m - 1, 2^{m+1} - 1]$ with two shifted `dbl` light-cones, the entire nonlinear collision at $x = 0$ is absorbed by a **single global closed-form invariant** across all $(n, x) \in \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{Z}$:

- **Key Lemma A1 (2-Equal-Inputs XOR Law for $f_{126}$):** Since $f_{126}(p, q, r) = 0 \iff p = q = r$ over `Bool` ($\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$), whenever **any two specified positions** of $(p, q, r)$ are known structurally to be equal — including the **outer symmetric pair $(a, b, a)$** — $f_{126}$ reduces to XOR of one of the equal inputs with the third:
  $$f_{126}(a, a, b) = a \oplus b, \qquad f_{126}(a, b, b) = a \oplus b, \qquad f_{126}(a, b, a) = a \oplus b.$$
  *(Crucially, $f_{126}(a, b, a) = a \oplus b$ turns the colliding-wave nonlinearity $f_{126}(1, 0, 1) = 1$ at $x = 0$ into $1 \oplus 0 = 1$, i.e., ordinary mod-2 Pascal addition of cell $-1$ and cell $0$!)*

- **Key Lemma A2 (Global Space-Time Closed Form for Rule 126):** Define $B : \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{Z} \to \mathrm{Bool}$ by
  $$B(n, x) := \begin{cases} \left(\dbinom{n}{\lfloor (n - |x|) / 2 \rfloor} \bmod 2 = 1\right), & \text{if } |x| \le n, \\[8pt] \mathrm{false}, & \text{if } |x| > n. \end{cases}$$
  *(Remark: By one step of Lucas's theorem $\binom{2n+1}{u} \equiv \binom{n}{\lfloor u/2 \rfloor} \pmod 2$, $B(n, x)$ for $|x| \le n$ also equals $\binom{2n+1}{n-|x|} \bmod 2$, simultaneously proving the unproved `A071035` $\leftrightarrow$ `A127872` right-half identification.)*
  Then **for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and all $x \in \mathbb{Z}$, $C_{126}(n, x) = B(n, x)$**, by a single induction on $n$:
  1. **Base $n = 0$:** $B(0, 0) = (\binom{0}{0} \bmod 2 = 1) = \mathrm{true}$ and $B(0, x) = \mathrm{false}$ for $|x| \ge 1$.
  2. **Step $n \to n + 1$:** Since $B(n, -x) = B(n, x)$ and $f_{126}(r, q, p) = f_{126}(p, q, r)$, spatial reflection reduces $x < 0$ to $x > 0$, leaving five clean cases on $x \ge 0$:
     - *Exterior $x \ge n + 2$:* $(p, q, r) = (0, 0, 0) \mapsto 0 = B(n+1, x)$.
     - *Outer edge $x = n + 1$:* $(p, q, r) = (1, 0, 0) \mapsto 1 = \binom{n+1}{0} \bmod 2 = B(n+1, n+1)$.
     - *Inner edge $x = n \ge 1$:* $(p, q, r) = (1, 1, 0) \mapsto 1 = \binom{n+1}{0} \bmod 2 = B(n+1, n)$.
     - *Interior $1 \le x \le n - 1$:* Let $u = n - x \in \{1, \dots, n - 1\}$.
       - If $u = 2j$ ($j \ge 1$), then $\lfloor (u+1)/2 \rfloor = \lfloor u/2 \rfloor = j$ and $\lfloor (u-1)/2 \rfloor = j - 1$, so **structurally $p = q = \binom{n}{j} \bmod 2$**, and Lemma A1 gives $f_{126}(p, p, r) = p \oplus r \equiv \binom{n}{j} + \binom{n}{j-1} = \binom{n+1}{j} \pmod 2 = B(n+1, x)$.
       - If $u = 2j + 1$ ($j \ge 0$), then $\lfloor u/2 \rfloor = \lfloor (u-1)/2 \rfloor = j$ and $\lfloor (u+1)/2 \rfloor = j + 1$, so **structurally $q = r = \binom{n}{j} \bmod 2$**, and Lemma A1 gives $f_{126}(p, q, q) = p \oplus q \equiv \binom{n}{j+1} + \binom{n}{j} = \binom{n+1}{j+1} \pmod 2 = B(n+1, x)$.
     - *Colliding-wave origin $x = 0$:* At $n = 0$, $(0, 1, 0) \mapsto 1 = B(1, 0)$. For $n \ge 1$, spatial reflection forces **$p = B(n, -1) = B(n, 1) = r$**, so the origin neighborhood is always symmetric $(p, q, p)$ and Lemma A1 gives $f_{126}(p, q, p) = p \oplus q$:
       - If $n = 2k$ ($k \ge 1$): $p = \binom{2k}{k-1} \bmod 2$ and $q = \binom{2k}{k} \bmod 2$, so $p \oplus q \equiv \binom{2k}{k-1} + \binom{2k}{k} = \binom{2k+1}{k} \pmod 2 = B(2k+1, 0)$.
       - If $n = 2k + 1$ ($k \ge 0$): $\lfloor 2k/2 \rfloor = k = \lfloor (2k+1)/2 \rfloor$, so $p = q = r$ and $f_{126}(q, q, q) = 0 = \binom{2k+2}{k+1} \bmod 2 = B(2k+2, 0)$ (since $\binom{2k+2}{k+1} = 2\binom{2k+1}{k}$ is even for every $k \ge 0$).

- **Key Lemma A3 (Central Binomial Parity `A001405(n) mod 2 = A036987(n)`):** Specializing Lemma A2 to $x = 0$ gives $C_{126}(n, 0) = g(n) := \binom{n}{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \bmod 2$. Then $g$ satisfies Zumkeller's binary recurrence (`%F A036987`):
  - $g(0) = 1$;
  - $g(2k) = 0$ for all $k \ge 1$, because $\binom{2k}{k} = \binom{2k-1}{k-1} + \binom{2k-1}{k} = 2\binom{2k-1}{k-1}$;
  - $g(2k + 1) = g(k)$ for all $k \ge 0$, by one step of Lucas's theorem mod 2 (`Nat.choose_modEq_choose_mod_mul_choose_div_nat` at $p = 2$: $\binom{2k+1}{k} \equiv \binom{k}{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} \binom{1}{k \bmod 2} = \binom{k}{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} \pmod 2$ for *both* parities of $k$).
  Immediate binary strong induction on $n$ yields $g(n) = 1 \iff \exists m \ge 0,\; n = 2^m - 1$.

- **Key Lemma A4 (Complementation Bridge to Rule 129 and `A043545`):** Combine `ecaOrbit 129 n x = !(ecaOrbit 126 n x)` for all $n \ge 1$ (§2.2, 10 lines by induction on $n \ge 1$) with `a043545 n = 1 - a036987 n` (§2.3, ~15 lines on `Nat.digits 2 n`) to conclude `a267442 n = a043545 n` for all $n > 0$.

### 4.3 Alternative Conceptual Route (Route B — Era / Block-Doubling Induction)

An alternative geometric route inducts on $m \ge 0$ that row $2^m - 1$ of Rule 126 is the solid interval $[- (2^m - 1),\, 2^m - 1]$ of $2^{m+1} - 1$ ones: step $2^m$ annihilates the interior (`111 -> 0`) into two isolated `11` blocks at $\pm\{2^m - 1, 2^m\}$; for $0 \le t \le 2^m - 2$ finite propagation speed keeps the two half-cones strictly separated across $x = 0$ (`000 -> 0`) while each half evolves via `DoubledAgree` as `dbl` of Rule 90; at $t = 2^m - 2$ ($n = 2^{m+1} - 2$) both half-cones become all-ones `11...11` up to $x = \pm 1$, and one step of $f_{126}(1, 0, 1) = 1$ at $x = 0$ closes the induction at $n = 2^{m+1} - 1$. **Assessment:** Route A is strictly preferable in Lean 4 (~60–90 lines vs ~200+ lines for Route B) because Route A requires zero shifted `dbl` embeddings, zero era-decomposition arithmetic $n = 2^m + t$, and proves the entire `A071035` / `A127872` closed form at once.

### 4.4 Main Obstacles / Formalization Hazards to Guard Against

1. **Infinite-line carrier `Int → Bool` is mandatory (do NOT use `Finsupp` or finite windows):** Because $f_{129}(0, 0, 0) = 1$, for every $n \ge 1$ the configuration $C_{129}(n, \cdot)$ is `true` at all $|x| > n$ (infinite support). Representing configurations via `ℤ →₀ ZMod 2` (`Finsupp`, as in `Doubling.lean`) or zero-padded finite arrays falsifies Rule 129's boundary transitions after step $1$. Defining `ecaStep` over total functions `Int → Bool` handles quiescent Rule 126 and non-quiescent Rule 129 uniformly.
2. **`Nat` truncated subtraction and `Int.natAbs` sign splits:** In Lemma A2, `n - x.natAbs` must stay guarded behind `if h : x.natAbs ≤ n` so exterior cells $|x| > n$ never evaluate `Nat.choose n ((n - x.natAbs) / 2)` at truncated `0` (which would give $\binom{n}{0} = 1$ instead of `0`!). Similarly, splitting $x = 0$ separately from $1 \le x \le n - 1$ avoids `Int.natAbs (x - 1)` sign-flip casework.
3. **Source-faithful definition of `A043545` in the Pin Manifest:** Defining `a043545 n` as `1 - a036987 n` in the frozen statement file would bake Michael Somos's `%F` reduction into the definition rather than proving Clark Kimberling's `%N` primary definition `(Maximal base-2 digit of n) - (minimal base-2 digit of n)`. Pinning `a043545` directly via `Nat.digits 2 n` avoids any shortcut.
