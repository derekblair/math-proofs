# Finite marked clusters cannot improve radial Davies clipping

This is a model-specific comparison to the optimal singleton Poisson construction, for Poisson and translation-invariant determinantal parents. It neither bounds the unrestricted unit-distance chromatic number nor excludes other center processes and cuts.

Use open seed balls of radius R=(1−ε)/2 ≤1/2 and gap A=1+ε, with ε≥0 tending to zero. Put g(r)=max_{−1≤t≤1}(A+2rt)√(1−t²) for 0≤r≤R. Each center defines the usual symmetric radial cuts against all other centers: for displacement su, an offset x is retained only when x·u<(s−A)/2. Own-cell diameter is ≤2R and different pieces have separation>A. The blocker set F_x={su:0<s≤A+2x·u} satisfies |F_x|≍v_d g(||x||)^d uniformly by the radial saddle estimate below. Marks may have arbitrarily dependent points within one pattern, but are i.i.d. over the parents and independent of their locations.

## An enclosing ball for the blocker

For r=||x||>0, let e=x/r, α(t)=A+2rt, t=u·e, and t* be the maximizer of G(t)=α(t)√(1−t²). Since A≥2r, t*∈[0,1/2]. Let z*=α(t*)t*=2r(1−t*²), by stationarity. The full blocker lies in the ball of radius g(r) centered at z*e. Indeed at s=α(t), its squared distance to z*e is α(t)^2+z*²−2α(t)z*t, a quadratic in t with coefficient 4r(r−z*)≤0. The derivative vanishes at t*: the perpendicular-radius squared already has derivative zero there and the axial coordinate is centered. Thus its maximum is g(r)^2. For fixed direction, distance squared is convex in s, so it suffices also to check s=0, where z*≤g(r) because t*≤1/2. For r=0 the blocker is a usual ball centered at zero.

If ||w||≥A, two copies of that enclosing ball shifted by w have intersection contained in the ball of radius √(g(r)^2−A²/4) about their midpoint (empty if the radicand is negative), by averaging squared distances to their centers. Therefore
|F_x∩(F_x+w)| ≤ C[1−A²/(4g(r)^2)]^{d/2}|F_x|.
In the unit-distance asymptotic this is ≤ C(23/27+o(1))^{d/2}|F_x| uniformly in r. In fact monotonicity of g and A≥1 imply g(r)/A≤g_(A=1)(1/2)=3√3/4, so 23/27 can be taken without an o(1) term whenever R=(1−ε)/2.

## Poisson cluster theorem

Let parent locations form a stationary homogeneous Poisson process of arbitrary intensity J. Each parent independently receives a random finite Borel pattern P, with any internally dependent point arrangement, and emits the translated child centers P. Orientations, sizes, and positions inside P can be chosen jointly and depend on dimension. Assume M_d is a deterministic upper bound on size. Distinct positions may be assumed within each pattern (replace duplicates by one center). Complementary radial cuts between TWO centers at distance ≤A need not empty either cell: each may keep an outward cap. The good and bad analysis below explicitly accounts for this.

If each pattern is already uniformly separated by ≥A, ignore same-parent cuts for an upper bound. For target offset x from one child, a parent with mark Q blocks it exactly for parent positions in a translate of U_Q=⋃_(q∈Q)(F_x−q). The pairwise blocker-intersection bound and Bonferroni yield |U_Q|≥ |Q||F_x|[1−C M_d(23/27)^(d/2)]. Thus for any κ<(1/2)log(27/23), about 0.0802, if M_d≤exp(κd), the bracket is 1−o(1), uniformly in marks and x. With I=J E|P|, Campbell–Mecke and the marked Poisson void formula yield E[density]≤I∫_(B_R)exp[−(1−o(1))I |F_x|]dx. This is bounded by (1+o(1)) times the best singleton Poisson expectation, by rescaling I.

For arbitrary patterns choose δ in (0,1) fixed and classify a child as bad if it has a sibling at distance <a=A−δ. The cut from one such sibling requires x·u<−δ/2, for a direction u fixed conditional on the target mark. On every radius r≤R the allowed directions therefore have fraction at most poly(d)[1−(δ/(2R))²]^(d/2), uniformly; if r<δ/2 they are empty. This standard spherical cap bound follows directly by integrating the coordinate density proportional to (1−t²)^((d−3)/2), and the fixed positive δ absorbs constants in the exponent. Conditional survival of any given x against other parents is at most exp(−J₀ |F_x|), where J₀=J·Pr(P nonempty), because every nonempty pattern gives a blocker union of volume at least |F_x|. This upper is radial and can multiply the angular cap fraction. The intensity of bad children is at most M_d J₀. Thus their total expected retained density is at most M_d poly(d)[1−(δ/(2R))²]^(d/2) times the best singleton radial Poisson expectation, uniformly in J.

For good children, remove all bad blockers (only enlarges their retained pieces) and ignore same-parent cuts. Each remaining pattern has minimum spacing ≥a, so repeat the first estimate with overlap fraction at most C[1−a²/(4g(R)²)]^(d/2). The good contribution is at most (1+o(1)) times the best singleton radial Poisson expectation whenever M_d times that fraction tends to zero. Combining gives that arbitrary patterns of size at most exp(κd) still cannot improve the sharp radial prefactor when
κ<min{−(1/2)log(1−δ²), −(1/2)log(1−(1−δ)²/(4g_*²))},
where g_*=3√3/4 and ε→0. The first term is increasing in δ and the second decreasing, so optimum is δ=1/(1+2g_*), where they are equal, giving κ≈0.0402. Uniformly separated patterns retain the stronger 0.0802 threshold. Sharpness within all covered models follows because singleton patterns attain asymptotically c_* times (R/g(R))^d. No dependence of target and other parent marks was assumed beyond iid marking, and the center-intensity rescalings are uniform.

Not covered by the Poisson argument: larger cluster sizes beyond these thresholds, coupling different parents, nonradial separators, or intentionally using some distances <1 between members of different seed pieces rather than imposing the global far-component condition. No construction with a better exponential rate was verified.

## Translation-invariant determinantal parents

The same sharp ceiling holds when the parents are a stationary convolution determinantal point process with Fourier symbol 0≤φ≤1 and integrable φ, while the independent marks follow the cluster hypotheses above. Put J=∫φ and K=φ̌ under the Fourier convention 2π. Under reduced Palm at a parent, the kernel is K⁰(z,w)=K(z−w)−K(z) overline(K(w))/J, by Shirai–Takahashi, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1236(03)00171-X. A blocker hit probability function q(z) from independent marks has Palm void det(I−√q K⁰√q)≤exp(−J∫q+J⁻¹∫q|K|²). The positive contraction and trace estimate apply first to bounded windows, or directly when ∫q is finite. Since ∫|K|²=∫φ²≤J, this is at worst e times the corresponding Poisson upper estimate.

At natural total offspring intensity, the Palm trace correction actually vanishes uniformly. For any spatial ball B(c,g) with g=g(R), majorize its indicator by exp(d/2)exp(−d|z−c|²/(2g²)). Fourier expansion of |K|², the Gaussian transform, and discarding the unit translation phase bound J⁻¹∫_(B(c,g))|K|² by exp(d/2)(2πg²/d)^(d/2) J × E exp[−(2π²g²/d)||Ξ−Η||²], where Ξ and Η are iid with density φ/J. Put h=(J/v_d)^(1/d) and ν=Jv_d g^d. Probability their difference is ≤h/2 is at most 2^−d by the pointwise density cap. On its complement the Gaussian gives exp[−π²g²h²/(2d)] ≤exp(−c d ν^(2/d)), since v_d^(2/d)~2πe/d. The prefactor is O(√d ν) by Stirling. The bounds are uniform in c and φ.

After removing bad children, independently thin empty good-parent marks; this is still a determinantal process with symbol multiplied by the thinning probability. Conditional nonempty marks have mean size m̄≥1, new parent intensity J′, and total good intensity I=J′m̄. The hit function is bounded by the mean sum of m̄ shifted enclosing-ball indicators. At I v_d g^d=Θ(1), multiplying the per-ball trace bound by m̄ cancels the dilution ν=Θ(1)/m̄, while ν^(2/d)≥e^(−2κ+o(1)) when m̄≤exp(κd). Thus the trace correction is o(1) uniformly in x and target position. When the normalized total good intensity tends to zero or infinity, the coarser e-factor suffices because the radial expected-density expression tends to zero at both ends after normalization (use g(r)/g(R)≥(r/R)^(1/3) at the high end). For bad child bounds, thin empty original marks and use the coarse e-factor; the exponential angular loss still makes the bad contribution negligible. The upper benchmark c_* is attained by the Poisson singleton process in the combined comparison; claiming attainment within a narrower class of fixed DPP kernels is unnecessary.

Attainability of the sharp asymptotic supremum even restricted to singleton DPP symbols: at fixed d and J choose φ_η=η 1_E with |E|=J/η and 0<η<1. The rank-one Palm trace correction J⁻¹∫q|K|² is at most J⁻¹∫φ_η²=η. Moreover reduced-Palm K⁰ is dominated by K in positive-operator order, so T=√q K⁰√q has norm at most η. For its trace-class eigenvalues, 0≤−log det(I−T)−tr(T)≤η tr(T)/(1−η), by expansion of −log(1−s). Consequently singleton Palm void (and radial clipped density) converge to the Poisson values as η→0 at fixed d,J, uniformly over target offsets in the seed ball because the blocker measures are uniformly finite. One can let η depend on d and approach the benchmark. This asserts asymptotic sharpness over the symbol family, not exact finite-dimensional equality of its supremum to Poisson.

## Normalization and scope

Use the singleton benchmark
\[
B_d=\sup_{I\ge0} I\int_{B_R}\exp(-I|F_x|)\,dx.
\]
The theorem shows that the supremal expected density over all indicated parent intensities and allowed laws is \((1+o(1))B_d\). The bound is uniform over the laws, even if dimension dependent. One does not need to assume that their near-optimal intensities stabilize. Put \(\lambda=I v_d g(R)^d\) and \(u=(r/R)^d\), uniform under normalized radial volume. The stationarity equation gives \(0\le d\log g/d\log r\le 1/3\): the slope equals \(2rt_*/(A+2rt_*)\), and \(t_*\le 1/2,2r\le A\). Hence \(g(r)/g(R)\ge(r/R)^{1/3}\). Uniform blocker-volume comparability gives
\[
\frac{I\int_{B_R}e^{-aI|F_x|}\,dx}{(R/g(R))^d}
\le \lambda\int_0^1 e^{-a c\lambda u^{1/3}}\,du=O_a(\lambda^{-2})
\]
as \(\lambda\to\infty\), for any fixed \(a>0\). The bound is at most \(\lambda\) at the other end, and \(\lambda=1\) gives a positive uniform lower bound for normalized \(B_d\). In the original blocker estimate one integrates along directions \(t\) with density proportional to \((1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}\). The integrand has exponential part \(G(t)^d\), its unique maximizer in \([0,1/2]\) is interior and uniformly nondegenerate, and the normalizing density constant and the saddle width have orders \(\sqrt d\) and \(d^{-1/2}\). This gives comparability with constants uniform in \(r\le R\) for the unit-distance limit; the end intervals next to \(\pm1\) are exponentially smaller by integrating with exponent \(d-3\).
