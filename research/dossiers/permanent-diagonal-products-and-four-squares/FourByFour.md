# Four-by-four exact complex-magnitude permanent decompositions

**Theorem (written proof).** For real variables X, the polynomial per_4(X^{∘2}) needs exactly ten squares of arbitrary real polynomials, hence exactly five complex polynomial absolute squares. In the stricter model of fixed complex entrywise-masked determinants with nonnegative real weights, it needs exactly six terms. The last lower bound holds also for Boolean-cube equality in this masked model, since row degree two gives unique multilinear-support folding; the arbitrary-polynomial SOS statement is about polynomial identities. Signed weights in the masked model are not covered by this exact n=4 minimum. The general dimension optimum without restrictions remains open.

Write v_σ for the vector of real coefficients of matching monomial X_σ in all squares. Any arbitrary SOS decomposition uses only matching monomials: the support of each squared polynomial before squaring lies in half the target Newton polytope by separating linear weights (nonzero leading real squares can't sum to zero). Half the target Newton polytope is the Birkhoff polytope, whose integer points are permutation matrices. In masked determinants the same monomial support is automatic. We may view complex-norm squared terms as real and imaginary pairs of coordinates; absorb square roots of nonnegative weights into them. The Gram H has diagonal 1. Different permutations of S4 differ either by a single cycle or by a double transposition. For a single cycle, the union of the two matchings has exactly two matchings, so its unordered cross coefficient is 2H_{στ}; hence H_{στ}=0. For a double transposition, the two disjoint switches give four matchings and exactly two unordered opposite pairs with the same monomial; their H entries must sum to 0. Thus H is block diagonal on six cosets of the normal Klein subgroup V4.

The 18 rectangle constraints link the even and odd cosets in a K3,3. The two disjoint-transposition switches have the same image in S4/V4≅S3. For each of three double transpositions d, its component switches image under projection to a distinct transposition of S3 (e.g. identify S3 acting by conjugation on the three nonidentity V4 elements). Between each pair of opposite-parity cosets there are exactly two rectangles, whose pairs on either side form one of the three perfect pairings of that coset's four vertices, namely x↔xd. For two distinct opposite neighbors the resulting pairings differ and together connect that coset.

If one coset has rank one, every pair of its Gram vectors is collinear and all entries are ±1. Each linked pair of its neighbor is forced to have opposite entry ∓1, hence is collinear by equality in Cauchy–Schwarz for unit vectors. If two rank-one cosets are of the same parity, the two matchings they force in each opposite coset connect it, implying every opposite coset has rank one and then all six do. This is impossible by the following odd rectangle certificate: each of nine rectangles would force the product of its four rank-one sign labels to be −1, but every permutation occurs an even number of times in the nine. Thus at most one rank-one coset in each parity, and rank H≥1+1+4·2=10. This bound is attained for arbitrary matching-polynomial SOS: pick one center coset E on even and O on odd side. Give each center a rank-one direction, and each of the four peripheral cosets two orthogonal directions, one for each pair linked to its unique opposite center. Take all six spaces mutually orthogonal. Give E all + signs, choose signs in O independently on its two pairs linked to E so each linked entry is −1. For each peripheral pair choose the pair-sign product opposite its linked center entry. All peripheral-to-peripheral constraints see distinct directions within each peripheral coset, giving zero plus zero. All single-cycle entries are zero and diagonal 1. This explicit Gram has rank ten, factoring into ten real squares. Pair them as real and imaginary parts of five complexes. The mask-status of those arbitrary polynomial coordinates is not assumed.

For actual determinant masks, each weighted atom coordinate c_σ obeys c_σ c_{σtu}=c_{σt} c_{σu} on every rectangle of disjoint switches t,u, since edge multiset and determinant sign product coincide. Assume rank-one cosets E and O of opposite parity, and choose a weighted mask with c_σ=z≠0 at any vertex of E (diagonal H=1 ensures one). Every entry in E is a ±z coefficient as rank-one Gram vectors are unit and collinear in the real space of weighted complex coordinates. For any rectangle from E to an odd coset, the odd pair is collinear with real sign opposite the even-pair sign; combining this relation in the chosen atom with the multiplicative rectangle identity gives c_τ²=−z² at both odd vertices. The two rectangles along any edge cover its opposite coset, so this applies to all odd vertices, in particular the O center. Repeating using O yields c_τ²=z² at all even vertices. Therefore in this single atom every even coefficient is ±z and every odd coefficient ±iz. Its multiplicative identity forces sign product −1 at all 18 rectangles. The same odd nine-set contradicts this. Hence there cannot be two rank-one cosets of opposite parity either; rank H≥1+5·2=11 and at least six complex determinant squares. The paired-row construction in [the main dossier](README.md) gives six upper terms, weighted one. This proof was independently checked.

## Reproduction and scope

Run `node permanent-square-n4-rank-replay.js`. It enumerates the 276 unordered off-diagonal pairs: 240 unique monomials and 18 rectangle collisions, with two pairs per collision. It verifies the nine-rectangle parity certificate and constructs and checks both the rank-ten arbitrary polynomial SOS and the six weighted complex masked squares.

Compile `PermanentSquareFourParity.lean` with `lake env lean -s 65536 /absolute/path/to/PermanentSquareFourParity.lean` under Lean 4.34.1 with Mathlib commit `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`. Lean checks odd count/even incidence (axiom-free) and the nine-equation contradiction over ZMod 2 (standard axioms only). It does not check the Gram expansion, PSD propagation, determinant constraints, or full minima. [Paffenholz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.3948) and [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.12655v2) give related Birkhoff polytope results. No priority claim is made, and the signed masked minimum is not settled.
