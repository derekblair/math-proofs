# Supports of diagonal products of masked determinants

For an n×n variable matrix X and integer q≥2, call a product ∏_{a=1}^q det(M_a∘X) diagonal if its only nonzero monomials have the form X_σ^q, where X_σ=∏_i X_(i,σ(i)). The masks are fixed and need not agree; they may have zeros or arbitrary scalars. In characteristic zero, or in characteristic p>0 with q not a pure p-power, every diagonal product covers at most 2^{floor(n/2)} diagonal monomials. In fact the graph of edges usable by a perfect matching in the common support is a union of even cycles and isolated matching edges whenever there is a perfect matching.

As a consequence, over C the exact minimum number of products in a sum representing per(X^{∘q}), under the condition that each individual product is diagonal, is n!/2^{floor(n/2)}. The expression attaining this count is also an unconditional upper bound when cross-term cancellation is allowed. For q=2 the construction uses complex absolute squares with all weights one. This complements the lower bounds in [PR #100](https://github.com/derekblair/math-proofs/pull/100), including n!/binom(2n,n) when cancellation across complex squares is allowed.

For the powered-square polynomial at order four, the [companion proof](FourByFour.md) gives exact minima of ten arbitrary real polynomial squares, five arbitrary complex polynomial absolute squares, and six nonnegatively weighted complex masked determinant squares. The last bound allows cancellation between different terms. The signed masked minimum is not asserted.

## A constructive matching obstruction

The coefficient of X_σ^q in the product is nonzero precisely when all q masks are nonzero along σ. Restrict the input to variables on the common support. Delete every edge outside all perfect matchings of that support. The remaining graph has the same supported permutations and hence the same diagonal monomials.

Fix one perfect matching P and identify each matched row-column pair with a contracted vertex. For every allowed off-matching edge from row i to the column matched to j, put an arc i→j. Each arc lies on a directed cycle, since a perfect matching containing that edge gives a permutation of the contracted vertices. Conversely any arc in a directed cycle is allowed, by toggling that cycle. The allowed directed subgraph can therefore be computed by keeping arcs within the strongly connected components of the full directed support. If every contracted vertex has outdegree at most one, this graph consists of directed cycles and isolated vertices. Lifting them gives the asserted even cycles and edges.

Otherwise take a vertex with at least two outgoing allowed arcs. Start with a directed simple cycle C using one of them, found by a simple return path. Starting with another outgoing arc, follow a shortest directed return to C, producing an ear whose interior avoids C. It can end at its start. Lifting C produces an alternating even cycle, and lifting the ear via the intervening matching edges gives a simple odd path with endpoints in opposite bipartition classes on the cycle. Even an ear returning to its contracted starting vertex has distinct endpoints in the original graph because they are opposite endpoints of the contracted matching edge. The three odd paths consisting of the ear and the two routes around C form a theta subgraph. Its complement has the restriction of P, because every contracted vertex touched by the theta contributes both original endpoints.

Restrict further to this theta and the fixed complement edges. On the theta both endpoints of the three paths must be matched along the same path, by parity of the internal vertices in the other two paths. This gives exactly three perfect matchings. For each choice, the edge incident to one endpoint on that selected path is used only in its corresponding matching. Assign three independent variables x,y,z to these edges and set all other edges of the restricted support to one. Each masked determinant becomes a linear form in x,y,z with all three coefficients nonzero. A diagonal product would now be
    ∏_{a=1}^q (u_a x+v_a y+w_a z) = A x^q+B y^q+C z^q
with A,B,C all nonzero. If q is invertible in the field, the right side is a smooth projective plane curve over the algebraic closure. A product of two or more lines is singular at an intersection (and also if a line repeats). If the characteristic divides q, strip its maximal p-power by Frobenius over the algebraic closure. The remaining ternary form has exponent prime to p and at least two. Unique factorization implies that if its p-power factors only into linear forms, it too factors only into linear forms. This contradicts smoothness. This is the required contradiction. In the excluded pure characteristic power case, a repeated determinant can itself be diagonal by Frobenius and the obstruction can fail.

For q=2 complex absolute squares there is also a real two-dimensional proof. Two supported matchings differing by one cycle have just two ordered factorizations of their union into matchings. Their cross coefficient is 2 Re(c_σ conj(c_τ)), with each c the complex signed matching coefficient. The three matchings of the theta differ pairwise by one cycle. Three nonzero pairwise-orthogonal vectors in R² are impossible. This connects the result to the bipartite matching-polytope skeleton classification of Naddef and Pulleyblank, but the directed-ear argument is self-contained and constructs an explicit obstruction. With one perfect matching in hand it uses linear graph traversals. [Naddef–Pulleyblank](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895684900431) [Bian–Zhang](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X09001204) [Paffenholz on Birkhoff faces](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v22i1p67/pdf/)

## Exact construction and optimality under the individual-diagonal condition

An even cycle uses at least two matched pairs and has two independent matching options. Isolated edges are forced, so any allowed support here has at most 2^{floor(n/2)} supported permutations. Every target permutation must occur in some summand. This proves the lower bound even if scalar weights between individually diagonal products are signed.

For the upper bound split rows into consecutive pairs and, if n is odd, one singleton. Group the n! permutations by the unordered column set assigned to each row pair and the column assigned to the singleton. Each class has 2^{floor(n/2)} permutations and its support consists of complete 2×2 blocks and an optional singleton. Within a block write x,y for its two matching monomials. For q=2 choose a mask whose determinant on the block is ix−y. Its absolute square on real variables is x²+y². Independently doing so on all blocks gives exactly all class matching squares, hence one complex absolute-square term per class. For general q choose λ_a such that ∏(1+λ_a t)=1+t^q and for the a-th determinant use the block determinant y+λ_a x. Thus each block product is x^q+y^q. Any global column-permutation sign across the q factors is corrected by a fixed scalar weight per class. This also works over any splitting extension containing roots for the binary form. No signed cross-term cancellation is needed. The universal formula has n!/2^{floor(n/2)} summands, so there is a remaining gap to the general lower bound where individual products may have mixed coefficients.

Among 40,315 cases with a perfect matching it constructed 14,622 certified matching triangles and counted 25,693 cycle-and-edge supports. It checked 108 dense integer complex-square identities through n=8, including 72 direct Gaussian determinant norm checks. Five exact cyclotomic identities, 150 direct masked determinant products and 180 dense permanent comparisons check q=2,…,6. ## Equal trimmed support and factor characterization

If the diagonal product is nonzero, each factor has exactly the same supported permutations, not merely a common intersection. To prove it, if σ occurs in one factor but not all, weight variables on edges of σ by one and other variables by zero. Let l<n be the maximum overlap of σ with a common supported matching. Every factor has a matching of weight l, and the factor supporting σ has maximum n. The product's top weighted homogeneous component is the nonzero product of its factors' top components, since the coefficient ring is a field; its degree is at least n+(q−1)l>ql. But its diagonal support is exactly the common matching set, whose highest product weight is ql, a contradiction. Hence after deleting each factor's edges unused by its perfect matchings the resulting supports equal, and for nonpure q they are isolated edges and even cycles. On each such cycle the product of q binary matching linear forms must itself be a two-term qth-power binomial; conversely this condition on each disjoint cycle gives a diagonal product because the cycle sets use disjoint variables. The reason necessity holds locally is that a nonzero mixed monomial on one cycle cannot be canceled by any distinct variables from the other independent cycle factors.

[Paffenholz, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.3948) gives triangle and cube faces of the Birkhoff polytope as related antecedents. The classification and decomposition here are stated for the exact fixed-mask model. No priority claim is made. For the four-by-four result, see [FourByFour.md](FourByFour.md).
