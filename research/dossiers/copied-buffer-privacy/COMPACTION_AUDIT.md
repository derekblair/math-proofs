# Exact custody audit with heterogeneous multi-epoch copied buffers

Model result for offline defensive audits. No new KEM, deployable compactor, active security or physical erasure claim. The catalogs are explicitly given, hypothetical facts about which logical values a whole-buffer exposure would contain; there is no collection or probing of live state.

## Model and information-theoretic criterion

The same scalar seed s is stored in independent degree-at-most-t Shamir versions, each at n>t distinct nonzero labeled coordinates of an eligible finite field. All nonconstant coefficient vectors across origins are jointly independent and uniformly distributed conditional on s; full independent refresh or resharing is an ideal prerequisite. In every phase e, there is a fixed explicit, secret-independent catalog of exposed compartments. A compartment discloses a set of pairs (origin version, coordinate label). This can represent a late copy, migrated backup or a working buffer containing more than one site’s share for the same origin. Equal pairs are exact duplicates. No additional secret-dependent occupancy, raw refresh messages, deltas, cryptographic keys, operations, transport or arbitrary linear combinations are modeled. Each phase has a compartment exposure budget b_e and there is an optional global budget K. A compartment costs one exposure no matter how many labels it contains. The catalog and eligibility are independent of secret values and given before an exposure. Multiple observations of precisely the same compartment in the same phase add nothing and can be excluded.

For any forced admissible sequence, let U_j be the set of distinct coordinates observed for origin j. The law of all exposed values is independent of the scalar s **iff** every |U_j|≤t. Up to t coordinates from a degree-t Shamir polynomial give a uniform secret-independent vector, and the distributions from different origins are independent conditional on the scalar even if compromised in the same buffer. Repeated copies are deterministic. Conversely t+1 coordinates of one origin determine that polynomial, so that fixed modeled view is not perfectly private. A finite read-only adaptive decision rule cannot change this iff: condition on its policy tape and compare each leaf's probability using the forced sequence labeling that leaf; an incompatible leaf has adaptive probability zero, not necessarily forced probability zero.

Thus exact safety over all admissible sequences equals having maximum coverage of each origin at most t. Each origin can be optimized independently; one does not need a single sequence maximizing all origins simultaneously. This is an extension of the simple stale-retention maximum-window check: origin coordinates now migrate or are copied across arbitrary compartment eligibility sets, rather than each physical site maintaining a fixed-depth array. It is not a proof for correlated low-randomness Shamir versions, where counting labels is only a necessary upper cutoff and cross-origin linear relations may leak below it.

For a fixed origin, the deterministic optimization is exactly **maximum coverage under a truncated partition matroid**: the ground set is phase-tagged compartments, with one partition block and cap b_e per phase; the global rank cap K truncates that matroid, and the objective is the cardinality of the union of selected label sets. [Sellier, ESA 2023](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2023.94) treats the wider matroid-constrained maximum-coverage problem. Our results give its exact interpretation for the stated custody model, plus restricted width/placement cases. They do not constitute a new general coverage problem or algorithm.

## Width one is exactly max-flow auditable

Fix an origin. Suppose each exposable compartment contains at most one distinct label of that origin (it may contain one coordinate of each of many other independent origins). Aggregate all compartments of a phase into the list of attainable labels, ignoring duplicates and empty entries. Max-flow network:

1. A source sends at most K units to an intermediate node (omit that cap when unbounded).
2. That node sends at most b_e units to each phase node.
3. A phase node links to each attainable label with unit capacity.
4. Each label sends at most one unit to the sink.

Integer flows correspond exactly to distinct covered labels: assign a discovered label to any one phase/compartment that revealed it; a distinct label necessarily has a distinct width-one compartment in its assigned phase. Conversely each unit chooses a distinct obtainable label at cost one. Thus the maximum flow equals the exact hazard count for this origin. A min-cut of capacity≤t is a compact checkable certificate that this origin’s entire modeled policy is safe. Physical state segregation to at most one label per origin per read can therefore restore efficient exact audits rather than just reducing a loose union bound.

## Width two gives a sharp audit-complexity jump, even with disjoint buffers per phase

A violation witness is a polynomial-sized list of compartments and an origin, with at least t+1 distinct resulting labels. Hence NONPRIVATE is in NP and universal safety is in coNP for an explicit catalog.

Start from the decision form of [Le–Pfender rainbow matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7253), which is NP-hard even for properly edge-colored bipartite graphs with no induced P₄ and at most two edges of each color (Corollary 2(4)). Given a target matching size k≥1, place graph vertices on distinct nonzero coordinates of a single origin; isolated dummy coordinates can ensure n≥2k, or dismiss trivial k>number of edges instances early. Use degree t=2k−1. Make each edge one width-two compartment containing its endpoints’ coordinate labels; its edge color is the phase in which the compartment is available. Cap every phase at one exposure and the global budget at k. A proper coloring ensures the two-coordinate compartments are disjoint inside any one phase. The cited restricted class gives at most two such compartments per phase. Every pair has one label from each bipartition. Coordinates can be embedded in a field of polynomial description and size (a prime between n and 2n suffices); the audit does not need numerical share examples.

Covering at least t+1=2k labels requires exactly k chosen pairs with no repeated label. Per-phase capacity requires their colors to differ. Thus there is an admissible threshold-violating model view exactly when the graph has a rainbow matching of size k. Together with membership, deciding modeled NONPRIVATE is NP-complete and exact universal privacy certification is coNP-complete at width two. This does not characterize arbitrary deployed cryptography or imply hardness for any fixed constant threshold. The reduction is a catalog-theory equivalence, not a workflow for obtaining real key material. If a cast requires each phase also to have a current origin, append a benign current label for each such origin; at most one exposure in that phase cannot cross t.

With no global cap, general width two still has an elementary hardness reduction from perfect [3-dimensional matching](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf): one phase per third-coordinate element with capacity one gives an implicit total k for balanced instances, but intra-phase disjointness need not hold there. The main proper-coloring result uses an explicit global cap and guarantees disjointness inside each phase.

## Useful exact and tractable alternatives

- **Identical partition across phases:** if every nonempty exposed set of an origin is exactly a block from one fixed disjoint partition, each block can be counted once, with weight equal to its size. Reuse the phase-to-block network with that block-sink capacity one and negative cost equal to its size; send a fixed K units with a zero-cost bypass of capacity K to permit unused count; if no global cap is specified, use K=sum of the phase caps or any equivalent finite bound. Minimum-cost flow gives exact maximum weighted coverage. Thus regrouping labels across time drives the hardness above; the mere presence of pairs does not.
- **Small threshold with arbitrary buffer width:** this is the color-coding approach of [Bläser, *Computing Small Partial Coverings*, 2003](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0020019002004349), adapted to per-phase budgets and the optional total cap by a local/global mask DP. Put p=t+1 and randomly color origin labels with p colors. For a fixed coloring, compute the minimum number of compartments realizing each OR-union color bitmask separately in every phase by a 0/1 mask DP, enforcing its phase cap. Combine phase tables via OR and additive costs, discarding costs greater than K. Full color coverage implies at least p distinct real labels, so there is no false positive. If p labels can be covered, those fixed labels receive all distinct colors with probability p!/p^p≥exp(−p). Repeating O(exp(p) log(1/δ)) independent trials gives one-sided error ≤δ. One trial uses O((B·2^p+E·4^p)) operations and O(2^p) streaming DP words beyond the catalog, for B buffer entries (plus time to form masks). This is a parameterized exact-with-confidence audit, not a deterministic safety certificate when it does not find full coverage. For small fixed universes, use identity labels as colors and inspect the exact coverage masks in the same DP. Alternatively a [Naor–Schulman–Srinivasan perfect hash family, Theorem 3(iii)](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~naor/PAPERS/splitters.pdf) guarantees injective coloring of each p-subset under at least one of exp(p) p^(O(log p)) log n functions, giving a deterministic 2^(O(p))·poly(catalog size) exact audit without relying on a confidence bound. This deterministic family was not implemented in the checks.

## PQ and prior-art boundary

[FIPS 203](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf) allows sensitive storage of the seed pair (d,z) for standard deterministic key expansion, including the rejection seed. The scalar can conceptually encode those bytes as one sufficiently large field element; the theorem says nothing about conversion to the arithmetic field of the KEM, nor threshold decapsulation performance. Under a separately proved ideal evaluator which exposes only authorized standard-game outputs independent of storage masks, a safe catalog has a dummy-seed share simulator and therefore does not worsen the logical leakage term under classical-interface adaptive queries. This conditional argument is the same as for the stale-window model, and does not supply active, coherent-query, whole-memory or forward-secrecy guarantees. A later whole-seed exposure still defeats a persistent standard key.

[NIST IR 8214C](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8214/c/final) asks for explicit active/adaptive/proactive threshold assumptions; [Ta–Liu–Chau–Wang](https://opencryptology.org/2026/2220) study compatible MPC threshold ML-KEM. Those requirements are not met by an at-rest catalog proof. [Proactive Secret Sharing with Constant Communication](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/942) distinguishes seeing both epoch versions during refresh, and [Cojocaru et al.](https://opencryptology.org/2026/1072) study significantly stronger no-erasures models. The candidate contribution is the sharp width-and-repacking complexity boundary for this ideal catalog, not the underlying matching problems, max flow, matroid-constrained maximum coverage, partial-cover color coding, Shamir privacy or the general importance of erasure. A primary-source check found no exact prior result in this model; no priority claim.
