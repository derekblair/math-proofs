# A single large support SCC defeats both static and witness-local grouping

For every k≥3 there is a directed positive-integer k-DSP YES-instance with one support SCC C of Θ(k²) vertices such that every solution must use Θ(k) distinct tight-potential groups inside C, **even if groups are recomputed on the exact local entry–exit-trimmed support of each solution**. The support SCC has a feedback vertex set lower bound Ω(k²). Thus the existing SCC sweep with grouping, actual-route endpoint trimming, or the small-SCC subset fallback does not obtain its linear exponent from these parameters alone. This is a limitation of those bounds, not a complexity lower bound for the instances or for the algorithm.

Let q=k−1, with original labels 1,…,q and one connector label c. For every i give s_i→a_i and b_i→t_i weight one; these are the only arcs leaving s_i and entering t_i, respectively. Have one shared vertex H, and add a_i→H and H→b_i each of weight four, plus a_i→b_i of weight eight. For every i<j add distinct u_ij,v_ij and four unit arcs H→u_ij, u_ij→H, u_ij→v_ij, v_ij→u_ij. Add a_j→v_ij and v_ij→b_i each of weight two. Finally the connector uses s_c→b_i, b_i→a_i, a_i→t_c each of weight one, for every i; it has also the private arc s_c→t_c of weight three. No arc enters any source or leaves any terminal.

**Original shortest paths and support.** In a transit from a_i to the next visited b-vertex, any passage through H has length at least eight: reaching H costs at least four, and reaching a b next from H costs at least four. A transit avoiding H can either use a private weight-eight arc or use the same petal's entry and exit, which has weight four and goes strictly from higher j to lower i. Visiting another a-vertex after a b requires the unit connector reverse arc b_i→a_i. Any route using two or more transits has length at least four plus one plus four, greater than eight. Therefore a_i→b_i distance is eight, and every original s_i→t_i shortest distance is ten. For each pair i<j, legitimate length-eight local routes for labels i and j are, respectively,
  a_i→H→u_ij→v_ij→b_i and a_j→v_ij→u_ij→H→b_j.
Every H spoke and every petal arc therefore belongs to an original shortest path. For c the only possible routes shorter than length three would need s_c→a_i or b_i→t_c, neither of which exists. Thus its direct and up to q bridge routes are shortest, and each b_i→a_i belongs to its shortest support. The own direct a_i→b_i arcs plus connector private s_c→t_c form a disjoint solution.

**One genuine SCC.** The complete internal support on H, the q port pairs a_i,b_i and the 2 binom(q,2) petals is strongly connected: H↔u_ij↔v_ij, and H→b_i→a_i→H. No terminal reaches back and no source can be reached internally. So |C|=1+2q+2 binom(q,2)=Θ(k²). The directed 2-cycles u_ij↔v_ij are mutually vertex-disjoint, giving feedback vertex set size at least binom(q,2), regardless of what is done to the shared hub.

**Endpoint-trimmed groups still unbounded.** Every solution has all original labels admitted to C, and their actual local fragment endpoints are exactly a_i,b_i, because of the degree-one terminal connections. In the local support restricted to arcs lying on some a_i→b_i route, every pair of original labels retains the two displayed shortest routes, regardless of which one was selected by the solution. Hence for every i<j the union of their restricted shortest DAGs contains both directions of u_ij↔v_ij as requested tight constraints. No finite φ can simultaneously satisfy φ(v_ij)−φ(u_ij)=1 and φ(u_ij)−φ(v_ij)=1. Thus pairwise different groups are compulsory among these q labels on *every* solution's transition, so q≤γ≤k. This argument does not require that the chosen connector visit C. It defeats global group compression, SCC-local group compression and witness-local trimmed group compression; it does not preclude eliminating irrelevant alternatives with a different algorithm or direct discovery of the obviously disjoint private routes.

Replay `python check_barrier.py` for exact terminal distances, all claimed supported arcs, SCC equality, endpoint trim preservation, and explicit disjoint witnesses. The higher-to-lower shortcut is permitted and deliberately invalidates global terminal assignment optimality. It never returns to the same label in one transit.

## Separation from the conflict-usage quota

The private solution has **zero** used conflict vertices on its large-SCC transition when a conflict is a vertex present in at least two admitted entry–exit-trimmed supports. Its connector is not admitted to C: entering via any b_j→a_j branch would collide with original commodity j's compulsory port vertices, irrespective of other routing. Every private local route uses only its own a_i,b_i. Those vertices are present in no other admitted support, since an original a_h→b_h shortest path cannot complete two transits, and consequently cannot traverse b_i→a_i or use another label's a or b as an intermediate. Even if quota counted against connector globally, the bound would be only 2q, but that is not needed under the endpoint-trimmed admitted definition.

For arbitrary integer M≥1, use M pairwise vertex-disjoint copies of each petal pair u_ij,v_ij, each connected to the same hub and old ports with the same edge weights. The transit proof does not change: without H a route remains within its single petal until exiting, and with H the same lower bound holds. Each clone is supported, the SCC size is 1+2q+2M binom(q,2), and each copy gives another disjoint 2-cycle. The forced grouping remains at least q while the private solution quota remains zero for all M. This separates the grouping and the conflict-usage parameters even at arbitrarily large n relative to k. The checker includes exact source/target, SCC, group-conflict and quota-zero tests through M=128.
