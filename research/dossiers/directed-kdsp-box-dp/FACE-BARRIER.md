# Quadratic active-face rosters are possible along a legal cut history

For every k ≥ 3, there is a positive-integer directed graph with k pairs whose unique shortest paths are disjoint, and a **legal sequence of longest-side middle-third cuts, each crossing fewer than 9k clipped solution edges**, that reaches a box with at least 2 floor((k−1)/2) ceil((k−1)/2) crossing solution arcs. This rules out an O(k) bound that follows solely from the existing rule's local guarantees, even for actual capped source-distance potentials and a unique solution. It is not an obstruction to an alternative cut-selection rule or to existence of an optimized O(k)-width hierarchy.

Use labels 1,…,k with anchor k; let J comprise a=floor((k−1)/2) labels and I all b remaining nonanchor labels. Make one old chain for each label. In chain i∈I, start with z_j=+1 for j∈J and all other coordinates zero. Visit j∈J in order: append one vertex that lowers only z_j to −1 and raises z_i by 2, then append a vertex returning z_j to +1 and again raising z_i by 2. Keep all other coordinates unchanged at these steps, in particular z_k=0. For the other chains use any two old vertices with all z coordinates zero. On chain i every z_i−z_h is nondecreasing: at each oscillating step z_i goes up by 2 and no other coordinate increases by more than 2. This holds also on the constant chains.

For realization, write q_h(v)=z_h(v)+3r(v), where r is its one-based chain index. Give each chain arc u→v of chain i weight q_i(v)−q_i(u)>0; this dominates q_h(v)−q_h(u) for all h. Add source s_i with outgoing arcs to the first old vertex of its own chain and every old vertex of other chains, of respective weight 1+q_i(v). Add t_i with unique incoming arc from the last old vertex of chain i, of weight T−1−q_i(last), with T greater than 1+max_{h,v} q_h(v). No arcs lead into any source or leave any terminal. The distances from s_i to all old v equal 1+q_i(v): this is direct or by the own chain, and other possible routes cannot lower it by the arc dominance inequality. The only route to t_i from s_i follows the entire own chain and has length T. Hence the actual capped π_i on old vertices equals 1+q_i(v); all differences from π_k are exactly z_h. At the 2k new terminals, capped distances (and thus differences) are integers and each difference is in [−T,T].

Take a geometric root [−M,M]^(k−1) with M=T+1. Split each j∈J in order at level 1/2, following the side z_j≥1/2. It is always a longest unsplit side and 1/2 is in its middle third. Levels avoid all ambient vertices. At cut j, every oscillating old chain contributes exactly two clipped hits: only its excursion at j crosses the plane, and both its endpoints are strictly inside all the earlier positive-side constraints. Constant old chains do not hit. The 2k added own-path endpoint arcs can add at most 2k hits to any cut; hence the required local bound holds. Every split is proper, since it separates each designated negative j-vertex while retaining every point of each oscillating chain's all-positive intersections. At the final box, the 2ab distinct old oscillation arcs each have exactly one endpoint inside. Thus even assigning every arc to just one corresponding active face cannot make the aggregate fewer than 2ab.

Replay `python face_barrier.py` to compute exact source distances and shortest-path multiplicities on the constructed actual directed graphs for 4≤k≤30, then verify clipped charges and final crossing rosters with rational arithmetic. Each graph has Θ(k²) vertices here, so the example does not assert state complexity n^{Ω(k²)}. In fact the solutions here can be found by independent shortest paths. The point is the failure of a universal aggregate estimate for admissible histories.
