# A polynomial parameter exponent for positive-weight directed k-disjoint shortest paths

**Theorem.** In the deterministic unit-cost comparison/addition model of Cao–Qi–Wang, vertex-disjoint directed shortest paths for k prescribed pairs and arbitrary strictly positive real arc weights can be found, or nonexistence certified, in n^{O(k²)} time after reading the input. No planarity, sparsity, or union-DAG condition on the input is assumed.

This answers the polynomial-exponent half of Question 11.1 in [Cao–Qi–Wang, Directed k-Disjoint Shortest Paths in Polynomial Time, arXiv:2611.34013v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34013v1#S11). It does **not** establish the n^{O(k)} target, give a polynomial bound on the source's sweep width (Question 11.2), or contradict the ETH lower bound n^{Ω(k)}. Compared with an earlier local balanced-compression analysis n^{O(k⁵4^k)}, this is a different exact algorithm, not a smaller memory estimate for that sweep.

The key difference is that geometric children are disjoint sets of **ambient graph vertices**. The DP selects a bounded roster of actual arcs that cross their boundaries. It does not store exposure witnesses from parent regions. To bound arc rosters across arbitrary recursion depth, each selected geometric cut is charged against **all full original solution segments clipped to its parent geometry, even segments whose endpoints are outside that parent**. This makes the charge valid for any descendant that inherits the corresponding face.

## 1. Potentials and a clipped cut bound

Discard heavier parallel arcs. If terminals of different pairs coincide, reject. A vertex with s_i=t_i forces its trivial path; it can be reserved without recomputing any other pair's shortest distances. Equivalently keep it as both terminal types in the local DP below. Reject any pair with no directed route. Compute exact shortest-path DAGs D_i and the finite capped potentials
```
π_i(v)=min(dist(s_i,v),dist(s_i,t_i)).
```
As in source Lemmas 3.1–3.2, π_i is tight on D_i and no other potential increases by more than the arc weight. Consequently on each D_i arc every own difference π_i−π_h is nondecreasing. The distance is finite at all vertices after capping, even if a vertex is unreachable from s_i.

Write z_h=π_h−π_k for h=1,…,d=k−1 and set z_k=0 by convention. Use the resulting z(v)∈R^d as the position of v. Unless k=1 (already shortest-path search), take an initial closed axis-aligned box with every ambient position strictly interior and all side widths positive. Realize every arc of a fixed solution P_i as the straight segment between its endpoint positions, including segments of zero length. This is solely an existence proof; the algorithm does not know or construct the solution.

Let B be any produced closed box, W>0 its largest width, and j a coordinate of width W. We seek a plane z_j=c with c in the middle third of this side. We count every original arc-segment whose intersection with the plane lies inside B. This equals the number of segments whose clipped z_j image contains c. Ignore the finitely many levels of ambient vertex coordinates, including the levels of all constant-z_j segments.

For commodity i, put u=π_i−π_k=z_i and v=π_i−π_j=z_i−z_j. Along its full piecewise-linear curve both u and v are nondecreasing because each D_i arc has all own differences nondecreasing and linear interpolation preserves this. Clipping each original segment to the convex box B gives at most one closed subsegment, possibly empty or a point. Since z_j=u−v, the length of its projection onto coordinate j is at most its u-increase plus its v-increase. The clipped pieces on any one path are still ordered and disjoint as parameter intervals except at endpoints. Therefore their u-increases sum to at most the total u-span on B, at most W; their v-increases sum to at most the v-span on B, at most 2W. This bound concerns the **entire** curve, not only edges with an endpoint in B.

Split the middle third into consecutive open intervals at every clipped segment projection endpoint and at all ambient vertex coordinate values inside it. The number of plane intersections is constant on each resulting open interval. The sum of that number times each interval length is at most 3kW: each clipped projection contributes at most its length. Since the interval lengths add to W/3, one open interval has count at most 9k. Choose a level in its interior. This crosses at most 9k full original solution segments **inside B** and avoids every ambient vertex-coordinate value. A segment clipped to a single point affects only a breakpoint and is harmless.

## 2. Bounded global boundary at every node

Recursively split every nonempty box whose ambient vertices have more than one position, choosing a cut on a longest side as above with respect to the same fixed full solution. Work with the two closed children, which share a face but have disjoint ambient vertex sets because cut levels avoid all coordinates. Each child retains side widths and shortens the split side to at most 2W/3. After any d successive splits along one branch, the maximum width is at most 2/3 of its initial value along that block: while a side is still greater than that threshold it must be chosen before all below-threshold sides, and a chosen side becomes below threshold. Thus eventually a box contains only one distinct ambient position, or is empty. This follows from the positive minimum sup-norm gap of finitely many distinct positions (and requires no bound on real bit size).

Consider any descendant B and an original solution arc with exactly one endpoint in B. Its straight segment meets the boundary of B. A boundary intersection lies on a current face of B, and no arc can cross an initial outer face since both endpoints lie strictly in the root box. So some current face hit by the segment originated at an ancestor's chosen cut. The intersection point lies in that ancestor parent box because the entire current closed box is contained in it. The original arc was therefore included among the at most 9k curve segments counted at that ancestor cut, whether or not its endpoints were both in that ancestor. This argument works at intersections of several faces, and all actual arc endpoints avoid cuts. Every axis-aligned box has at most 2d current faces, regardless of how many cuts preceded it. It follows that every produced box has at most
```
C=18k(k−1)
```
arcs of the solution with exactly one endpoint in it.

Remove empty boxes from the finite geometry tree and contract a unary split with the same nonempty ambient vertex set as its only nonempty child. This changes no vertex sets, crossing rosters, or leaf position property that survive in the resulting tree. For existence, all its retained boundaries still have the bound by referring back to the original geometric boxes. Thus a tree of proper binary ambient subdivisions of size O(n), with single-position leaves, works for some fixed solution whenever one exists. Neither the chosen arbitrary real cut levels nor the contracted geometry depth is part of the algorithm.

## 3. Dynamic program over ambient box vertex sets

Only n^{2d+O(1)} different ambient vertex sets are obtainable as positions in axis-aligned boxes: along each coordinate a level interval is determined by an interval of sorted coordinate ranks, including tied groups. Represent and deduplicate sets, so there is no numerical scale dependence. Enumerate their possible binary splits by a coordinate threshold strictly between consecutive distinct coordinate values among the current vertices. A child's ambient set is again a box set. All the proper subdivisions in §2 are candidates, because their thresholds induce such rank splits. Sort states by number of ambient vertices. Use the complete vertex set as the root.

For each nonempty box vertex set U, enumerate rosters R of ≤C arcs with exactly one endpoint in U, each labeled by its commodity i and belonging to D_i. Outside endpoints and arc IDs are part of the roster. Reject contradictory duplicated labels on the same arc if represented. The state asks for the existence of internal labeled arcs within U such that:

* every used vertex belongs to a single commodity (terminals of any commodity are always used for that commodity);
* for each commodity, every used ordinary vertex has exactly one incoming and one outgoing incident arc of that commodity, counting all its prescribed crossing arcs and chosen internal arcs;
* s_i has zero incoming and one outgoing, t_i one incoming and zero outgoing; a coincident s_i=t_i is forced used with no incident arcs;
* unused vertices have no selected incident arcs.

Each internal arc of commodity i must lie in D_i. Selected boundary arcs impose degrees only on their inside endpoint. Internal cycles are impossible for each commodity (all D_i are acyclic), so all internal components are directed path fragments; no information about which fragment starts match their fragment ends needs storing in a DP state.

At a proper split U=L⊔R, enumerate a set Q of ≤C labeled arcs between L and R. The parent roster is split between the children by its unique inside endpoint, and both children receive Q as part of their boundary roster. Discard if either child roster exceeds C. If both child states accept, unite their local witnesses and add the interchild arcs Q once. Vertex sets are disjoint and both children prescribe exactly the same interchild arcs, so all inside degrees and commodity assignments hold. Conversely, restricting any witness for a fixed binary box partition with bounded boundary on all nodes gives the two child witnesses and that Q. Its size≤C because every interchild arc belongs to **each** child boundary. Try all proper cuts if the box has multiple positions. Record one successful split and respective child rosters to reconstruct paths.

At a single-position leaf, all arcs of the induced union of D_i go strictly forward in π_1: their endpoints have all pair differences equal, so every potential increases by the common positive weight (source §3.2). Thus they share a topological order. Incoming crossing arcs and internal sources are the starts of fragments; outgoing crossing arcs and internal sinks are the ends. Require each vertex's prescribed stub counts fit the degree/commodity constraints. If a vertex is both a start and an end of the same commodity, its fragment is forced to be the one-vertex path, so reserve it and remove those two stubs. If a vertex is a terminal of another commodity, an incompatible stub rejects immediately. This includes s_i=t_i. Check equal numbers of remaining starts and ends per commodity and guess a bijection between them. There are at most k+C fragments, since each start comes from either a terminal or one crossing arc. With the bijection, solve prescribed-terminal vertex-disjoint paths in the common DAG, restricting each fragment to D_i inside the leaf. The ordinary earliest-token DP applies also to per-fragment arc sets: keep one current vertex per unfinished fragment, advance the one earliest in the common topological order, and forbid collisions with current vertices or any other fragment's fixed endpoints. A forgotten interior vertex lies before every later token position. Completed endpoints remain forbidden to other fragments. This yields the desired iff; complexity at most n^{O(k+C)} for each guessed bijection, with at most (k+C)! guesses (or cap by n fragment starts after checking distinctness).

At the full-vertex root state demand an empty boundary. The global witness meets all degree/exclusivity conditions, so for each commodity with distinct terminals the arcs give precisely the desired s_i–t_i path and nothing else: all other components would have both directions everywhere and hence a cycle, impossible in D_i. Coincident source/sink contributes the trivial path. Conversely if a true solution exists, the construction in §2 gives a valid bounded-roster hierarchy. Its bottom-up states are all accepted, including the root.

## 4. Running time and arithmetic

Count labeled arcs in O(k n²) after parallel normalization, and enumerate at most its O(C)-th power for rosters or merge choices. Number of box vertex sets is n^{O(k)}, and O(kn) coordinate cuts are enough per box. Leaf work is n^{O(k+C)} including the finite port matching choices, since k≤n after repeated-terminal rejection and surviving fragment starts are distinct. If desired cap C by the number of arcs in a candidate actual solution, ≤n, without compromising completeness; large-k functions are absorbed in n^{O(k²)}. Standard Dijkstra and edge tightness tests are polynomial and never dominate this use of C. All implemented real operations are shortest-path additions/comparisons and sorting of coordinate differences (subtraction is addition with a sign; comparisons of differences can alternatively be made by cross-adding their original potentials). No real division, floor, geometric transcendental, or coordinate magnitude bound is needed. The mathematical averaging argument is not an algorithmic arithmetic requirement.

Independently checked finite tests are provided separately; they are not a substitute for the general argument. The accompanying Lean file verifies the algebraic projection bound and common progress at tied positions. The full separation and DP correctness proofs above have not been formalized in Lean.
