# Compressing the parameter exponent by tight-potential groups

**Theorem.** Let D_i contain precisely the arcs on some shortest path for terminal pair (s_i,t_i) of a directed positive-real graph. Set U=∪D_i, retaining ambient vertices. Define g as the minimum number of groups partitioning the k labels such that each group has one potential feasible on all of U and tight on every arc of that group's D_i. Deterministic exact directed k-DSP admits n^{O(kg)} time on the original addition/comparison model. No partition or certificate need be given. For fixed g this is n^{O(k)} despite possibly cyclic U. This does not prove the worst-case linear exponent in [Cao–Qi–Wang Question 11.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34013v1): g can equal k on a feasible instance.

## Exact grouping characterization

Reject any infinite requested distance first. If terminals of different pairs coincide reject. Trivial pairs may either be kept as singletons at their terminals in the box DP, or reserve their vertices while retaining all other original shortest-distance constraints. They introduce no D_i arcs and no difficulty below.

Fix a group I and write d_ij=dist_U(s_i,t_j); each d_ii equals original requested finite shortest distance. Unreachable cross distances are infinity. The following are equivalent:

1. Some finite φ obeys φ(v)−φ(u)≤w(uv) for all uv∈U and equality on every D_i, i∈I.
2. Some such feasible φ is tight along one actual shortest path for each i∈I.
3. The prescribed identity pairing minimizes sum of terminal distances within I among all permutations σ (an infinite sum never improves the identity).
4. The auxiliary label digraph with finite arcs i→j of weight d_ij−d_jj has no negative directed cycle.
5. Add to U a possibly parallel reversed terminal arc t_i→s_i of weight −d_ii for each i∈I; the resulting finite weighted digraph has no negative cycle.

(2) implies (1): telescoping gives φ(t_i)−φ(s_i)=d_ii, and on ANY shortest path relative to U the total of the nonnegative edge slacks is d_ii−(φ(t_i)−φ(s_i))=0. (1) implies (2) directly. (1) and (5) are equivalent because feasibility on the reversed terminal arc is precisely φ(t_i)−φ(s_i)≥d_ii, while feasibility along an original shortest path gives the reverse inequality. Difference-constraint feasibility and finite values follow by adding a fresh zero-weight supersource with a direct arc into each vertex and computing Bellman–Ford distances. No infinity or substitution constant is used in the returned potentials.

(3) and (4) are equivalent by permutation-cycle decomposition and extending any simple negative cycle by identity fixed points. (4) and (5) are equivalent because a negative label cycle lifts to a negative closed walk by following its finite U-shortest routes interleaved with reversed terminal arcs. Conversely, any negative augmented cycle uses at least one reverse arc (U has positive weights); break at consecutive reversed arcs into U-walks s_i→t_j, replacing their weights with d_ij as lower bounds. Its negative sum implies a negative label closed walk and thus a negative label cycle. Length-zero walks and zero-weight reversed arcs for trivial pairs need no exception. The reverse arcs are only auxiliary constraints and must not be introduced in the routing DP.

Compute cross distances once, or verify each subset directly on the augmented original support. Then solve minimum group partition by subset DP over admissible groups in O(3^k) subset choices. For an optimal partition get each finite feasible tight φ by one Bellman–Ford run. Trimming unrelated arcs first is safe, retains original shortest paths in both directions, and can improve g. If deleting h labels leaves a minimum-sum identity on U, then g≤h+1 by one group and h singleton groups; no witness h needs to be given.

## Algorithm

Use the g returned potentials in the box-DP argument instead of k individual shortest-distance potentials. Every required path still has its own geometric potential, now φ_G shared by all commodities in its group. On its arcs this potential increases by exactly the positive weight while any other group potential increases by at most the weight. Therefore all its own differences are nondecreasing. Define ambient coordinates z_H=φ_H−φ_anchor with g−1 coordinates. For any clipped solution edge on a path whose group is G, the projection onto split coordinate j is the difference of the two individually nondecreasing functions φ_G−φ_anchor and φ_G−φ_j. Their ranges inside a longest-width W box are at most W and 2W. Averaging the total clipped projections over the middle third still charges at most 9k original solution edges per chosen face, as there are k actual paths, not g.

The ambient box rank representation has n^{O(g)} candidate sets and each box inherits at most 2(g−1) active faces. Thus its unknown actual roster is bounded by 18k(g−1). Exactly the same labeled-arc degree and disjointness DP as in BOX-DP.md serves: no boundary pairing is required at merges, and an equal-position leaf has common strictly increasing φ_anchor on the induced union of all D_i, so the standard fragment-token DP costs n^{O(k+kg)}. For g=1 the entire support is already such a DAG and use the same token algorithm directly. Interchild arcs and labels are retained unchanged; common feasibility is only a coordinate tool, never a relaxation to an unrestricted group route. Partition preprocessing O(3^k poly(n)) is absorbed since k≤n after terminal checks. For a strictly addition/comparison interface represent x−y by (x,y): add componentwise and compare by cross-addition. No division or finite-bit weight assumption is needed. Allow an additive O(m) pass for explicit parallel-arc input and use a minimum-weight representative per ordered pair.

## Limits and non-DAG examples

For any simple graph H on k labels construct private bidirected gadgets for each edge {i,j}: x↔y, s_i→x, y→t_i, s_j→y, x→t_j, all arc weights 1. Also give every i a private direct arc s_i→t_i of weight 3. Sources have no incoming arcs and sinks no outgoing arcs. Each pair's own shortest distance is 3. Every H-edge yields two length-2 cross routes and no other cross pairs are reachable. The arcs of every gadget lie in D_i∪D_j. Both directions x↔y prevent any feasible potential tight on both. An independent set is admissible by the distance characterization, so g=χ(H) even after safety trimming, and the direct arcs always supply a disjoint solution. Thus g=k for H=K_k, and g=2 for a nonempty bipartite H although U contains cyclic SCCs. A rank-based improvement for g constant is an actual algorithm theorem, not a worst-case advance to Q11.1.

A next target is safe lower-dimensional compression without insisting that a single coordinate be tight on *all* shortest-path alternatives: this grouping parameter can be quadratic-regime even when discarding unused alternatives would trivially share one potential. Optimizing over the chosen paths themselves is circular unless accompanied by a certified preservation/elimination rule.
