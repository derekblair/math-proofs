# Generic signed step quartets for two six-orbit Sheehan families

## Statement and explicit finite grids

Let p range over odd primes. There are sets E_p ⊆ (F_p^*)⁴, with |E_p| = O_η(p^(7/2+η)) for every fixed η>0, satisfying the following. For every (a,b,d,e) outside E_p, **every** admissible choice of the base gains and every nonzero red closing voltage in either of the two specified graph families has a Hamilton cycle distinct from its red Hamilton cycle.

The graph has vertex set {0,1,2,3,4,5}×F_p. For each sheet, red edges follow 01,12,23,34,45 without shift; red 5→0 advances by a given nonzero voltage λ. These form a 6p-cycle. The blue quotient is either (Z2) rims 0,4 with doubled pairs 13,25, or (Z6) rims 0,3 with doubled pairs 15,24. A rim of step a or b connects every sheet j to j+a or j+b respectively within that base orbit; the undirected edges allow either sign. Each doubled pair carries two distinct translations, so the physical graph is simple and 4-regular. Interpret d,e as the gain differences of the two doubled pairs oriented along the following chain, not necessarily in increasing base order; its existence depends only on their signs, and either sign can be chosen.

Keep blue and the red perfect matching 01,23,45. In Z2 the resulting components arranged by these links are rim0→digon(1,3)→digon(2,5)→rim4; in Z6, rim0→digon(1,5)→digon(4,2)→rim3. Each rim is one p-cycle because its step is nonzero; each digon is one 2p-cycle because the two gains differ. The chains have identical sheet combinatorics. This theorem is about the marked step grid of these two families only, not all six-orbit graphs, and not the full Sheehan conjecture.

## Carrier surgery and finite cap table

Take a set S of sheets such that the edges to be deleted in each rim provide a perfect matching on S. In each intervening digon choose one of its gain types, delete exactly those translated edges for which the left sheet is in S, and absorb its gain by translating all later sheet origins. This makes the three retained interpiece matching bridges identity and the deleted gains zero. After deletion the digon decomposes into through paths covering all vertices: from a deleted left port s, the surviving edge advances by the other nonzero step, and one keeps alternating with zero edges until back at S. Thus the induced sheet map is the cyclic successor in S along d or e, or its inverse if the other deleted gain type is chosen. All offsets have been absorbed consistently along the chain, and λ is never used by this red matching. In each rim, the retained paths join the head of each cut edge to the tail of the next cut, following its circular order, and exhaust the rim. After transport, their two perfect matchings on sheet endpoints must form one alternating circuit. If so, the literal path expansion is a Hamilton cycle using blue edges and distinct from red.

When a≠±b, the four initial labels L=(0,a,b,a+b) are distinct. Their rim cap matching is the same diagonal in both rims. For a step g, choose positive signed distances of a/g and b/g by reanchoring the parallelogram; after normalizing in the unit circle, its circular order is 0, min, max, sum with sum<1. Its opposite pairing is the side matching of the larger distance; it is never the diagonal. The four-cycle of sheet order induces on the three matchings a transposition fixing this opposite pairing. If d and e assign opposite rank to a and b, the two transpositions differ, so their product changes the diagonal and two unequal cap matchings give one circuit.

If the ranks are equal, use eight sheets S=L ∪ (L+h), indexed 0,1,2,3 and 4,5,6,7. The digon 4-orders are either both in A={0123,0321} or both in B={0213,0312}, because there are only four four-orders with nondiagonal opposite matching. For group A choose normalized h/a near 1/2 and h/b,h/d,h/e positive near 0. For group B put h/b near 1/2 and the other three near 0. Choose the sign of the other rim so that the two original cut tails in the blocked rim are at 0 and a residue in (0,1/2); this only reanchors and relabels L, preserves the two side rank classes, and preserves all clearances below. In each near-zero order, shift h is short and positive, so each original label is followed by its shifted twin. In the blocked rim, both original tails precede both shifted tails. The a-rim cuts have tails 0,2,4,6 (head index +1), and the b-rim cuts have tails 0,1,4,5 (head +2). The cap arrays, indexed 0..7, are:
A-block aCap=[7,2,1,4,3,6,5,0], bCap=[7,6,4,5,2,3,1,0];
B-block aCap=[7,4,5,6,1,2,3,0], bCap=[7,2,1,4,3,6,5,0].
Use the inverse successor in first digon; use inverse in the second iff the two underlying 4-orders agree, natural otherwise. For each of the eight pairs, the composition of aCap and transported bCap has orbit of length four, so the alternating sheet graph is one 8-cycle. The exact table is kernel-checked in sheehan-z6-two-box-table.lean and independently checked by the literal graph replayer cited below.

## Fourier moment and the shift test

Write br(x) for the field representative in [0,p−1] divided by p. Fix 0<ε<1/32. Require br(a/b),br(b/a) at distance >4ε from 0 modulo 1 and from 1/2, and in each role g=d,e require pairwise circular separation >4ε of the four normalized labels. The latter is exactly requiring distances of a/g,b/g,(a+b)/g,(a−b)/g from 0 modulo 1 to be >4ε. The target box in the four normalized h/step coordinates has radius ε around 1/2 in the blocked role and around 2ε in the three tiny roles. Hence all strict interleaving/block orders asserted above hold, and the d role alone ensures the eight actual sheets are distinct.

Put K=ceil(1/ε), L=2K−1. Suppose no nonzero integer vector k of coordinate sizes ≤L satisfies k₁/a+k₂/b+k₃/d+k₄/e=0 mod p. To locate h, set g(t)=[sin(πKt)/(K sin πt)]⁴, continuously at integers. It is the square of f with triangular Fourier coefficients c_j=(K−|j|)_+/K². Its degree is 2(K−1). The mean I₀=(2K²+1)/(3K³) follows by summing c_j². The first-coefficient gap ĝ(0)−ĝ(1)=(1/2)Σ(c_j−c_{j−1})²=1/K³; thus I₂=∫sin²(πt)g(t)=1/(2K³), and I₂/I₀=3/[2(2K²+1)]. If c_i are the four box centers, the polynomial
  F(u)=[sin²(πε)−Σ_i sin²(π(u_i−c_i))] ∏_i g(u_i−c_i)
is nonpositive outside the strict box. Its torus mean is I₀⁴(sin²(πε)−4I₂/I₀)>0, since sin²(πε)≥4ε² and 4I₂/I₀<3/K²≤3ε². Every coordinate degree is ≤L. The assumed absence of a short relation and geometric-series character orthogonality give the same positive discrete mean over h∈F_p. At least one h lies in the required strict box.

## Anisotropic estimate for the exceptional set

Give each coordinate its own clearance δ_i, capped at 1/32: for each rim coordinate the clearance is its normalized two-tail distance to 0 or half; for each digon coordinate it is the smallest of the four normalized pairwise differences in its own role, to the nearest integer. Choose box radii ε_i=δ_i/5 with exactly the same centers (1/2 for the blocked rim, 2ε_i elsewhere). Orienting a rim step still preserves each clearance and the availability tests, since signed coordinates and signs of frequencies can be reversed. The local construction works unchanged. In place of the isotropic polynomial use
  F(u)=[1−Σ_i sin²(π(u_i−c_i))/sin²(πε_i)] ∏_i g_{K_i}(u_i−c_i), K_i=ceil(1/ε_i).
It is nonpositive outside the strict box. The normalized moment in each coordinate is I₂,i/[I₀,i sin²(πε_i)] ≤ 3/16, from the exact moment formula above, sin²(πε_i)≥4ε_i², and K_i≥1/ε_i. Their sum is at most three quarters, so the torus mean is positive. If there are no nonzero short relations with |k_i|≤L_i=2K_i−1, the discrete mean is again the same, giving h. Thus any excluded quartet has a short relation with |k_i|=O(1/δ_i), unless its clearances were set aside.

To count such failures normalize by a: t=a/b, X=a/d, Y=a/e and retain a itself as an independent nonzero scale. The relation is k_a+t k_b+k_d X+k_e Y=0. Fix t≠±1. The two rim clearances depend only on t and its inverse; write R for their inverse product. If a given digon's clearance lies in one dyadic band u<δ≤2u in the uncapped range, its normalized step variable (X or Y) takes O(pu+1) possible values, since the four relevant gaps concern X, X/t, (1+1/t)X, and (1−1/t)X, all nonzero multiples with t≠±1. The top capped band can be counted as O(pu) with its fixed absolute u. Exclude all profiles with any clearance ≤C/p for a sufficiently large absolute C, e.g. 100. Each such rim condition leaves only O(1) possibilities for t (or t⁻¹), and each such digon condition leaves O(1) possibilities for its normalized step for each t. Including the independent scale and other coordinates, this costs O(p³). On every remaining band, L_i=2⌈5/δ_i⌉−1≤10/δ_i+1<p for sufficiently large p, and O(pu+1)=O(pu), so a nonzero integer coefficient used in an equation is nonzero modulo p.

Count for each fixed t before multiplying by the scale p−1:

1. If both digon coefficients are nonzero, and their bands are u,v, there are O(R/(uv)) possible relations in the bounded coefficient boxes. Each relation fixes one variable uniquely given the other, so contributes at most O(p min(u,v)) pairs. The union count in this band is at most min(O(p²uv), O(pR/max(u,v))). Sum over u≤v and then double. Put δ=(R/p)^(1/3). For v≤δ sum the first (geometric) bound to obtain O(p²δ²). For v>δ, split the inner sum at u*=R/(p v²)=δ³/v². The small-u terms form a geometric series O(pR/v), and the remaining O(1+log(v/δ)) terms each have that bound. Summing across dyadic v>δ still gives O(pR/δ). The total per t is O(p^(4/3)R^(2/3)). If a transition lies outside the allowed bands, the same upper bound applies by summing an enlarged band family or comparing to total p².
2. If just one digon coefficient is nonzero, on its band u there are O(R/u) relations, each fixes its normalized variable, with at most p choices for the other. Thus sum min(O(p²u),O(pR/u)) over dyadic u to get O(p^(3/2)√R) by the geometric split at √(R/p). This count needs no band partition for the free variable.
3. If both digon coefficients are zero, the two rim coefficients are nonzero and determine t by their ratio. For R<√p their integer product has magnitude at most O(√p), giving O(√p log p) signed coefficient pairs by the elementary divisor/harmonic sum. For R≥√p use the tail bound below for t instead. Each such t contributes at most p² normalized digon pairs.

To bound R across t, let U,V be the centered integer representatives of 2t,2/t. The normalized clearance of t from 0 or half is |U|/(2p); likewise inverse t has clearance |V|/(2p). Since capping the maximum clearance 1/4 at 1/32 changes it by an absolute factor, R is comparable to p²/|UV|. Also UV≡4 mod p. The O(1) possibilities with integer UV=4 cost O(p³) full marked quartets and can be set aside; otherwise |UV|≥p−4 and R=O(p). For all M≥0, the number of remaining t with |UV|≤M is O_η((1+M/p)p^η): there are O(1+M/p) integers 4+kp in the range, and each has O_η(p^η) signed divisor pairs (each U then determines t). The uniform divisor bound follows from τ(m)=O_ν(m^ν) for any ν>0: for large primes ℓ, r+1≤2^r≤ℓ^(νr), and the finitely many remaining prime factors can each be bounded by a fixed constant times ℓ^(νr). Use ν=η/2 because |UV|=O(p²). Hence the tail #{t:R≥r} is O_η(p^(1+η)/r) for 1≤r≤O(p), implying Σ_t R^α=O_{η,α}(p^(1+η)) for fixed α<1 by dyadic summation. It also gives O_η(p^(1/2+η)) t with R≥√p.

Multiplying the counts by the free scale choices, case 1 contributes O_η(p^(10/3+η)), case 2 O_η(p^(7/2+η)), and case 3 O_η(p^(7/2+η)), where the logarithm at power 1/2 is absorbed in p^η. The separately removed O(p³) profiles are smaller. This proves the main rate for a common exceptional set independent of sheet offsets; the cutoff, short coefficient boxes, and excluded UV=4 profiles themselves do not depend on η, which only enters the counting constants. No Weil bounds are used.

- sheehan-six-orbit-blue-density-replay.js explicitly builds all 6p vertices and resulting edges and checks degree two and connectivity, 75 prime-1009 examples for each of eight port order classes, independently on both base types (1,200 literal graph replays). - SheehanBlueDensityMoment.lean checks rational moments and their normalization, the anisotropic moment margin (anisotropic_ratio_le and anisotropic_four_positive), the positive finite-sum witness, and complex alias-free sample identity on pinned mathlib and Lean 4.34.1. It does not formally link the full polynomial and cap graph construction or prove the asymptotic count.
Run the two JavaScript files directly with Node.js. Compile the two standalone Lean files with `lake env lean -s 65536 /absolute/path/to/file.lean` under Lean 4.34.1 and Mathlib commit `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`. The complete theorem and exceptional count are written proofs.

## Context

[Wagner's thesis, §4.7](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/OOU/TC-OOU-30290.pdf) proposes the full six-orbit strengthening. The marked partial result here does not prove it. [Šajna and Wagner](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.21838) prove a second-cycle result under a sufficiently large automorphism-group hypothesis; the single cyclic action here does not guarantee that hypothesis. [Liu, Liu, and Ning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06985) prove the second-cycle theorem in degree at least 17, rather than the quartic class considered here. No novelty or priority claim is made.
