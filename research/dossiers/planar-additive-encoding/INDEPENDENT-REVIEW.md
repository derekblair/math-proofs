# Independent review: additive collinearity labels

**Green with scope distinctions.** The two-matching criterion, 6/9/10 thresholds, and rational realization check as paper arguments. The named Lean handles for the finite witnesses, local torsion/residue arguments, determinant chart and colored coordinate gadget use standard axioms only. The general criterion/threshold deduction/grid dominance are not currently a single formal theorem. The independent matching replay passed all 10,620 labeled inputs and the explicit 10-point contrast.

- In a disjoint no-four colored instance, at fixed color-A anchor each B/C vertex can have at most one neighbor: two would give anchor + vertex + two neighbors on a line. An edge cannot receive both colors, which would give both anchors + its endpoints on a line. Thus the graph really is a pair of edge-disjoint matchings. Normalize desired B+C sums to 0,t; any actual edge forces t≠0 because its other color is forbidden. With cycle half-length k, order(t) divides k. If its order m is smaller, a proper length-(2m−1) segment forces the other color between nonadjacent endpoints. The same reasoning on a path gives the 2m−2 cap; if a segment starts in the wrong color, swap colors/directions. On a path within cap the representative d=r−s belongs to [2−m,m−1], so residue 0/1 occurs at d=0/1 only. A Z component tag separates disconnected components with no added equalities.
- With at most eight total points, a part has size≤2. A two-point part permits up to seven other vertices even in the more restrictive threshold statement: after a C4, a leftover path has at most two edges; after a C6, at most one vertex remains; two cycles cannot fit. The nine-point trade has five true triples and no shared 2-point subset between any of them, so no four points can be collinear, and the trade forces a false rainbow triple in any abelian group. The ten-point (2,4,4) C4+P4 obstruction is exact. For torsion-free targets ≤5 forces an empty/singleton color; the exact six-point obstruction is equivalent to nonzero 2-torsion in the chosen target group. These numbers concern exact tripartite per-point label representations, not complexity lower bounds for planar decision algorithms.
- Rational realization: assign x to each first-matching singleton/edge component and y to each second-matching component; cells cannot repeat because that would put one edge in both matchings. Each row or column has ≤2 vertices. Otherwise the largest-power-of-M y term dominates the two lower powers in a triple determinant, since the coefficient is a nonzero integer and all coefficient magnitudes are ≤R−1 with M>2R. The projective chart preserves triple incidence; on the positive grid its denominator is nonzero and inverse is given by division by 1−x'−y'. Coordinate equality is detected by the corresponding anchor (0,1) or (1,0); x'+y'<1 rules out a triple with both anchors. A global common denominator preserves incidence and distinctness. Thus arbitrary pairs of disjoint matchings, not just the checked witnesses, are realized by integer points as a written proof.
- The OV transfer is **colored coordinatewise** collinearity: A and C positions must encode the same vector value (not necessarily the same input index), and B comes from the other list. Each coordinate chooses its own line interpolation parameter. Even two all-zero repeated bits with B bits 0 and 1 show parameters −1 and 2, which fail the single scalar required in R^(2d). This says nothing directly about uncolored, actual planar, or conventional higher-dimensional collinearity. State the conditional hardness under the matching version of OVH and logarithmic dimension, not as an unconditional runtime bound.
