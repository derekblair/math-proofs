# Formal route for marked cut compactness without measurable relabeling

Target is compactness of finite triangle-free step kernels with one marked vertex subset, under the cut distance after common realization. No claim of Lean compactness yet.

1. Work with finite labeled measurable partitions. Approximate W by its block mean. Orthogonality of the residual to functions constant on rectangles follows directly by integrating each cell, including zero-measure cells. This gives the exact energy increase ||W_Q||²−||W_P||²=||W_Q−W_P||² on refinement. If a rectangle witnesses cut error >τ, refine each cell by its intersection with each side (factor ≤4). The witness is in the new block subspace, so Cauchy and probability mass imply an energy increment >τ². The energy lies in [0,1]. Thus a bounded number of refinements suffices, uniformly preserving the marked initial partition.
2. For each kernel build a nested sequence of approximations, with τ_k→0 and universal finite branching capacities, allowing empty cells. Extract a subsequence on which masses of every nested cell and all joint block integrals converge. This is a diagonal extraction through countably many [0,1] coordinates, not a simultaneous search over kernels. Construct a nested partition of [0,1) by intervals of the limit cell lengths. The nested step means are consistent by additivity in the numerical limits. Their energies are increasing bounded; orthogonality for refinements makes them L² Cauchy, giving a bounded measurable symmetric representative (take a rapidly convergent subsequence for pointwise a.e. convergence, then clamp and symmetrize). The L² norm dominates cut norm, so the limit is cut-close to the step means at large levels.
3. Use finite matched-block couplings, not isomorphisms between arbitrary measurable subsets. At a fixed level, pair the overlapping mass min(original cell, limit cell) by independent restricted product measures at each label. Couple leftovers arbitrarily by one product. This is measurable on the product with exact marginals; unmatched mass is half the ℓ¹ distance of the two finite class-mass vectors. Pull back both kernels along the projections onto this common probability space. On the matched cells the finite block densities converge (ignore zero-limit cell masses). Pullback does not enlarge a cut error: test each measurable rectangle in the coupling space by the Radon–Nikodym derivatives of the corresponding pushforwards, which are [0,1] fractional test functions against the original marginals. A bound is already Lean checked in CutEnergy.cut_product_test. Then diagonalize the levels or choose k(i) slowly enough to get cut alignment, with marked A_i/A disagreement bounded by unmatched mass.
4. Establish invariance under a measure-preserving projection for t3 and beta, and enough transfer for alpha side caps. t3 follows by measure-preserving product pushforward. Express energy of arbitrary fractional weights as the kernel integral against products of submeasures (Lean ReweightedEnergy). Pushforward an arbitrary half submeasure, using domination by base measure, and identify a fractional half via its Radon–Nikodym derivative clipped into [0,1]; pull back original half functions to show the reverse beta inequality. The finite side cap in the proof only uses the finite graph twin cells on the actual coupling space, so still valid. The final edit assignment uses overlap of finite twin cells with the limit binary template cells, also unchanged.
5. Treat all integration domains as finite real measure spaces until the construction of the limit interval. Keep separate test functions/cut bound, compactness construction, and graph combinatorics. No need to formalize full general colored graphon quotient as a prerequisite.

Primary external statements for comparison only: Lovász–Szegedy limits of dense graph sequences, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408173; Grébík–Pikhurko finite-colored cut compactness https://pikhurko.github.io/E/GrebikPikhurko26ejp.pdf. Explicit coupling approach should avoid invoking an unformalized result as an axiom.
