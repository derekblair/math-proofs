# A saturated-neighborhood route to Erdős #128
Working theorem, not a claimed solution of Erdős #128.

**Statement (paper proof).** In a finite weighted triangle-free graph of total mass one, suppose a maximum independent set A has mass a in [0.38, 0.4] and every positive-weight vertex outside A has neighborhood mass in A at least a/2. Then the graph has a fractional half of edge mass at most 1/50. All scalar optimization branches have Lean proofs; setting up the graph profiles and rounding them on real graphs is still paper only.

**Reduction.** Discard zero-weight vertices. Every edge outside A has disjoint A-neighbor sets, both with mass at least a/2, hence both exactly half and exactly complementary. Complete each pair of complementary nonempty signature classes to a biclique; other outside vertices are isolates in the outside graph. This maintains triangle freeness and dominates the objective. At a nontrivial pair each side is independent and can be combined with the A-half it avoids, hence has mass at most a/2 by maximal mass of A (also after completion, since completing can only reduce the independence number and A remains independent). Nontrivial means both capacities positive. All leftovers are assigned to the isolate class h. Write all following quantities on a scale multiplied by ten: A mass a ∈[19/5,4], B mass 10-a, target mass 5, edge target 2.

**B-only baseline.** Select all larger sides and h, trimming independently if already at 5. Otherwise greedy fill in order of ascending larger-side price p, with small-side capacity q. The last used price θ satisfies 0≤θ≤a/2≤2 and certifies cost
C=−θ(5−a)+∑q min(θ,p).
At θ≤1 one may use cost ≤θ(a−h)/2≤2, since the initial selection has size at least (10−a+h)/2. The same price bound handles all θ when h≥a−2.

**Pivot.** If a regular block has both sides at least 1 and total p+q≥2+h, split A into the signature halves of that block. In two alternative profiles, keep an A-half and the free opposite side of this block. From every other regular block choose the side with ≤a/4 A overlap; both profiles choose opposite sides (break ties oppositely). Their regular outside capacities sum to 10−a−h. Write W=(10−a)/2. If either profile has capacity ≥W, trim outside masses keeping the free side if possible, and pay at most a/4(W−1)≤2. Otherwise the two deficits to W sum to h, and the corresponding free-side masses sum to p+q≥2+h; at least one difference (free side less deficit) is ≥1. Add the necessary fraction from h, costing at most a/2 per unit, and pay at most a/4(W−1)≤2. If both sides exceed 1 and p+q>2+h, the conclusions are strict when a=4.

**Remaining scalar cases after pivot exclusion.** A heavy block means q>1; others are light. Zero heavy uses OutsideGreedy.cost_le_two (trim all outside capacities by a common factor to total 6 if remaining). One heavy uses OneHeavyPacking.outside_dual. At least three heavy uses ThreeHeavy.two_prices, selecting the two lowest prices among three heavy blocks, whose available smaller-side masses suffice. Two heavy uses TwoHeavyDual.light_sum and TwoHeavyFinish.finish: if B-only fails, no pivots imply light mass L<2/3, h+L≤a/2, and r*(5−X−a/4)≤2 for larger prices p≤r and X=max(p+s,r+q). Choose either both-large or a crossed selection from the two heavy blocks, according to the two equal-sized classes of opposite sign-pattern pairs for A. One class has zero A-neighborhood cell x≥a/4; that class contains a choice with combined outside capacity S≥X. The needed A mass b=5−S obeys 0≤b≤3a/4 because S≤a and X≥(10−a−h−L)/2. Take b from A in ascending cost-multiplicity cells 0,1,1,2, weights x,a/2−x,a/2−x,x. The resulting cost ≤r max(0,b−a/4)≤2. No light or isolated vertices are needed in this last profile.

## Rigidity at a=0.4: paper argument, ingredients independently checked
Still needs a fully pinned clean proof with edge deletion and graphon versions before any public claim. Reusing the above profiles appears to leave only balanced C5 and Petersen in the completed model. This may be useful for Razborov's Case 2 near 2/5; Case 1 remains open.

- Zero heavy, price θ=1, equality in the dual forces h=0 and exactly three (1,1) pairs (Lean SaturatedEndpoint.priceOne). θ<1 is strictly sparser. Interior θ∈(1,2) is strict by θ+(2−θ)^2<2, and if h>0 the upper bound can only improve after including h as zero-small-side blocks. θ=2 equality forces h=0 and two (2,1) pairs (priceTwo); the two-signature construction is strictly cheaper: in aligned orientation take both 2 sides, then A mass 1 all from the ≥1 zero cell; in crossed favorable orientation take 2 and 1, then A mass 2 by filling next at cost at most 1 per unit. For three unit pairs, any nonorthogonal pair of A signatures gives cost <2 by taking an A-half and cheaper complementary sides. If all pairs orthogonal, each 2-coordinate margin consists of four unit cells. The eight joint cells have parity weights x and 1−x. Choose a zero cell of lesser parity mass x≤1/2, and fill A mass 2 by going first to that cell and then three distance-one cells, each cap 1−x. Cost ≤2−x<2 for x>0. If x=0 the four A cells and six outside sides form Petersen. Lean SaturatedOrthogonal has the finite arithmetic.
- The baseline h≥2 is strict unless h=2 and p=q=2 for the sole nontrivial block: equality forces initial large-side mass exactly 4 (= h + half the remaining mass) and all prices used equal 2. With A split into halves of mass 2, the standard pivot plus half of C costs <2 if any C-to-A edge mass is missing in either half. Otherwise this is balanced C5. If any edges of the completed outside biclique are absent in the original, choosing whole C and one B-side and half of the other gives <2.
- Assume 0≤h<2 and at least one heavy. A strict pivot already yields <2, so all heavy total masses may be assumed ≤2+h. At a=4 there cannot be three strict heavy blocks. One-heavy normalized bound is strict for 2<u<4 (heavy total u), with one endpoint u=2 excluded by q>1 and the other u=4 forcing h≥2. For the proof: in the L0≤θ+1 branch at u≤14/5 use ≤u²/4≤1.96; for 14/5≤u≤3 use the decreasing parabola u²/4+7−5u/2 (<2); for 3≤u<4 use u²/4−u/2+(4−u)^2<2 by convexity between u=3 (1.75) and u=4 (2). In second branch L0>θ+1 previous normalized proof reduces to three endpoints θ=1, u/2, or 7−2u. At θ=1 use u/2+(3−u)^2<2 for 2<u≤3; at θ=u/2 with u≤14/5 use strict two-point convexity of u²/4+(7−5u/2)^2/4 as endpoints u=2 (2) and u=14/5 (1.96). At θ=7−2u (u≥14/5) bound ≤1.96. Convex interpolation or the decreasing post-kink tail preserve strictness.
- Exactly two heavy: if the optimal B-only cost is ≥2 rather than >2, same arguments yield L≤2/3 (L≥2 impossible as total mass 6 and both heavy masses exceed 2); the small-second branch is strict since p²≥17/9>16/9 under a candidate equality. The oblique branch is strict if L>0 from −rL/2+L²/4; if L=0 equality would require r=4/3 and q≤3r/4≤1, not heavy. Main certificate has an extra (L²−rL)/4<0 whenever L>0, so only L=0, r=3/2, |D|=0 and error saturation can remain. The strict main branch q>3r/4 gives r=p (otherwise there is strict slack before bounding d(r−q)); then both Δ=0 and q=s follow for equality. Thus h=2/3 and p=r=3/2, q=s=7/6. Refine orientations: if aligned zero cell x≥1, take both 3/2 sides, then A2 at cost≤3/2. If crossed favorable, need A mass 7/3; if x≤5/3, at least 1/3 of its positive fill can use the cheaper (7/6) single-incidence cell, saving ≥1/9 against the cost bound 2. If x>5/3, the multiplicity bound on edge mass is r·(2·7/3−4)=1. Thus strict in all these cases.

Missing edge check for Petersen: by edge transitivity each template edge is in the support of an exactly sparse half using positive masses on both endpoints. Any positive missing mass yields <1/50 by choosing an appropriate tight half. This is straightforward in a finite uniform quotient, but state and prove the graphon/measure version rather than assuming it.
