# Candidate resolution of Razborov's epsilon question (paper proof)
Do not count as solved until independent audit, primary priority check, and end-to-end Lean verification. The claim is existence of universal ε>0 for triangle-free graphs with α≥(2/5−ε)n, i.e. the explicit intermediate question in [Razborov §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09406v2). It is not the unrestricted Erdős #128.

## Limit reduction (both original cases)
Suppose counterexamples G_i with a_i=α(G_i)/n_i→.4 from below. Mark maximum independent A_i. Razborov §4.5 greedy procedure B_i would give cost ≤(.5−a_i)^2 if it reached size .5, impossible eventually. Otherwise all outside B_i have d_B>.5−a_i. Choose its eligible D_i. In Case 1 the cost bound is F_1(a,b)=(.5−a)(b−a)+b(.5−b)/2, with F_1(a,a)= a(.5−a)/2 and ∂_b F_1=.75−a−b ≤−.03 for a≥.39 and b≥a. Thus b_i−a_i→0. Case 2 gives same from CaseTwoDeficit.lean. Applying this on a subsequence, no original case exclusion is needed.

Take two-colour graphon cut limit (W,A) of (G_i,A_i), with aligned cut norm and A_i converge in measure. As in CASE-TWO-COMPACTNESS.md, A is independent mass .4 and β(W)≥.02. Row degrees converge in L1 into any aligned measurable set by cut-norm duality. Therefore:
- d_A≥.1 almost everywhere outside A (greedy stops, |B_i\A_i|→0).
- d_W≤.4 almost everywhere, since every finite triangle-free neighborhood is independent and its degree ≤a_i.
- K={x outside A : d_A(x)≤.2} has mass ≥.2. For any t>0, the limiting set d_A>.2+t is approximated in measure from below by original vertices with d_{A_i}>a_i/2 outside A_i, an exact independent set of mass at most a_i (two rows overlapping in A_i cannot be adjacent). Let t↓0.

## New key claim: saturation follows without assuming Razborov's Case 2
Let L={outside A : d_A<.2}, ℓ=|L|. If a root v∈A has positive-neighborhood support of mass ≥.1 in K and sees L positively, then that independent support has a subset E of mass .1 meeting L positively. The half A∪E has cost <.02. Thus almost every root seeing L has support in K of mass <.1.

Suppose ℓ>0. The following general consequence of the proof of Case 2 will be used: if there is a [0,1]-valued allocation D supported in K of mass .2 with ∫_L D>0, with d_D(v)≤.1 for almost every v∈A, then β(W)<.02. In fact extend Case 2 to weighted graphons with E_v(x)=D(x) W(v,x) (support independent), remainder F=D−E_v, and the same uniform proportional fill from F. The weighted triangle-free Mantel bound gives its quadratic cost. The Fubini second-moment proof is unchanged, giving at a=b=.4 the exact envelope Q=.02−3 Δ²/(16 b²), where Δ=b(.2)−ρ_{AD} = 2∫ D(.2−d_A)>0. See CASE-TWO-COMPACTNESS.md and CaseTwoDeficit.lean. If ℓ≥.1 pick D of mass .2 with at least .1 from L. A root seeing L has entire K support <.1, and one not seeing it sees at most the remaining .1. Contradiction.

Now 0<ℓ<.1. Write U=outside A∪L. On every edge of W inside U, the A profiles are exact complementary half indicators: disjoint supports, both masses ≥.2. All edges are thus supported on countably many distinct complementary block pairs P_j,Q_j (proof by the L1 profile pushforward and Fubini); leftover outside vertices have no edges in U. In particular all block vertices lie in K. Put T=∑ min(|P_j|,|Q_j|).
1. If 2T≥.2−ℓ, choose equal amounts (constant fractions are fine) from each side of finitely or countably many complementary pairs, for total mass .2−ℓ. Every root in A receives load exactly (.2−ℓ)/2 by complementarity, outside a common null set. Add all of L to form D. For roots seeing L the whole K support has mass <.1, and for other roots the uniform load is below .1. This is the preceding Case 2 strict certificate.
2. If 2T<.2−ℓ, select from U all leftover vertices plus the larger side of each pair. This independent set I has mass .6−ℓ−T>.5−ℓ/2. If |I|≥.5 trim it. Otherwise add a subset of L of mass r=.5−|I|<ℓ/2<.05; there is adequate capacity. Each such point has total degree into U at most d_W−d_A≤.4−.1=.3, so the resulting half costs at most .3r+r²/2 <.01625<.02. No maximum independent-set upper bound on the graphon itself is used here.

Both are impossible. Hence ℓ=0 and the limit is saturated.

## Checked bridges and scope
The short weighted Case 2 derivation can also be read without the original polynomial: write z(x)=.2−d_A(x), Z=∫Dz, e(v)=d_D(v), h(v)=.1−e(v) and R(v)=∫D(x)W(v,x)z(x)dx. The fractional neighborhood-and-fill construction gives β−.02≤.1h−.75h²−R (bound d_A≤.2 on the F remainder, EF by complete adjacency, FF by weighted Mantel). Fubini gives ∫_A h=Z and ∫_A R=.2Z−∫Dz²≥.1Z. Thus averaging and Cauchy give β−.02≤−.75(Z/.4)²<0. The finite weighted averaging identities and strictness are checked in FractionalCaseTwo.lean; deriving the half for the full graphon and applying the ambient compactness theorems remain paper steps.

If constructing a graphon sequence from finite counterexamples, uniform independent twin blowups preserve normalized α and fractional optimum exactly, by aggregation of the vertex weights; use even multiplicities tending to infinity. Conversely in a uniform graph a fractional optimum may be rounded until at most one coordinate is fractional by varying two coordinates in opposite directions: the cost is concave in that move because the off-diagonal coefficient is nonnegative. If the order is even all are integral; if odd discard the last fraction to get a floor half. Thus this is an ordinary sparse-half statement including odd sizes.

For completeness, weighted Mantel in the graphon proof follows by giving the selected residual capacity its own finite measure of mass M. For every positive edge the degrees into that measure sum to at most M by disjointness of supports. Multiplying by W and integrating gives 2∫d²≤M∫d, and Cauchy yields ∫d≤M²/2, i.e. unordered edge mass at most M²/4. This includes residual measures with overlap with E in the original coordinates; edge bilinearity accounts for the cross term.
