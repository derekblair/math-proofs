# Erdős 128: near-endpoint Case 2, a compactness bridge
Working paper argument, not a proof of general near-independence or of #128. The important point is that cut convergence alone does **not** pass an upper bound on the independence number. The active-side cap below bypasses this.

Let G_i be triangle-free finite graphs with normalized maximum independent A_i, a_i→.4 from below, whose optimal fractional half has cost ≥.02. In Razborov §4.5 suppose the growth stops at B_i, and the lowest-degree eligible set D_i of mass 1−a_i−b_i falls in Case 2 (all B_i roots have D_i-degree ≤.5−b_i). Define Δ_i=b_i(1−a_i−b_i)−ρ_{B_i D_i}. The difference identity in CaseTwoDeficit.lean yields b_i−a_i=o(1), Δ_i=o(1). Since D_i consists of the lowest d_{B_i} values outside B_i, all outside vertices at d_{B_i}<b_i/2−t occupy mass ≤Δ_i/(2t) for every fixed t>0, once Δ_i/(2t)<|D_i|. (D_i lies below or at b_i/2, by the independent high-degree set bound.) Thus outside A_i the mass of d_{A_i}<.2−t is o(1), since |B_i\A_i|=o(1).

Take a subsequence in the 2-coloured cut metric of (G_i,A_i), with W_i → W in cut norm and |A_i△A|→0 after common relabeling. For fixed S, the L1 norm of row degree differences into S is at most twice cut norm: integrate positive and negative sets separately. The error from moving S=A_i to A is at most their symmetric-difference measure. Hence W on A×A is zero, |A|=.4 and d_A≥.2 almost everywhere outside A. Triangle density zero follows by the counting lemma. The fractional half objective β is cut-continuous with constant 1/2 for aligned graphons: express f(x)f(y) on 0≤f≤1 by layer cake, apply the cut norm pointwise, and take infima over the common mass-.5 domain. Therefore β(W)≥.02.

## Countably many active complementary pairs
On almost every edge xy of positive W outside A, triangle freeness forces the positive A-row supports of x,y to be disjoint. Their row integrals total at least .4, and each is bounded by the support mass; hence both rows are 0/1 indicators of complementary half-sets. The profile map into L1(A) is measurable, and the pushforward of outside measure has only countably many positive-mass atoms. The product measure of the pairs of complementary profiles with one non-atomic profile is zero, by Fubini. For each pair with positive W mass, remove vertices of zero degree toward the opposite profile and call the resulting positive sets P,Q. The rest outside A has zero W edges outside A. Completing each active P×Q is triangle-free and can only increase β. For a given active pair there is a partition A=S∪T into two equal parts, W=1 on P×S and Q×T, and W=0 on P×T and Q×S.

## The active side cap (do not assume the limit independence number)
Fix such a pair. For τ>0 let Pτ={x∈P : d_Q^W(x)>2τ}, so Pτ↑P as τ↓0. On W_i choose X_i⊂Pτ\A_i by imposing d_Q^{W_i}(x)>τ and d_{S∩A_i}^{W_i}(x)>|S∩A_i|/2. By L1 convergence of these two degrees, |X_i|→|Pτ|. Choose T_i⊂T∩A_i by imposing d_Q^{W_i}(y)>|Q|−τ; since W=1 on T×Q, |T_i|→.2. Any two members of X_i have a common neighbor in S∩A_i. Any x∈X_i, y∈T_i have a common neighbor in Q. Thus triangle freeness forces X_i∪T_i to be independent in the *finite* G_i split into identical twins (or water-filled across the relabeled vertex cells). The original independent-set cap gives |X_i|+|T_i|≤a_i: a measurable independent set can intersect only a family of independent original vertex cells in positive measure. Hence |Pτ|≤.2, and |P|≤.2 by monotone convergence. Swap P,Q for the other side. Finite common-anchor and active-cap step stated and checked in ActiveProfileCap.lean and ActiveProfileCapWeighted.lean.

## Closing the bridge, conditional on auditing the endpoint rigidity branches
The saturated normalized proof now applies to W without assuming α(W)≤.4. Countably many block pairs do not cause a new extremum: the larger/smaller capacities have summable mass, greedy fill is the threshold rule on this countable distribution, and at any positive threshold only finitely many prices lie above or at it. Convex rebate and scalar bounds extend by absolute convergence; the zero-heavy equality tests force every nonzero block rebate and mass-cap constraint tight, leaving finite templates. For the weighted side distances, profiles choose fractions constant on their cells. Audit all rigidity algebra/strict cases in SATURATED-THEOREM.md before using the conclusion that equality β(W)=.02 permits only the balanced C5/Petersen graphons. Missing edge mass in the Petersen template is seen by a rotated/relabeled exact cost-.02 half using both endpoints of the affected pair. The C5 missing edges have directly specified detecting halves.

Cut convergence to either finite 0/1 step template implies aligned L1 convergence: sum the absolute edge discrepancies rectangle by rectangle, where sign is constant. For finite graph edit distance, randomly assign each original vertex to a template class using the proportions of its relabeled cell in each part; expected mismatch count is O(n_i²||W_i−W||1+n_i), and part sizes concentrate near their expectations. Thus there are partitions close to the balanced blowup in edit distance. Apply the local exact result of Norin–Yepremyan §5 for C5 (or their density threshold) and Thm 6.3 for Petersen. This contradicts β(G_i)>.02 and would give some ε>0 for the stated Case 2 branch. This remains paper only and is a conditional step until endpoint rigidity/countable extensions receive a full audit. No implication for Case 1.

Case 1 new necessary condition to attack: for a limit W with A independent mass .4, β≥.02, let K={x outside A: d_A(x)≤.2} and L={d_A(x)<.2}. For almost every root v∈A its positive-neighbor support is independent. If its support in K has mass ≥.1 and intersects L in positive mass then select an independent .1 subset of that support meeting L. Adding it to A costs strictly less than .02, a contradiction. Thus any root seeing a positive L set must have support in K of mass <.1. In a near-extremal limit high roots cannot meet L even when one discards the particular bottom-degree choice D. Derive a new half from separation, or use it to force Case 2. Currently not achieved.

### Small-low-mass restriction beyond the original Case 2 split
For a putative near-endpoint limiting graphon, the stopped growth always gives d_A≥.1 a.e. outside A. The set K has mass ≥.2: for each t>0 the limit set {d_A>.2+t} is approximated in mass from below by original vertices outside A_i with d_Ai>a_i/2, which form an exact independent set and hence have mass ≤a_i; let t↓0.

If L has mass ≥.1, choose D⊂K of mass .2 with at least .1 mass from L (possible as |K|≥.2). A root seeing L positively has support in K of mass <.1 by the preceding separation argument. All other roots see at most |D\L|≤.1. Thus the Case 2 inequality can be applied directly in W with A=B and this D, whose Δ>0 gives β(W)<.02. To translate its proof to a general [0,1]-valued triangle-free graphon, use E's allocation W(v,x)·1_D(x) for each root; its positive support is independent, its mass is e≤.1 and the remainder capacity is (1−W(v,x))·1_D(x). Fill proportionally from this remainder. All bounds still hold: the FF term uses weighted triangle-free Mantel; the E cross term uses Fubini and exactly the same square of d_A. Thus any genuine remaining nonsaturated near-endpoint obstruction has 0<|L|<.1. The full case remains unresolved; consider whether a size-.2 subset/fractional D can be selected whose saturated portion loads every root disjoint from L at ≤.1.

A further exact limiting necessary condition uses the *first variation*: for every tight E⊂S={d_A=.2} of mass .1 supported on the positive neighborhood of one high root, A∪E has cost .02 and degrees of selected E points into A∪E equal .2. If a subset outside A∪E has d_{A∪E}<.2 bounded away from it, replace a sufficiently small equal mass of E by that subset; first-order gain dominates the O(ε²) term. Hence for a.e. x∈L, ∫_E W(x,z) dz≥.2−d_A(x). This holds simultaneously over all tight E outside a common null set, since their indicator functions form a separable subset in L1 and the integral is 1-Lipschitz in E. In particular for a high root v with a saturated support S_v of mass ≥.1, |S_v\supp W(x,·)| ≤ d_A(x)−.1: choose the sparsest E of mass .1 using as many nonneighbors of x as possible, then the integral is at most max(0,.1−|S_v\supp|). This constrains the high/low separation but does not yet contradict a low population of mass less than .1.
