# Digits of the permanent above the rank-forced prime-power divisor

Let B_p(n,r) be the sharp universal lower bound on ν_p(per A) for n×n integer A of rank r modulo p. For an integer τ≥1, define the normalized residue
    D_{p,τ}(A) = (per A / p^{B_p(n,r)}) mod p^τ.
This is the same data as per A mod p^{B_p(n,r)+τ}, after dropping the guaranteed zero digits. Here are an algorithm for p=2 and a fixed-odd-prime hardness result for τ=1 and r=1. Neither asserts hardness for unrestricted exact permanent in a particular time exponent. [Related global modular-rank PR #97](https://github.com/derekblair/math-proofs/pull/97)

## Binary normalized digits in n^{O(r+τ)} time and polynomial workspace

Compute r=rank_F₂ A. Choose an F₂ factorization U V^T with r latent colors and representatives U,V in {0,1}. Write A=UV^T+2C exactly, with signed integer C. In the bipartite graph with C-edge weights, give each unmatched original row i the monomer weight Σ_a U_ia X_a and each unmatched original column j the weight Σ_a V_ja Y_a. Let P(X,Y) sum the product of edge and monomer weights over all partial matchings, including the empty matching. Then
    per A = Σ_{α∈N^r, |α|≤n} 2^{n−|α|} (∏_a α_a!) [X^α Y^α] P.
A partial matching of C-edges fixes the number n−|α| of chosen corrections. Selecting rank-one colors on unmatched rows and columns gives the two monomial exponents. Equal color counts permit α_a! bijections within each color.

For any color count α, the integer factor has binary valuation n−Σ_a s₂(α_a), where s₂ is the popcount. Since |α|≤n, its valuation is at least B₂(n,r). To see equality for the minimum, maximize the total popcount by taking the cheapest binary positions at most r times each. For r>0 put L=floor(log₂(1+n/r)). This gives maximum rL+floor((n−r(2^L−1))/2^L). All r units positions are chosen, so the minimizer can have every α_a≥1 and is also the rank-only optimum proved earlier. For r=0 the factor is 2^n, with only the zero-dimensional coefficient. Divide the factor by 2^{B₂} in Z before modular arithmetic. Every coefficient of P now needs precision τ only, even when B₂+τ grows linearly with n.

Encode P as one weighted hafnian. List all m=2n original vertices v_i. For i=1,…,m introduce a private port b_i and a backbone vertex a_i. For i>1, join a_(i−1),b_i,a_i in a triangle, and for i=1 just join b_1 to a_1, as if a_0 were absent. All these auxiliary edge weights are one. Join v_i to b_i by its monomer linear form. A perfect matching first selects a partial matching among original vertices. Every unmatched original is then forced to use its attachment, deleting the corresponding port from the auxiliary completion. Sweep along the backbone. When b_i is available, exactly one choice pairs it to an available left vertex or, if that vertex is unavailable, to the right vertex. This preserves the availability state. When b_i is deleted, a free left vertex must use the backbone edge, and an already used left vertex leaves the right vertex free. This toggles the state. Start with a_0 unavailable and require a_m used at the end. Every even deletion set therefore has one completion and every odd deletion set has none. The number of unmatched originals of a partial matching on 2n vertices is even. Consequently the hafnian of the 6n-vertex graph is exactly P over Z. Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt already give a weighted matching-to-hafnian reduction via parity gadgets and an n^{O(τr)} multivariate bound. The direct lifted-ring evaluation below gives an additive exponent and polynomial workspace. [Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00629)

Choose the lifted ring R=(Z/2^τ)[T]/(f), with f a monic lift of an irreducible degree-d polynomial over F₂ and 2^d≥n+1, d=O(log(n+1)). Find f by enumerating O(n+1) candidates and using the usual finite-field irreducibility test. Elements with nonzero residue in F_{2^d} are units. Invert the residue first, then correct its lift by a geometric series in the even error. Use n+1 representatives with distinct residues as interpolation nodes. Their pairwise differences are units. For each α, extract [X^αY^α]P in R by serial tensor-product Lagrange across (n+1)^{2r} nodes. Precompute the univariate weights polynomially. There are at most (n+1)^r coefficient queries, which can be visited serially. Coefficients known to come from Z/2^τ are read from the constant basis coordinate of R. No factor of two is inverted modulo a power of two.

For each scalar evaluation, use the Hirai–Namba hafnian recurrence over the lifted ring. If every entry in the first row is even, the usual expansion evaluates only smaller matrices at precision τ−1, with the even edge coefficients raising the result to precision τ. Otherwise permute a unit to position (1,2). For each j>2 add c_j times row and column 2 to row and column j, reset the diagonal to zero, and choose c_j to zero entry (1,j) exactly. The hafnian change is c_j times the hafnian with row and column j copied from 2 and the mutual entry zero. In that matrix the copied vertices can be paired to any two distinct other partners in either order, giving 2 times a sum of products of original weights and hafnians on four fewer vertices. Thus correction terms require only precision τ−1. After all operations the first row has one entry, giving a single same-precision recursive call on two fewer vertices. At precision zero return zero, and at the empty matrix return one modulo the requested precision. This gives n^{O(τ)} ring operations and polynomial space by depth-first traversal, since every recursive call reduces matrix order and there are polynomially many lower-precision branches between precision decreases. Arithmetic in R takes polynomial bit time and space in τ and d. The total time is n^{O(r+τ)} times a polynomial in the input and precision, with polynomial bit workspace. [Hirai–Namba, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1603.08073)

## At a fixed odd prime, the first normalized digit is hard even at rank one

Fix any odd prime p. Start with a bipartite graph and ask for the number m_k of k-matchings modulo p. Let k≥1, choose L≥k+1 and increase it until N=p^L−1+k is at least the larger part size, and pad the two parts with isolated vertices to obtain square binary incidence M. The integer matrix A=J_N+pM is dense, has two entry values {1,1+p}, and has rank one modulo p. Expand exactly
    per(J_N+pM) = Σ_{q=0}^N p^q (N−q)! m_q.
Write t₀=p^L−1 and v₀=ν_p(t₀!). For a term with q=k+j, j>0, the relative valuation of its integer multiplier compared with p^k t₀! is j−ν_p(j!)>0, since the factors t₀,t₀−1,…,t₀−j+1 have exactly the same valuations as 1,…,j. For q=k−j, 1≤j≤k, the relative valuation is L−j+ν_p((j−1)!)>0, since t₀+1=p^L and the following j−1 factors have valuations of 1,…,j−1. Thus q=k is the unique minimizing multiplier. The prior rank-one formula B_p(N,1)=min_{1≤t≤N}(N−t+ν_p(t!)) gives B_p(N,1)=k+v₀. The normalization is attained among rank-one matrices: the block diagonal matrix with blocks $J_{t_0}$ and $pI_k$ has rank one modulo $p$ and permanent exactly $p^k t_0!$. The valuation is minimized among all correction counts, including q=N. A single normalized digit query returns (t₀!/p^{v₀}) m_k mod p. The factorial unit is invertible and computable by stripping powers of p from each factor modulo p. The order N is O(p^{k+1}+p n) where n is the initial larger part size. This is an FPT reduction with a single oracle call, all at the fixed rank and fixed normalized precision τ=1.

To pin the source hardness to bipartite inputs, use Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt Lemma 5 and the construction in Lemma 6. Restrict the cubic pattern class to cubic bipartite graphs of unbounded tree-width. An explicit class comes from even toroidal grids replacing every degree-four vertex by a four-cycle of cardinal direction ports. Flip the two local colors by grid parity and connect corresponding ports as prescribed by the grid. The resulting graphs are cubic and bipartite and contain the original grids as minors. Discard host edges whose pattern colors are not adjacent in the pattern. This preserves all colorful copies, and the two sides of the bipartite pattern now induce a bipartition of the host. In Lemma 6 all three port copies of each host vertex stay on its side, and its consistency gadget is bipartite with those ports on the same side. The AND gadget uses an odd three-edge path between copies of adjacent host vertices, on opposite sides, and pendant leaves. It remains bipartite. Thus the paper’s modular cancellation proof still yields Mod_p W[1]-hardness of colorful matchings on bipartite hosts. Its inclusion-exclusion reduction to uncolored k-matching only deletes edges and preserves bipartiteness. This gives the desired Mod_p W[1]-hard source without any ETH implication. [Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt, §3 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00629)

Combining the pieces gives binary tractability at fixed r,τ and Mod_p W[1]-hardness under FPT Turing reductions at every fixed odd p already at r=τ=1. The latter finds the first digit beyond a large guaranteed divisor and is separate from the variable-p hardness of small absolute precision in PR #97. The proof goes through one normalized-residue query after the source problem is bipartite. There is no unrestricted running-time lower bound.

## Reproduction and scope

- permanent-normalized-prime-power-replay.js checked 96 signed binary quotient cases, including 67 nonzero residues, and compared every polynomial coefficient of the weighted hafnian gadget with an independent partial-matching enumeration on 56 cases. It checked the weaker finite-difference variant on 80 odd-prime cases. It checked the sharper single-query digit identity on 150 padded cases, 100 with nonzero source residue, at order up to 1,332. The padded exact evaluator assigns original rows among special columns and counts the symmetric ordinary columns independently, then completes with the all-ones rows.
- permanent-normalized-binary-hafnian-replay.js checked 200 recursive lifted-ring hafnian evaluations against direct pairings and 320 tensor Lagrange coefficients using degrees two and three and precisions one through four.
- The standalone [FactorialWindow.lean](FactorialWindow.lean) checks the unique factorial-multiplier window and its local factorial valuation identities. It does not check the hafnian algorithm, the permanent expansion, the valuation bound for matrices, the source reduction, or fixed-prime hardness. Run `lake env lean -s 65536 /absolute/path/to/FactorialWindow.lean` under Lean 4.34.1 with Mathlib at `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`. Run both JavaScript replays with Node.js.
