# Candidate affirmative solution: subdivided-K4 edge Folkman existence
Written proof draft (27 November 2026). Independent mathematical review checked the exact hypothesis, spherical spectral theorem, determinant bound, second moment and deletion estimates and found no gap. The probabilistic and spectral portions are not checked in Lean. No claim of publication priority.
Target Hassan–Radziszowski–Van Overberghe Problem 1, arXiv:2605.16542v1 §6 (still open in their May 2026 paper), also Xu–Liang–Radziszowski arXiv:1705.06268. Let H be K4 with one edge subdivided once, i.e. complement(P2 disjoint-union P3). On vertices u,v,x,y,z use edges xy, ux,uy,vx,vy,uz,vz: 7 edges, exactly 2 triangles. This is non-induced.

## Random geometric route, in six pieces

1. Fix a sufficiently small constant tau>0, put c=1/(tau^2-(5/6)tau^3), and d=ceil(c log N). Uniform independent points on S^(d-1); put an edge iff inner product ≥tau. For a fixed finite graph F set D_F(tau)=max det of correlation matrix with entries ≥tau at its edges, and I_F=−1/2 log D_F. Joint Gram density for m fixed points is C(d,m) det(M)^((d-m-1)/2) on the PSD unit-diagonal region, with polynomial-in-d normalizer for fixed m. For a direct derivation, expose points sequentially. The projection z of point j onto the span of j−1 preceding points has density Γ(d/2)/(π^((j−1)/2)Γ((d−j+1)/2))·(1−||z||²)^((d−j−1)/2). Changing its orthonormal coordinates to dot products with the previous points contributes det(M_(j−1))^(-1/2). The Schur complement is det(M_j)/det(M_(j−1)), so the powers telescope to the displayed density. The gamma ratios are polynomial by Stirling’s formula. Thus Pr(F on labeled points)=exp(−d I_F+o(d))=N^(−c I_F+o(1)). Upper bound integrate on a bounded cube; lower approximate maximizing matrix by positive-definite matrices strictly above threshold on all constrained entries. There is a strictly feasible PD correlation matrix (e.g. equicorrelation 2tau for small tau); convex mixing and continuity justify approaching the same maximum. No independence of the edges is asserted.

2. For each fixed F, I_F(tau)=e(F)tau^2/2 − t(F)tau^3+O_F(tau^4) (t=#triangles). Let A adjacency, B have entry (A²)_ij at distinct nonedges and zero at diagonal and on edges, M=I+tau A+tau² B. It is PD and feasible at small tau, tr(AB)=0, so log det M=−e(F)tau²+2t(F)tau³+O(tau^4). Any determinant maximizer M* is I+O(tau): det≥det M, trace m, and for eigenvalues s∈(0,m], log s≤(s−1)−(s−1)^2/(2m²), because −log''(s)≥1/m² between 1 and s. This bounds Frobenius distance. By concavity of log det, log det M*−log det M ≤ tr(M^-1 (M*−M)). Diagonal difference zero. On edges M^-1_ij=−tau+O(tau²)<0 and M*−M≥0, so contributions ≤0. On nonedges M^-1_ij=O(tau³), by the definition of B, times O(tau), so total O(tau^4). This yields the uniform expansion for each finite F considered (tau fixed sufficiently small). Can also derive from max-det completion via implicit function theorem, not necessary.

3. Let E, T, D denote edge, triangle, diamond (two triangles sharing an edge). The elementary triangle detector rate is D0=I_T−I_E=tau²−tau³+O(tau^4). More exactly det(T)=(1−tau)^2(1+2tau) and det(E)=1−tau², so I_T−I_E=I_E+I_eff where I_eff=−1/2 log(1−(tau/(1+tau))²). The triangle determinant follows by averaging over the six vertex permutations using log-det concavity; the determinant of the averaged matrix is decreasing in its common off-diagonal entry on [tau,1]. Then with this c, 1−c(I_E+I_eff)>0. Each vertex link has binomial m=N^(1−c I_E+o(1)) vertices uniformly in its spherical cap conditional on m. [Theorem 5.1 of Liu, Mohanty, Schramm and Yang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00158v1) with q=ΦDip(d)(tau/(1+tau)) and q m=N^positive holds, d=Theta(log m). It gives both extreme nontrivial normalized eigenvalue magnitudes ≤tau/(1+tau)+o(1) with arbitrarily strong inverse polynomial failure in m. Chernoff for m and a union bound give all links at once. Choose tau small so the upper value is λ<1/3. For any sign assignment on link vertices, normalized Rayleigh quotient gives cut ≤(1+λ)/2 of link edges (ignore isolated vertices, which contribute no edges to either side). Each coloring of original graph edges induces a sign cut in each link. Counting mixed wedges twice per bichromatic triangle, and three total wedges per triangle, every coloring has ≥(1−3λ)/4 of its triangles monochromatic. This is the standard Spencer–Lu link-maxcut argument; see [Lu, Corollaries 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1137/070686743).

4. Concentration of number of triangles: expectation N^(3−c I_T+o(1)), tends to infinity since c I_T≈3/2. For distinct triples with disjoint vertices independent, sharing one vertex also independent by conditioning on that rotation-invariant common vertex. Covariance for two shared vertices bounded by P(diamond); normalized variance ≤O(1/ET)+N^(−2+c(2I_T−I_D)+o(1)), tends to zero because 2I_T−I_D=tau²/2+O(tau^4). Thus T count is ≥1/2 expectation w.h.p.

5. For every H copy mark a single fixed edge type (e.g. uz); delete all those marked edges. Count triangles lost by pairs (H embedding, triangle touching its marked edge). Third triangle vertex inside H gives O(count H), expectation N^(5−c I_H+o(1)); outside gives a fixed F=H plus a fresh triangle sharing only its marked edge, with 6 vertices, 9 edges, 3 triangles, expectation N^(6−c I_F+o(1)). The exponent gaps vs total expected triangles are negative because c(I_H−I_T)>2 and c(I_F−I_T)>3: I_H−I_T=2tau²−tau³+O(tau^4), I_F−I_T=3tau²−2tau³+O(tau^4), and a=5/6 lies between 2/3 and 1. Markov gives o(T) triangles lost w.h.p. The deleted graph is H-free; even if many copies share an edge, the overcount is only in the safe direction. This avoids any maximum codegree assumption.

6. Any coloring of the remaining edges extends arbitrarily before deletion. Step 3 gives a fixed positive fraction monochromatic in the original; Step 5 loses o(T), so at least one remains. Therefore the remaining finite H-free graph arrows triangles. This proves the assertion on paper using the cited spectral theorem. The Lean files check finite deletion/charging and the aggregate mixed-corner inequality, not Steps 1–5 as a complete probabilistic proof. No explicit numerical witness or Lean proof of existence is claimed.


## Extension to a finite forbidden family

The same proof applies to a finite family of simple graphs F with v(F)≥4 and e(F)≥2v(F)−3, provided that each F with e(F)=2v(F)−3 has strictly fewer than v(F)−2 triangles. Choose one constant a<1 larger than every t(F)/(v(F)−2) for the equality cases and put c=1/(tau²−a·tau³). In Step 5 the two exponent conditions for F are c(I_F−I_T)>v(F)−3 and c(I_(F+T)−I_T)>v(F)−2. They hold at quadratic order when e(F)>2v(F)−3. For equality, their cubic slacks before multiplying by c are a(v(F)−3)−t(F)+1 and a(v(F)−2)−t(F). Both are strictly positive by the choice of a: t/(v−2)≥(t−1)/(v−3) whenever t≤v−2. A finite sum of the loss bounds is still negligible. This extension has not been checked in Lean. No priority claim.
