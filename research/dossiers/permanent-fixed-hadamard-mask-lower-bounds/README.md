# Factorial lower bound for universal Hadamard-masked determinant powers

**Theorem (written proof).** Let K be a field, q≥2 an integer not divisible by char K, and X be an n×n matrix of independent variables. Consider the exact identity
per(X^{∘q}) = Σ_{i=1}^m w_i det(M_i∘X)^q,
with fixed scalar Hadamard masks M_i over K and arbitrary scalar weights w_i. Then m≥n!, and n! is attainable (use the support mask of each permutation and the determinant sign to the qth power as its weight). This prohibits exact input-independent universal cubature of the Godsil–Gutman square pattern with fewer than n! masks, even with signed weights or zero mask entries. It is not a bound on adaptive algorithms, different masks in the factors (for which a separate bound follows), affine masks, complex absolute squares, general determinantal complexity, or exact dense permanent algorithms.

Write X_σ=Π_j X_{j,σ(j)}, v_i(σ)=Π_j (M_i)_{j,σ(j)}. For σ,τ, look at the monomial X_σ^(q−1)X_τ. On a common edge, all q determinant factors must choose it. Each nontrivial component of the union of the two bipartite matchings is an alternating even cycle. In an individual factor, choosing either edge orientation anywhere in a cycle forces that orientation around the entire cycle. The coefficient target forces precisely one of the q factors to choose the τ orientation, independently on each of c cycles. Hence there are q^c ordered factorizations (even when q=2 and the ordered names σ,τ are reversed). All yield the same mask monomial v_i(σ)^(q−1)v_i(τ). All yield the same product of determinant signs sign(σ)^(q−1)sign(τ), since each switch toggles the sign by (−1)^(length on the row-side−1), once per component independently of the assigned factor. Thus the coefficient is q^c times this nonzero sign times Σ_i w_i v_i(σ)^(q−1)v_i(τ). If σ=τ, c=0 and sign(σ)^q may be −1 for odd q. On the target per(X^{∘q}) this coefficient is exactly 1 for σ=τ and 0 otherwise. Therefore the matrix G_{στ}=Σ_i w_i [sign(σ)v_i(σ)]^(q−1)[sign(τ)v_i(τ)] is exactly I on the n! permutations. It factors as an n!×m matrix times m×n!, so m≥n!. Note the sign on the diagonal: writing G without signs for general odd q gives a nonsingular signed diagonal, not literally I.

For q=2 the same proof works if the identity is required only on Boolean input matrices. Each function has unique multilinear polynomial on the Boolean cube over any field, by Möbius inversion in the support variables. A determinant-square monomial's support specifies original exponents: on each row there are either two distinct edges, each exponent 1, or one edge of exponent 2. Hence Boolean folding adds no distinct monomials with the same support. The same lifted identity and lower bound follow over odd characteristic. Over a characteristic-zero field, agreement of fixed masked sums on the positive-integer matrix grid implies the formal polynomial identity by repeated univariate interpolation; just excluding zero inputs does not change the lower bound within this model. For formal border variants in characteristic zero, the lift assigns to each (σ,τ) the selected coefficient divided by q^c (note no sign needed when absorbed in v), producing rank≤m from m atoms; closed rank minors extend to the closure. This does not rule out constant-relative-error estimators.

In characteristic p with p dividing q, write q=p^a q' with p not dividing q'. Pass to a perfect extension, choose p^a-th roots of weights, and pull back Frobenius using its injectivity; the identity becomes its q' version. If q'≥2, factorial lower bound still applies. A pure p-power reduces instead to the ordinary same-size linear sum of masked determinants representing per(X), a different model; in characteristic two a single determinant suffices. The q=2 Boolean consequence in characteristic two similarly gives one determinant, not this lower bound.

## Context

The fixed-mask square setting is motivated by the unbiased determinant-square estimator of [Godsil and Gutman](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jgt.3190050203); compare [Barvinok's approximation work](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/barvinok/wp-content/uploads/sites/1434/2025/05/permanent.pdf). These exact representation bounds do not rule out approximation or adaptive evaluation. No historical priority is claimed.

## Distinct factors and complex magnitudes

For each summand allow q independently chosen fixed masks and take a product of q determinants, still with target per(X^{∘q}). Let C be a Birkhoff clique of permutations: any distinct pair differs by one alternating cycle. On a clique-indexed coefficient lift, for offdiagonal σ,τ take the coefficient of X_σ^(q−a)X_τ^a; for diagonal take binom(q,a) times the coefficient of X_σ^q. Here 1≤a≤q−1 and the integer B=binom(q,a) is nonzero in the field. In a given atom, the offdiagonal coefficient selects an a-subset of factor labels to use τ on the single cycle. The same sum over a-subsets gives the diagonal factor B. Thus with v_j(σ) the signed matching product in mask j, the local lift is a sum over a-subsets S of the outer products (Π_{j∉S}v_j(σ)) (Π_{j∈S}v_j(τ)), rank at most binom(q,a) as an integer. The local target lift is B I. Specialize to any disjoint block partition with cliques K_j, zeros off blocks and identity in leftover dimensions. Since each atom and the target become products in disjoint block variables, product lifts are Kronecker products. The rank bound is Π|K_j| ≤ m binom(q,a)^r over r blocks. For fixed q and char∤q choose a=1; any clique size k>q, e.g. identity plus k−1 star transpositions in S_k, makes this exponential in n. For positive characteristic and q not a pure power of p, take a=p^v_p(q) and use Lucas to get nonzero C. For q=2 still valid with Boolean-only equality in odd characteristic by exact row-total-degree Boolean folding. For q>2 Boolean-only equality not implied. This extension covers mixed masks without claiming the full n! bound there.

If a=1 and an atom has only t distinct masks among its q factors, rank ≤t by coalescing identical factor-label contributions, over char∤q. In particular real variables, complex masks and real signed weights in Σ w_i |det(M_i⊙X)|^(2q) have local rank ≤2 (q real-natural exponent parameter; total degree power is 2q), independent of the magnitude exponent. Directly divide offdiagonal coefficients selected at (2q−1,1) by 2q, and leave diagonal: local matrix is |vσ|^(2q−2) Re(conj(vσ)vτ), rank ≤2 over the reals. Huang–Li–Swartz–Sze, https://arxiv.org/html/2212.12655v2 §2 and App A.1, exhibit a 13-clique on S_5, so m ≥ ceil((13/2)^floor(n/5)). S_3 is a 6-clique giving 3^floor(n/3). Both bounds hold for Boolean inputs at q=1. For one generic 3×3 block at q=1, three masks attain the bound: partition the six permutations into three pairs and put each paired matchings' union in the mask support; that union has exactly two perfect matchings. One nonshared entry's phase makes their signed coefficients differ relatively by i; the absolute square is exactly the sum of the two squared matching monomials. Product attains 3^r on arbitrary disjoint 3×3 block diagonal inputs, not claimed tight for the unrestricted n×n model.

For complex magnitude q≥2 and nonnegative real weights the unrestricted polynomial lower bound is exactly n!. If a mask supports both σ and τ, the coefficient of X_σ^(2q−2)X_τ^2 is |vσ|^(2q) times the product, over nontrivial alternating cycles, of C(q,2)(z²+conj(z)²)+q²|z|², where z is that cycle's nonzero signed ratio. On each cycle choose exactly two of the 2q factor labels for τ: both unbarred, both barred, or one of each. The local factor is ≥q|z|²>0. If the mask does not support both, that coefficient is 0, because at least one needed entry appears positively with scalar zero. The permanent target coefficient is 0 for σ≠τ and q≥2. Nonnegative weights prohibit cancellation, so every positive-weight atom supports at most one permutation, and every permutation must be supported somewhere by its positive diagonal polynomial coefficient. n! sparse zero masks achieve equality. This last result does not apply to signed weights or Boolean-only q≥2.

Compile with `lake env lean -s 65536 /absolute/path/to/PermanentPowerMaskRank.lean` under Lean 4.34.1 with Mathlib pinned at `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`.

The identical-power lower bound, the mixed-mask exponential bounds, the signed complex magnitude bound, and the factorial bound for nonnegative weights at higher powers have different hypotheses. None bounds unrestricted exact algorithms for the dense permanent, general determinantal representations, masks chosen from the input, or approximation error.
