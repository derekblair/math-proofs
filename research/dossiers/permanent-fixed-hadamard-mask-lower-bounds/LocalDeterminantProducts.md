# Factorial-scale lower bounds for products of different local determinants

Let K be a field, q≥2 with q nonzero in K, and X an n×n matrix of independent variables. Consider a polynomial identity
    per(X^{∘q}) = Σ_{i=1}^m w_i ∏_{a=1}^q det(A_{i,a}(X)).
The scalar weights and all matrix coefficients are fixed. Each affine determinant row may depend on the union of up to b specified raw rows or raw columns of X. A row may use both types. Let M bound the sum of the orders of the q determinants in every summand. When M≥qn,
    m ≥ n! / ((b+1)^M binom(qn,n) binom(M,n)).
At homogeneous order n in every factor, (b+1)^M improves to b^{qn}. In particular, fixed q,b and M=O(n) require exp(n log n−O(n)) summands. The trivial support-per-permutation expression uses n!, so the leading factorial order is sharp in this model. The result covers distinct factors, fixed affine offsets, and factors with more than n rows as long as their total order is linear. It is an exact coefficient-rank lower bound for this specified model.

## Coefficient map and rank factorization

For a permutation σ let X_σ=∏_j X_{j,σ(j)}. Define the linear map E from input polynomials to the n!×n! coefficient matrix
    E(F)_(σ,τ)=q^{a(σ,τ)} [X_σ^{q−1}X_τ] F,
where a(σ,τ) counts their agreements. The target is q^n times the identity. At σ≠τ the selected monomial uses different variables in some raw row, so it is absent in per(X^{∘q}). At σ=τ its coefficient is one. The rank is n! over every permitted K.

For one summand expand the determinant multilinearly in its output row slots. A row vector is the sum of its constant vector and at most b linear vector forms, one for each descriptor giving a raw row or column. If a variable lies at the intersection of two descriptors, split its coefficient arbitrarily between them or place it in just one. There are at most (b+1)^M descriptor choices across all slots. Only choices with exactly qn active linear slots contribute to the selected degree.

For one such choice mark n active slots for the τ edges and reserve the other active slots for the σ edges. An active raw-row descriptor j takes X_(j,τ(j)) or X_(j,σ(j)), according to its mark. A raw-column descriptor k uses X_(τ⁻¹(k),k) or X_(σ⁻¹(k),k). Require that the marked slots use each τ edge exactly once, and that the unmarked active slots use every σ edge exactly q−1 times. The two tests depend independently on τ and σ once the marked slots are fixed. This counts each ordered coefficient allocation once when σ and τ disagree on an edge. On an agreed edge each allocation has q indistinguishable copies among q distinct determinant row slots. It can designate any of those q slots as marked, giving exactly q^{a(σ,τ)} choices. Thus summing marked, accepted allocations gives exactly the scaled coefficient E, not merely an estimate.

In factor a, let k_a be the number of its marked slots and N_a its order. Extracting the term for an accepted assignment gives the determinant of the row coefficient vectors selected by σ or τ, with constant vectors in inactive slots. Expand by Laplace on its marked slots. There are binom(N_a,k_a) choices of output columns. For each, the determinant contribution is a product of a function of τ from the marked minor and a function of σ from the complementary minor. Both independent coverage indicators can be absorbed in these functions. Taking the q factors together gives rank at most the product of binom(N_a,k_a), which is at most binom(M,n) by Vandermonde because Σ k_a=n. There are binom(qn,n) marked choices per descriptor assignment. Summing rank bounds and comparing with rank n! proves the result. If M<qn for every summand the identity is impossible by degree. The rank constraints are closed, so the same bounds hold for limits of the stated model in coefficient space over the algebraic closure.

## Sharper special cases

1. Pure Hadamard factors. If A_a(X)=M_a∘X+C_a with unrelated fixed masks and affine offsets, only their homogeneous parts contribute. Each nontrivial alternating cycle in σ∪τ is assigned to the one factor that uses its τ orientation. A common edge can be designated to any factor and accounts for the agreed-edge scaling. Equivalently partition raw rows into labeled sets S_a such that σ(S_a)=τ(S_a), then sum over the common labeled column partition T_a. With S_a,T_a fixed, every mask product separates as a function of σ times a function of τ. The sign product also separates, as sign(σ)^{q−1}sign(τ). This gives per-summand rank at most Σ_(k₁+⋯+k_q=n) multinomial(n;k)^2 ≤ q^{2n}. For q=2 the bound is binom(2n,n), so m≥n!/binom(2n,n). This includes complex absolute squares on real variables, by taking the two masks and offsets to be coefficientwise conjugates. A polynomial equality on an open positive real orthant has the same bound. The earlier shared-mask n! bound remains sharper in that narrower model.
2. Order-n determinants with one raw row or column per slot and the usual bijective labeling on each chosen side. If all factors are row-local, specify the q-way row partition of the τ edges directly and split each determinant by Laplace. The rank bound is Σ_(k₁+⋯+k_q=n) multinomial(n;k)∏_a binom(n,k_a) ≤ q^n binom(qn,n). All-column follows by transposing. At q=2 the sum is Σ_k binom(n,k)^3 ≤8^n. For one row-local and one column-local factor at q=2, choose row subset S for the τ edges in the column-local factor and label its image T. Compatibility is σ(S)=τ(S)=T. Two Laplace factors then give rank at most Σ_k binom(n,k)^4 ≤16^n.
3. Boolean inputs for homogeneous order-n factors at q=2 with bijective row or column locality. Boolean folding recovers the selected union-support coefficient. Every support component in σ∪τ is one common edge or one alternating cycle. A single row-local or column-local homogeneous factor allocates exactly one slot per raw vertex of its respective side within that component. Thus the two factors contribute two edge occurrences to a common-edge component and exactly 2ℓ occurrences to a cycle with ℓ raw rows, ℓ raw columns, and 2ℓ supported edges. All cycle edges occur once. The unique exponents on an occupied support are therefore the selected ones. This permits arbitrary mixtures of whole-factor row or column locality at q=2 on the Boolean cube. Affine offsets and nonbijective raw slot assignments were only proved in the formal polynomial setting. This statement is outside characteristic two, where a determinant square directly equals the target.

The coefficient map may have factorial dimension, which is legitimate for a lower bound. It is not an algorithm for permanent. These bounds do not imply a lower bound for arbitrary arithmetic circuits, determinant circuits whose slots see global input, adaptive procedures, or approximate estimators.

## Reproduction and comparison

Run `node permanent-factorial-determinant-products-replay.js`. The exact replay compares independent symbolic coefficient extraction by determinant expansion with the descriptor and Laplace factorizations. It checks 150 mixed row/column cases, 35 wider raw-row neighborhood cases, 42 affine cases with mixed descriptors and enlarged determinants, 42 Boolean union-support coefficients verified by Möbius inversion, and 80 scaled target entries. This supplement is a written proof plus an exact replay, not a Lean formalization. See the [main dossier](README.md) for the sharp shared-mask result, the nonnegative higher-power result, and the separate Lean verification boundaries.

Primary antecedents checked include [Karmarkar–Karp–Lipton–Lovász–Luby’s real and complex estimator setting](https://doi.org/10.1137/0222021), [Barvinok’s complex unbiased estimator](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/barvinok/wp-content/uploads/sites/1434/2025/05/permanent.pdf), and [Sheshadri’s bound on the order of a single affine determinant in odd characteristic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40484). These serve different scopes. Focused indexed searches did not surface the same bound for sums of distinct locally supported determinant products. No novelty or priority claim is made.
