# Exact linear local-exchange barriers around Hamilton cycles

## Statement

For every m≥D≥3, let G(m,D) have bipartition A_i,B_i, i∈Z_m, and edges A_i–B_(i+s) for shifts s=0,1,…,D−1. It is simple D-regular. Put d=D−2. Distinguish the red Hamilton R using shifts d,d+1; all other shifts 0,…,d−1 are blue. For another spanning 2-factor F or Hamilton C define its distance to R as |E(F)\E(R)| or |E(C)\E(R)|, the number of new edges (equal to deletions). Then the two exact nearest-alternate distances are
  spanning 2-factor: ceil(m/(D−1));
  Hamilton: ceil((m−1)/(D−2)).
In particular, cubic examples have nearest Hamilton at m−1 of the 2m edges (within one of the universal maximum m for two Hamiltons in a cubic graph, since each vertex has at least one shared incidence). For even D, this graph is the ordinary circulant C_(2m)(1,3,…,D−1), via A_i↦2i and B_j↦2j−(D−1). In the quartic case D=4, the two blue shifts themselves are a Hamilton too, and the same sharp distances hold from either distinguished Hamilton.

Moreover for every n≥8, even or odd, there is a simple quartic graph on n vertices with two edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles, **each** at exact distance floor(n/4) from its nearest other Hamilton and at exact distance ceil(floor(n/2)/3) from its nearest different spanning 2-factor. Below is the even circulant proof and a degree-four odd-order graft. These are exact barriers to any uniformly sublinear exchange guarantee, not uniqueness assertions and not a proof of the full Sheehan conjecture.

## Degree balance and the winding constraint

Red order is A_i→B_(i+d+1)→A_(i+1), hence one cycle. For any different spanning 2-factor F, let E^- be deleted red edges and E^+ be added blue edges. At each vertex counts in E^-,E^+ agree because both original and changed factors have degree two. Match their incidences arbitrarily at every vertex: following an edge then its paired opposite-color incidence decomposes this difference into closed alternating edge-distinct trails. A trail may repeat vertices. This standard k-opt decomposition is also described in Bonnet–Iwata–Jansen–Kowalik, [Fine-Grained Complexity of k-OPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09325), §2; the graph/winding arguments here are separate, with no priority claim.

Orient each trail so its deleted red edges go A→B and its added blue edges B→A. This is possible by bipartition and alternation; reverse direction if needed. Each two-step advances its A-sheet by the integer r−b with r∈{d,d+1} and b∈[0,d−1], hence by a strictly positive integer between 1 and d+1. Closing modulo m forces advance equal to a positive multiple of m. If k new edges total, summing across trails gives k(d+1)≥m, and ≥2m if total winding at least two. This proves the 2-factor lower bound directly, without connectivity.

## Component count for one winding

Suppose total winding one, so there is a single trail. Its chosen A-sheet indices are strictly increasing cyclically around Z_m, returning only at the end: lifts progress positively with total m. Hence its k deleted-red cuts occur in this same order on R. At each chosen A_i one deletes exactly one red edge, either high red r=d+1 outgoing from A, or low red r=d incoming to A. The red order of these cuts is unchanged if some adjacent cuts share a B vertex; that remaining segment is simply a singleton. Contract each retained red path segment P_i from just after cut i to just before cut i+1 to a single vertex i, indices cyclic mod k. Each added edge joins the B endpoint of cut i to the A endpoint of next cut i+1. Therefore on segments its endpoints are
  i−[cut i is low],   i+[cut i+1 is low].
That graph records components exactly even if loops or parallel edges appear after contraction. The four type cases are: high/high yields loop at i; high/low edge i–(i+1); low/high edge (i−1)–i; low/low edge (i−1)–(i+1).

If no cut is high, this is the step-two cycle graph on k indices (allowing loops for k=2), with gcd(k,2) components. If at least one cut is high, partition indices into cyclic intervals from each high cut's index to just before the next. No edge crosses these boundaries by the four cases above. Each such interval is connected: length one has a loop, length two gets its boundary edge, and for length ≥3 the inner jump-two edges plus the first boundary step-one edge connect both parities. Thus the number of components is exactly the number of high cuts. In particular, a Hamilton from a one-winding difference can use at most one high cut.

It follows that a Hamilton one-winding move has m≤dk+1, since all low steps are ≤d and at most one high step is ≤d+1. A move with winding at least two has k≥ceil(2m/(d+1))≥ceil((m−1)/d); the underlying strict inequality follows from 2dm−(d+1)(m−1)=(d−1)m+d+1>0. This proves the Hamilton lower bound for every possible transform, not just sequential moves assumed up front.

## Explicit sharp witnesses

A marked sequence of moves (r,b) traces A_i→B_(i+r)→A_(i+r−b). Once it closes, replace along that alternate sequence. To attain the Hamilton bound k=ceil((m−1)/d), let ρ=kd+1−m ∈[0,d−1]. Begin with (d+1,0), take k−2 instances of (d,0), finish with (d,ρ). Its total advance is m, with exactly one high cut. A-sheet labels are distinct by monotone progress. All earlier B labels are the next A sheet, lying in [d+1,m−1], and the last B label is ρ≤d−1, so all B labels are distinct too. Therefore a literal alternating cycle yields a spanning 2-factor; its one high cut makes it connected by the previous lemma.

To attain the 2-factor bound k=ceil(m/(d+1)), let ρ=k(d+1)−m∈[0,d]. Use k−1 instances of (d+1,0) then (d+1,ρ) when ρ≤d−1, otherwise (d,d−1) (one final step). Again total advance is m, both bipartition lists are distinct, and replacement is a valid spanning 2-factor at the announced distance.

For D=4, the reflection A_i↦A_(-i), B_j↦B_(3−j) swaps shifts by s↦3−s, carrying red Hamilton and blue Hamilton to each other. The even quartic statements follow.

## Odd-order quartic graft, including the 2-factor triangle case

For m≥4 remove red edge e=A_0–B_2 and blue edge f=A_1–B_1, replacing both with two-edge paths through the SAME fresh vertex O. Four endpoints distinct, so the graph is simple quartic and the subdivided red/blue Hamiltons remain disjoint as edge sets. Its order is 2m+1. All four possible A–B endpoint choices through O suppress to actual base edges: e, f, and cross edges A_0–B_1 and A_1–B_2 (both blue shift 1). For a Hamilton, O's two neighbors must be in opposite bipartition sets because the full remaining path has odd length. Suppress O to a base Hamilton. For a spanning 2-factor, all components not through O are bipartite even cycles and the total order is odd, so the O-component is odd, again with opposite-part neighbors. Suppression yields a spanning base 2-factor, allowing a doubled cross edge in the exceptional case of a mixed triangle through O.

In either comparison, suppression cannot increase the new-edge distance to the corresponding base red or blue reference: if two O spokes of the reference color are used, reinstall the reference e or f; if one is used, at most one new edge replaces its other-color spoke; if none, at most one new edge replaces two other-color spokes. A suppressed factor equals the base red (or blue) precisely if the removed graft edge of that color is restored, since otherwise that edge is absent; its endpoints then force exactly the original subdivided route. Hence a genuinely different factor never suppresses to the distinguished reference of its comparison.

A Hamilton suppression never creates a duplicate: its residual path spans all 2m vertices. Apply even lower bounds. For a 2-factor, a duplicate can only be a cross edge of blue shift 1, because the other two candidates e,f were deleted. Compare multiplicities. Relative to red, both blue copies are ordinary added blue steps; the alternating-walk argument did not require distinct vertices or distinct physical edges across copies. Relative to blue, the one added extra copy of cross edge c is a rogue blue-shift-1 edge rather than red. In the oriented alternating decomposition that uses removed reference blue A→B and added edges B→A, the edge paired into its B endpoint must be removed blue shift 0: the only shift-1 blue at that endpoint is c itself, which is not removed (one copy stayed). Hence that rogue two-step decreases the sheet by exactly 1, while all normal steps decrease by 1,2,3. The same winding lower bound applies. This handles the triangle case without treating a multigraph as a simple graph.

For sharpness translate each even witness around its sheets so it retains the reference-colored graft edge and avoids the other deleted edge; then subdivide the retained edge via O. For red Hamilton witness, at most k−1 lower red cuts can forbid a translation by e, and at most k shift-0 blue additions can forbid one by f, with 2k−1<m. For red 2-factor witness, at most one lower red cut and at most k blue shift-0 additions give 1+k<m. For blue witnesses reflect first: f counts as a high red cut in the preimage, and e counts as a blue shift-1 addition. The Hamilton preimage uses one high cut and at most one blue shift-1 addition, so at most 2 forbidden translations; the 2-factor version at most k+1<m. Thus all exact distances achieved on odd orders too. Since floor((2m+1)/4)=floor(m/2), the theorem has the same floor form for all n≥8.

## Literal verification and limits

Run `node sheehan-linear-hamilton-exchange-replay.js`. It constructs literal vertex/edge sets, tests degrees and exact exchange counts, and checks connectedness of all Hamilton witnesses. It checks 2,032 sharp witnesses across m=4..257, for even/odd quartic orders, both distinguished colors and both metrics. It exhausts all spanning 2-factors on these colored quartic graphs for n=8..19, computing minima for both references and counting Hamiltons independently. For general even orders, D=3..13, m≤127 passes 2,640 sharp literal witnesses; all spanning 2-factors enumerated additionally for D=3..5, D≤m≤8 match both proved minima. These are exact graph tests alongside a uniform paper proof. Neither the graph theorem nor the full Sheehan conjecture is formalized. The construction forbids merely bounded or even sublinear exchange from a given Hamilton as a universal proof strategy. There is no optimality claim over all graphs. [Thomassen and Zamfirescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06347) study quartic graphs with few Hamilton cycles; the quantity here is distance from a specified cycle rather than the number of cycles. No algorithmic running-time conclusion is asserted.

`HamiltonLinearExchangeArithmetic.lean` checks only the three arithmetic ingredients `winding_lower`, `one_high_step_budget`, and `multiple_windings_gap` (Lean 4.34.1, mathlib `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`). From the repository Lean project, run `lake env lean ../research/dossiers/hamilton-linear-exchange/HamiltonLinearExchangeArithmetic.lean`. The winding decomposition, component analysis, witnesses, and odd graft are proved only on paper.
