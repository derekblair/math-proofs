# Independent all-odd class-five review

**Verdict:** No proof blocker in the checked Lean scope. For a finite group of odd order and class **at most five**, and any two nonidentity rim elements, `RibbonP3.odd_class5` proves a simple C4, C8, C16, C32 or C64 in the single-matching bi-Cayley graph. Oddness makes both nonidentity rim elements non-involutions, so the resulting graph is cubic. There is no coprime-to-three condition, no whole-group generation or connectedness hypothesis, no lower bound on individual element orders beyond oddness and nonidentity, and no claim about all cubic graphs or general bipartite Haar graphs.

The wrapper passes to the subgroup generated by the two elements and uses subgroup order divisibility. In that subgroup, project to class two and distinguish the order of the commutator c:
- If the projection is trivial, the quotient is abelian by generation; γ2=γ3 and stabilization to the assumed γ6=1 implies γ2=1.
- If its order is three, work in the class-three quotient H. Its third layer is central and generated by u=[c,x] and v=[c,y]. Since c³ belongs to this central layer, both u³ and v³ are one. If both u and v are trivial, stabilization reduces the original subgroup to class at most two. If only v is nontrivial, swap the two ports (and reflect the rim bit on the final graph map). The character on H's third layer then sends u to 1 in F3 by linear dual separation; all nine values of q=χ(v), t=χ(c³) are covered.
- Otherwise the nontrivial projected commutator has odd order other than three, so powers 1 through 4 do not hit the identity. The earlier class-five bridge applies exactly under that local condition.

In the order-three branch, equality in the original graph gives equality in the class-three projection and then equality of the class-two commutator residues. Commutating with the projected generators recovers the i,j residues modulo three. The integer row certificate now promotes those congruences to equality of the actual integer i,j coordinates for the specific paths; it does **not** assume an abelianization order window. The separately mentioned exponent range [-1,2] is a checked range of the collector's integer input rows, not a group-order condition.
