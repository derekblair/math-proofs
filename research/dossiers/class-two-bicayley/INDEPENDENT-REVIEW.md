# Independent ribbon theorem review

**Green, with scope and attribution boundaries.** `Ribbon.class2_pair` and `RibbonSharp.sharp` replay logs list only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. No source placeholders or native-decide trust axioms found. Separate exact Python checker reran successfully.

- Adj is precisely the undirected right-multiplier single-matching bi-Cayley relation: rim 0 has edges for a and a⁻¹, rim 1 for b and b⁻¹, and spokes preserve the group coordinate. The cubic case requires both a²≠1 and b²≠1. Lean needs only a≠1 and b²≠1, so it also covers a potentially degree-two first rim. Neither global class two, odd order, finite order nor generation is used: the relation ab=bac with c commuting with the pair is sufficient. In the cubic case the graph is connected iff a,b generate G; no connectivity premise is needed. A sole nonidentity matching label can be renormalized to 1 by relabeling the second copy, conjugating its multiplier; this is a mathematical corollary, not a separately checked transport theorem here.
- Cycle uses an injective Fin n sequence with modular successor, so lengths 4,8,16 are simple cycles, not necessarily induced. Commuting pairs give rectangle or shared rim edge. For the noncommuting eight group labels, conjugation recovers the two small exponents; when c²=1, the exceptional ambiguity requires b²c=1, which forces the b-rim element to have order four given b²≠1. Otherwise sixteen distinct vertices pass the alternating-rim and matching steps.
- Sharp means the dyadic bound 16, **not** girth 16. The explicit 27-element upper-unitriangular group over F3 gives a 54-vertex example with triangles. Every simple candidate translates to one of two roots and has one of three port labels at each step; both roots and all port words of lengths four/eight are exhaustively kernel-checked. Positive C16 is independent of the negative census.
- The underlying eight-letter ordinary Cayley identity is `[a,b][a,b⁻¹]` under the aba⁻¹b⁻¹ commutator convention, or a commutator of b and an a-conjugate of b up to inversion/cyclic shift. Its closure is standard central-commutator algebra and not the new claim. [Burkhart](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05060) treats ordinary Cayley graphs (including the finite-nilpotent corollary); his construction also uses short commutator cycles, but no direct theorem for the single-matching lifts follows. The classical commuting rectangle for cyclic I-graphs overlaps the commuting subcase; see [Klobas–Krnc](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2024.11.034).
- The noncommuting theorem also properly includes non-Cayley graphs: take G=UT3(F3)×C5 and generators a=(a_H,1), b=(b_H,z) for z generating C5. They generate G because b has order 15 with independent powers for its factors. The first rim consists of triangles, the second of 15-cycles; no triangle crosses the matching (two spokes would require a nonloop rim edge to preserve the group coordinate). Thus only first-rim vertices lie on a triangle, making the graph non-vertex-transitive, hence not Cayley. This is a scope example, not the sharpness example.
