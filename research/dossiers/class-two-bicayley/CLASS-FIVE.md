# The nilpotency-class-five extension

The same standard single-matching bi-Cayley construction uses separate right
rim generators $x,y$ and one identity matching between the two copies.
Here $x,y\ne1$; to call the underlying simple graph cubic they must not
be involutions. The Lean group assumption LS(G,5)=bottom says the sixth
lower-central term vanishes.

**Finite theorem.** For groups of odd order not divisible by three and of
nilpotency class at most five, the graph contains a simple cycle of length
4, 8, 16, 32, or 64. The local theorem needs only that the image of the
basic commutator in the class-two quotient have no power 1, 2, 3, or 4
equal to the identity; that local branch gives 32 or 64 without a global
finite or odd hypothesis. The finite wrapper restricts to the subgroup
generated by the two rim generators. If the basic commutator becomes
trivial in its class-two quotient, the generating pair commutes: the first
two derived lower-central terms agree and nilpotence collapses them.
The proof does not cover all odd-order groups at class five. It gives no
claim about the full Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture.

## Two linked words

The 32-edge word from the class-four argument need not close in class
five. In the class-five group it is the fourth power of the evaluation of
a 26-edge word W; W's evaluation is central. The exact word law is
proved in Ribbon5LawAlgebra.lean using finite Schreier rewrites and
commutator algebra, not by assuming formal Magnus coefficients determine
the calculation. The 26-edge word is 12002021212120200212120212; the
six-edge connector T is 020021, with digits denoting forward rim, reverse
rim and spoke. If W evaluates to the identity, the 32-edge word closes.
Otherwise traverse W, T, reverse W and reverse T. Centrality closes
this 64-edge word.

Simplicity comes from the checked class-two projection chart. The two
rim exponents and the basic commutator exponent separate every same-side
pair by a nonzero gap of magnitude at most four, except exactly seven
pairs in the 64-edge word. Those pairs are the two traversals of the
connector, whose actual group labels differ by the nonidentity central
value of W. This is why the ambient condition excludes elements of
order three in the relevant quotient: some chart separation tests
really use gap three. The finite bound uses quotient group order
divisibility, not a claim that torsion-free groups have torsion-free
lower-central quotients.

The chart generator is untrusted. Its output is checked by recurrence,
closure and all distinct-position separation obligations in Lean.
Existing merged Haar lemmas supply generic collection and centrality,
not statements about these graphs. Earlier Cayley and Haar graph
prior art does not match the single-matching model. No priority is asserted.

## Exact experimental sharpness check, not fully Lean

A separate exact enumeration in UT6(F17), with first superdiagonals
(4,4,4,10,7) and (12,12,9,12,2) and all other above-diagonal entries zero,
finds a simple 64-cycle but no 4-, 8-, 16- or 32-cycle. It checks both
root rims using meet-in-the-middle and exact matrix entries; left
translation transfers to every vertex. The ambient group is nilpotent
of class five. The explicit positive graph and generating pair also
have local Lean proofs, but the complete no-short-cycle census has not
been replayed by the Lean kernel. Therefore this draft does not claim
fully formalized class-five sharpness. Replay the separate check with
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 check_class5_ut6.cpp -o check5 and ./check5.
