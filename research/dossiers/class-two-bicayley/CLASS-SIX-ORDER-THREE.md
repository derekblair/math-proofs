# The dyadic 64 bound in class six at all odd orders

For finite groups of odd order and nilpotency class at most six,
any nonidentity pair of generators on the two rims of the
single-matching bi-Cayley graph yields a simple cycle of length
4, 8, 16, 32 or 64. This removes the restriction that 3 not divide
the group order from the earlier class-six theorem.

In the remaining characteristic-three branch, consider the
class-three quotient. The basic commutator has order 3 in its
class-two quotient and a normalized F3 character of the third term
can be chosen with value 1 on the first third-layer commutator,
swapping the rims if necessary. As in the class-five proof, the
full case where both third-layer commutators vanish reduces to
class two in the subgroup generated by the rim pair.

For the 64-step word of the class-six proof, the x-exponents stay
in [0,2] and the y-exponents in [-2,0]. Thus agreement modulo 3
implies exact agreement of the integer exponents for this chart;
there is no restriction on the order of x or y in the abelianization.
Writing q,t for the character values on v and c³ respectively,
all six rows where (q+t) mod 3 ≠ 2 leave no equalities other than
the same nine exact free-conjugacy pairs. In the other three rows,
replacing x with x⁻¹ leaves the graph unchanged. In the class-three
quotient c'=c⁻¹u, u'=u, v'=v⁻¹ and (c')³=(c³)⁻¹, so the same
character gives (-q,-t), one of the certified rows. If the path
residue vanishes the first 32 edges close and are simple; otherwise
all nine pairs separate and the full 64-step word is simple.

The class-three collection, inversion identities, characters,
finite comparisons and final finite-group theorem have kernel
proofs. The class-seven bound in this dossier still assumes order
prime to 6. No claim is made about the full Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture.
