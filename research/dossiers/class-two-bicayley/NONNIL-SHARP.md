# Sharpness for genuinely nonnilpotent finite odd metabelian ribbons

Let H be the existing checked sharp UT4(F11) group with ports a,b. Let J be the order-21 affine group on F7×C3 with product (u,i)(v,j)=(u+2^i v,i+j). Use t=(0,1), l=(1,0). Take the subgroup S generated by x=(a,t) and y=(b,l) in H×J; use x,y as the ports. We do not need S to equal the ambient product or to know its precise order.

The J projection of S is onto: every element (u,i) has the exact normal form l^(u.val)t^(i.val). J is metabelian because the translation kernel of projection to C3 is abelian. Its lower central series never becomes trivial: l≠1 and [l⁻¹,t]=l, using [u,v]=uvu⁻¹v⁻¹. Both identities are kernel checked; induction puts l in every LS J n. By surjectivity and preservation of lower central series, S cannot be nilpotent either. H is metabelian from the existing checked class-three construction. Products and subgroups preserve metabelianity and subgroup order divides the finite odd ambient cardinal. The ribbon on S is connected: represent any group element by a port word in x± and y±, using two matching edges for each y-rim move to return to the x side, and one final matching edge if desired.

The H projection preserves all three oriented ports and is injective on every neighbor set, because the corresponding three H port moves are distinct; it need not be injective globally. Thus an actual simple cycle projects to a closed walk with no immediate backtracking, including at the seam. Its opposite halves are reduced, have distinct first ports and meet at the same midpoint. Translate the projected start to the identity using the left group action. For half-length h=2,4,8 this contradicts the checked disjoint endpoint sets of reduced half-walks from the old UT4 certificate. Neither projection of simplicity nor a global injection has been assumed. The new all-metabelian theorem gives a simple 32-cycle once the three shorter dyadic lengths are excluded. Sharpness is genuinely within the nonnilpotent subfamily, not inherited just from an example outside that subfamily. No minimum-order assertion.
