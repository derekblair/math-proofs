# Independent affine-port certificate audit

**Verdict:** No mathematical blocker found for odd-characteristic fields with the prescribed affine ports x=(a,0), y=(b,1), a≠1, and a,b of finite odd multiplicative order. Moreover the C32 lifts to any metabelian group with that affine port image. This is an exact finite algebraic certificate, not Lean or a result for all nonsplit metabelian pairs. It does not need the original group or the field to be finite, but the stated condition on port multipliers matters.

**Word closure and lifting.** Interpret the rim-labeled moves as a free word in X,Y, ignoring spokes except for switching the interpretation of each move. Track its based lift to the free abelian cover of the two-petal bouquet. The cover has integer lattice vertices and oriented X- and Y-edges; its fundamental group is F' for F free on X,Y. If exponent sums vanish, the lifted path is closed. Its first homology class is exactly its signed edge-count chain (the graph has no 2-cells). Thus zero signed edge flow implies membership in [F',F']=F''. The code records lower endpoints for inverse edges, with the correct sign and the rim specifying which coordinate increments. Independently confirmed that the base word and all seven fallback words have length 32, even spoke count, zero exponent sums and zero net flow on every directed lattice edge. Every evaluation of such a word in an actual metabelian group is the identity. Given a homomorphism to the affine group with the prescribed port images, the rim bit and affine coordinate at each of the first 32 actual positions are their simulated prefix images. Therefore affine injectivity implies actual injectivity. It is not enough to lift an arbitrary cycle in a quotient, but the F'' closure property supplies exactly the missing step here.

**Affine computation and reduction.** For right multiplication with the standard affine composition rule, the multiplier is a^i b^j. A forward Y-step adds that current multiplier; a reverse Y-step subtracts the multiplier after multiplying by b⁻¹. This gives the recorded Laurent translations. A same-rim collision must simultaneously have multiplier relation a^i b^j=1 and zero translation difference. For a nonzero base-word slope, the code checks gcd(|i|,|j|)≤2; the order of a^i b^j is odd, so saturation by this gcd is justified. A primitive relation with j=0 forces a=1 and is excluded. Write the remaining primitive slope (u,v) with v>0. If sv−tu=1, choosing the single parameter z=a^s b^t gives a=z^v, b=z^(−u); z is odd-order and z≠1. For zero slope the two-variable Laurent polynomial is tested directly. Shifting by monomials does not change vanishing on nonzero labels. Factorization uses Z-polynomial content, with only powers of two discarded. Irrelevant factors are constants/nonzero monomials, a−1, t−1 in their respective cases, or divisors of a verified t^n+1 (of a+1,b+1 in the zero-slope case). An odd-order nonzero element in odd characteristic cannot have a power equal to −1. Therefore the 14 base obstructions are sufficient across every eligible odd characteristic, not merely over Q.

**Resultants and degree drops.** For integer polynomials f,P, the resultant is an integral polynomial combination of f and P (Sylvester determinant/adjugate). Thus a common root after reduction in any field of characteristic p forces p to divide that integer, even if one or both degrees drop modulo p or P reduces to zero. This implication needs no monic or leading-coefficient-preservation hypothesis. A zero resultant is treated as unrestricted; gcd(0,R)=|R|, and two unrestricted results are not used to certify that candidate. For each fallback collision, the additional multiplier condition is either unrestricted or a t^m−1 test after removing powers of two; dropping exponent one is justified by t≠1. Taking gcd of the two resultant conditions and then the union over pairs covers every characteristic in which that fallback could fail. In each exceptional prime the nonzero reduced f is factored in F_p[t] with actual reduced degree. Factoring by irreducibles handles all roots in arbitrary extensions; testing row polynomials by remainder is exact. For any nonzero root of an irreducible of degree e, multiplicative order divides p^e−1 and can be computed by factor-stripping; conjugate roots have the same order. A zero root is not eligible (recommend explicitly skip the factor t in future versions, even though it cannot be missed as an eligible case here).

**Reproduction/status.** Independently reran universal.py: 14 slope-factor cases, no failures, seven fallback words total plus the base word. Exceptional prime candidate sets include 3,5,7; the final admissible exception rows were the two linear order-three factors in characteristic seven. The prime-field sweeps by themselves do not prove field uniformity; the integral resultants plus finite-field factor/remainder checks are what extend the certificate to all eligible extensions. A future formal release should freeze/export the compact witness table and prove or check the polynomial identities and divisibility under explicit assumptions rather than rely on heuristic search or opaque SymPy output.
