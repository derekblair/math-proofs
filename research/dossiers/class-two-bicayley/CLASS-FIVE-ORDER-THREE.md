# Characteristic-three ribbon C32/C64 construction

This section concerns the actual **single-matching ribbon** graph. A vertex is
(side,g), with right steps x or x⁻¹ on the first rim and y or y⁻¹ on the
second rim, and a spoke switches sides without changing g. Alphabet 0/1/2
means forward / reverse / spoke. This is not the ordinary bipartite Haar
graph modeled by the separate unprefixed analysis scripts here.

## The parameter and words

Let G be generated by x,y, nilpotency class at most five; γᵢ denotes the
usual lower central series with γ₁=G. Suppose c=[x⁻¹,y⁻¹] has order 3 in
γ₂/γ₃. The graded layers γᵢ/γᵢ₊₁ are elementary abelian 3-groups for
i≥2: degree two is generated by c and the higher layers are generated by
its repeated brackets with x,y, using graded multilinearity. In particular
if G has exact class≥3, swap x,y if needed to make
u=[c,x]≠1 in γ₃/γ₄; the other generator is v=[c,y].
Choose an F3 character λ on that layer with λ(u)=1. Write q=λ(v) and
t=λ(c³), arbitrary in F3.

For t=0,1 use
 A = 00200212112120020021121200212112
 B = 20020021121200212112002002121121.
For t=2 use
 A'= 02002121121200200211212002121120
 B'= 20021211200200212112120020021121.

These are length-32 words starting and ending on the first rim. Relative to
A, the others are cyclic rotations by respectively 12,1,23 edges; the
rotated-away prefix has even spoke count in each case.

## Universal endpoint closure

In the convention [a,b]=aba⁻¹b⁻¹, write C=[x,y], U=[x,C], H=[x,y²].
Free reduction gives
   endpoint(A) = x [H,x⁻¹ H⁻¹ x] x⁻¹.
One way to see the free factorization is to rotate after the first x edge
in the signed generator word. The remainder is the Wicks commutator
I J K I⁻¹ J⁻¹ K⁻¹ = [I J,K I⁻¹], for
I=x y² x⁻¹, J=y⁻², K=x⁻¹ y² x. Here I J=H and
K I⁻¹=(x⁻¹ H⁻¹ x)H⁻¹; the terminal H⁻¹ does not affect [H,–].

By weight filtration, H≡C² modulo γ₃. The product
(x⁻¹ H⁻¹ x) H lies in γ₃ and is congruent to [x,H] modulo γ₄ (it is a
conjugate of [x,H]). Fourth layers commute with γ₂, γ₃ with itself, and
degree-(2,3) pairings are central and bilinear in this class. Hence
   [H,x⁻¹ H⁻¹ x] = [H,[x,H]] = [C,[x,C]]⁴.
Thus the common group endpoint T of all four words is central; shifts
B,A',B' only conjugate it. If T=1, the A/A' word itself is a simple C32
by the separation below; otherwise A followed by reverse B, or A'
followed by reverse B', is a closed 64-walk.

## Exact separation reduction

Modulo γ₄, collect each vertex into y^j x^i c^k u^l v^m with integer
coefficients, and include the actual rim. The data/recurrence follow
from x*y=y*x*c, c*x=x*c*u, c*y=y*c*v with u,v central. For example,
x^i y = y x^i c^i u^{i(i−1)/2}. Spokes only change the rim.

Any equality of two same-rim group vertices, projected further to class
two, implies the differences of i and j are multiples of 3: commuting
with x and y reveals the corresponding powers of the order-3 c.
The finite row recurrence has **no nonzero same-rim (i,j) difference
divisible by 3 in both coordinates** on either selected pair. Consequently
equal vertices have exactly the same integer i,j (no abelianization
order-window assumption). Then k≡k' (mod 3) by canceling x,y in the
class-two quotient. Finally canceling the common i,j and low residue of k,
and applying λ to the remaining central class-three difference, yields
equality of
   l + q m + t floor(k/3) (mod 3).

Using both paths' vertices as their union, taking A indices 0..32 and
B indices 1..31 (so the shared endpoints are counted once), an exact
finite enumeration of these keys leaves only:
- t=0,1: (A0,A32), (A1,B31), and (Ai,B_(32−i)) for i=27..31.
- t=2: (A'0,A'32) and (A'i,B'_(32−i)) for i=1..6.

In every indicated pair across paths, the ratio of the two actual
free-group prefixes is a conjugate of the endpoint of A or its inverse.
This is stronger than a fifth-degree signature: direct free reduction
verifies all 12 equalities (see generated RibbonP3Identity). Hence if
T≠1 they are all separated in the actual group. If T=1 the only projected
repeat on the *single* A or A' path is its permitted closing repeat,
which gives the C32 alternative.

This establishes a paper proof with an exact finite collision certificate
for the entire cbar-order-three case of the class-five ribbon construction,
given the standard class-at-most-two case when γ₃=1. It is not a claim
about bipartite Haar graphs or all cbar orders. The universal group
bridge is kernel-checked in Lean as `RibbonP3.local_p3`; see below.

## Separate structural restriction (not needed for the path pair)

If G/γ₂ has exponent three and γ₃/γ₄≠0, then in G/γ₄ one has c³=1,
and x³,y³∈γ₃. CubeClassThree.lean verifies the algebra from
x³=C^a R, y³=C^b S (R,S central, a,b in {0,1,2}), third commutators
central of exponent 3 and at least one nontrivial. With U=[x,C] and
V=[C,y], one has U^a=V^b=1 and C³=[x³,y]=V^a=[x,y³]=U^b.
Any nonzero a or b would kill both U,V; thus a=b=0 and C³=1.

Returning to class five, C³∈γ₄ gives U³,V³∈γ₅ via Hall cube collection
and terminal central exponent 3. Then every basic weight-four commutator
has cube one, e.g. [U,x]³=[U³,x]=1; conjugates and γ₅ also have
cube one and γ₄ is abelian. Further, dim_F3 γ₄/γ₅≤2 in this abelian
exponent-three case. Otherwise weight-four A=[x,U], B=[y,U] and
D=[y,V] would be independent, with [x,V] agreeing with a sign of B
in the graded quotient by Jacobi. The identities [x,x³]=[y,y³]=1
force the projections of x³,y³ in γ₃/γ₄ to vanish. Then mod γ₅ the
class-four Hall expansion gives [x³,y]=A C³=1 and
[x,y³]=D⁻¹ C³=1, contradicting independence of A,D.

## Complete pinned Lean theorem

`RibbonP3.odd_class5` in `RibbonP3Odd.lean` proves that, in a finite odd-order
group of nilpotency class at most five, any nonidentity rim pair produces a
simple cycle of length 4, 8, 16, 32, or 64 in the actual single-matching
ribbon. Generation of the whole ambient group is not assumed. This is a
family theorem, not a proof of the general Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture.

- `RibbonP3Structure.L2_eq_two` shows the central third layer in the
  class-three quotient equals the closure of the two port commutators:
  quotienting by their closure makes the commutator of the generating
  pair central, so the quotient is class two. `cube_L2` derives the
  third layer's exponent three from centrality of c³, and `char_exists`
  constructs the normalized F3 character by linear dual separation.
- `RibbonP3Endpoint.endpoint_all` proves the exact common central endpoint
  without needing the optional fourth-power identity in the paper proof:
  the Wicks factor equals a nested commutator of layers 2 and 3, belongs
  to layer 5, and is consequently central.
- `RibbonP3Transport.projected_wordAt`, `RibbonP3KeySound.key_sound`,
  `RibbonP3Glue.at64` and `RibbonP3Cycle.ribbon_cycle` connect the exact
  finite table to genuine simple cycles. The proof enumerates all nine
  char rows in the kernel and checks all twelve residual identities.
- `RibbonP3.local_p3` assembles the characteristic-three branch.
  `RibbonP3.generated_odd` treats trivial projected commutator, order 3
  (including generator swap and third-layer drop), and every other odd
  order (using the earlier 1–4 exponent-separation branch). The final
  theorem restricts to the generated pair subgroup and embeds the cycle.
