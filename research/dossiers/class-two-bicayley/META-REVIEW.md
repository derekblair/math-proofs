# Independent unbounded-nilpotency ribbon audit

**Verdict:** Both upper theorems and the metabelian sharpness statement are sound in their stated Lean scope. These are for the one-matching bi-Cayley graph, not arbitrary cubic graphs or three-cross-matching Haar graphs. For the global results take any finite odd-order nilpotent G and any two nonidentity rim elements; they need not generate G. This gives a simple cubic graph because a nonidentity element of odd order cannot equal its inverse. “At most 32/64” refers to a power-of-two cycle, not a statement of ordinary girth or all smaller lengths.

**Metabelian local and global results.** In `RibbonMeta.local_C32` the exact condition is C⁴≠1 for C the image of [x⁻¹,y⁻¹] in G/γ3. No finiteness, nilpotence or generation is needed. In the finite odd case, C≠1 implies C² and C⁴ are nontrivial. Under C⁴≠1, split off C of order three; every other case avoids all of the first four powers, even when the order is infinite. The low chart's first 32-step word closes in a metabelian group because its exact residual is a conjugate of a commutator of two derived elements. The order-three word has exact endpoint x⁻¹ trace(y,x,A) x, trivial because its Wicks commutator factors are derived elements. Transport to the class-two quotient, commuting against the two projected ports and using order three gives the i,j congruences; the checked chart windows upgrade them to integer equalities, then cancellation gives the k congruence. This does not impose abelianization order conditions. In the global theorem, restrict to the pair subgroup first. If C=1 its class-two quotient is abelian by generation, so γ2=γ3 and nilpotence gives γ2=1; the commuting-rim fallback yields C4/C8/C16, else the local result gives C32. Don't drop global nilpotence for the C=1 branch, or suggest the local hypothesis is automatically necessary for C32.

**Derived subgroup class≤2 extension.** The headline hypothesis is exactly that the second-index lower central subgroup of [G,G] is trivial (class at most two in conventional γ-indexing). This implies commutators of derived elements centralize that derived subgroup; no claim of centrality in the entire group. R is a conjugate of such a commutator and U lies in the derived subgroup, so R commutes with U. As R=UV, canceling U gives VU=UV, exactly the condition for the 64 trace to close. The previous certificate separates projected mates by actual nontrivial R; when R=1 it instead gives a 32-cycle. The exact order-three case uses the checked character chart and the inversion argument from the class-six work. Pair subgroup restriction preserves the derived-class condition. Nilpotence is used without any bounded class, and oddness does not exclude 3-divisibility. The extension yields C4/C8/C16/C32/C64 but does not establish 64 is sharp.

**Sharpness and verification.** The imported UT4(F11) certificate is genuinely Lean-checked for absence of C4/C8/C16 and presence of C32, with class exactly three (in separate `class_exact`) and odd order; `meta_of_class_three` proves metabelianity rather than presuming it. The imported connected-component restriction preserves the cycle spectrum, so a connected cubic sharp witness is also defensible. Neither the upper theorem nor the sharp wrapper relies on the Python discovery script. Headline and sharpness logs list only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. This source/log audit is not an independent clean package rebuild. Prior Cayley results are not automatically results for arbitrary one-matching graphs; priority remains a separate question.
