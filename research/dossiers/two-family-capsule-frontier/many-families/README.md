# Exact uniform-threshold capsule frontier for r independent KEM families

A mathematical extension of the local two-family black-box envelope model, not a new KEM, fixed-wire threshold ML-KEM, active/CCA-safe envelope, or deployment claim. “Family disclosure” is an explicit abstract leak of all private KEM keys of that family, not an assertion that real cryptanalytic failures are independent.

## Model and two exact optima

Let n≥1 be the number of endpoint domains, r≥1 the number of primitive families, c≥0 the allowed exposed endpoints, and f≥0 the allowed endpoint outages. Recovery must work after any f or fewer outages with surviving endpoints acting honestly. Privacy must hold when all private keys of any r−1 families and those of any c or fewer endpoints are disclosed. The survivor is the family not designated for wholesale disclosure, even if exposed endpoints happen to contain all its keys in a proposed layout. Otherwise that insecurity would be excluded by definition. The main theorem considers c,f<n; if c≥n all endpoint keys can be disclosed, and if f≥n all endpoints may be unavailable, so the stipulated model is infeasible. The aggregator who recovers the secret must remain uncompromised. Each black-box capsule is labeled by one family and one endpoint and is available only via that labeled secret key; no other secret channel, recovery procedure requiring a new sender interaction, live ciphertext methods, cross-family capsule or implicit disjointness assumption is admitted. Independent honest KEM key generation and encapsulation are used, with correlated share plaintexts permitted. Choose a field with enough distinct nonzero Shamir sites for the respective layout, and matching independent uniform masks in the ideal hybrid. The capsule-count model does not constrain field, encoding or key sizes to a specific standard. Threat and ciphertext caveats are discussed below.

Set a=c+f+1. Under arbitrary access policies within this black-box model, the optimal number is **r·a**, feasible exactly when n≥a. If any one family reaches only c+f or fewer distinct endpoint domains, choose outages and exposed endpoints covering that support and disclose all other families. The nonpublic inputs to the required honest recovery are then all inside the permitted observer view, contradicting privacy. Thus each family needs a separate distinct-domain support of size at least a, regardless of multiplicities or nonlinear packing/recovery. Achieve the bound by splitting the secret as a sum of r offsets, chosen jointly uniformly subject to that sum. Share each offset separately with threshold c+1 among a endpoints of its family and mask each share by a distinct independently encapsulated key. Supports may overlap completely between families. Outages leave at least c+1 shares in every family. After disclosure of the other r−1 families, at most c coordinates of one offset are seen, which remain secret-independent conditional on the exposed offsets. This is the standard AND of family thresholds; the cost cut, not AND sharing, is the point.

Now restrict the access policy to one **uniform Shamir threshold across all individual capsules**, allowing any family allocations and even arbitrary positive integer multiplicities at individual endpoint/family pairs. Let w_(i,j) be the count assigned to family i and endpoint j. Denote its family count by E_i, total capsule count by M=Σ_i E_i, by C_i the sum of the largest c entries in family row i, and by L the sum of the largest f endpoint totals `Σ_i w_(i,j)`. Include zeros and assume c,f≤n; if either is ≥n the positive-threshold model is infeasible. The exact optimum is:

- **Infeasible unless n≥c+rf+1.**
- Otherwise let
  `Lmin_n,f(M)=f⌊M/n⌋+min(f,M mod n)`.
  The exact minimum count is **r·m***, where m* is the least integer 1≤m≤n satisfying
  `m ≥ c+1+Lmin_n,f(rm)`.
  An optimal layout has exactly m* capsules per family, one per selected endpoint in that family. The layout can be computed by a cyclic placement, not just asserted as a balanced degree sequence.

The theorem gives simultaneous per-family lower bounds E_i≥m*. Thus positive fixed capsule costs by family attain their optimum on the same allocation, including different family ciphertext lengths. This does not cover per-endpoint placement-dependent costs.

## Uniform threshold proof with multiplicities

**Exact cut criterion.** Worst-case authorized recovery sees M−L capsules. In the disclosure scenario with family i the one not exposed, the largest observable count is M−E_i+C_i. For ordinary degree-(threshold−1) Shamir sharing of the common scalar, distinct capsule shares at or above threshold reconstruct, whereas fewer are perfectly private. A feasible threshold therefore exists exactly when every

    E_i ≥ C_i + L + 1.

Under this condition each family support has more than c nonzero endpoints, so C_i≥c. Similarly the overall layout must retain capsules after f outages.

**Averaging cutoff.** For any n nonnegative numbers their top-k sum is at least k/n times their total (sorted-prefix average is no less than the remaining average). Thus C_i≥cE_i/n and L≥fM/n. Summing feasibility gives `(n−c−rf)M ≥ rn`, so n≥c+rf+1. Conversely put one capsule of every family at every endpoint: E_i=n, C_i=c and L=rf, so that endpoint cutoff suffices.

**Exact balanced top sum.** For M=nq+s with 0≤s<n, degree profile consisting of s weights q+1 and n−s weights q has top-f sum fq+min(f,s). No profile with total M is better. For f>0 sort the loads; if the f-th largest is at least q+1 then the top prefix already sums to at least f(q+1). Otherwise the tail has every weight ≤q, so the top prefix has sum at least M−(n−f)q=fq+s. Either case gives the displayed lower bound. The f=0 case is immediate. The minimum is monotone in M: any load vector at a larger total can have individual coordinates reduced to realize a smaller total without increasing any top-f sum, or this follows directly from the formula. This elementary load-balancing fact is also a standard majorization property; no new majorization theorem is claimed.

**Optimality despite duplicates and unequal family totals.** If m=min_i E_i, then M≥rm and L≥Lmin(M)≥Lmin(rm). Feasibility with C_i≥c forces m≥c+1+Lmin(rm). If m≤n, this is a legal candidate in the stated minimization; if m>n, every E_i already exceeds n≥m*, since the saturated layout made n a legal candidate. Hence every feasible weighted layout has E_i≥m* for all i and count at least r·m*. For attainment at any legal m≤n, number endpoints cyclically 0,...,n−1. Assign family i the residues of im, im+1,...,im+m−1. They are distinct within that row because m≤n. Across all r rows these are the residues of the complete integer interval 0,...,rm−1, hence global endpoint loads are exactly balanced. Since the legal inequality implies m≥c+1, C_i=c, and L= Lmin(rm). This proves exact attainment with binary endpoint/family edges, without limiting the lower bound to that class. For this layout any global threshold between (r−1)m+c+1 and rm−Lmin(rm), inclusive, works.

### Formula evaluation in O(r) quotient intervals

For each integer ℓ from 0 to r, intersect 1≤m≤n with the interval of m having floor(rm/n)=ℓ. Its endpoints are ceil(nℓ/r) and ceil(n(ℓ+1)/r)−1, clipped as above. In that interval let s=rm−nℓ. In the low-remainder portion s≤f, the feasibility gap is `(1−r)m+(n−f)ℓ−(c+1)`, nonincreasing, so only the first m of that portion may be needed. In the high-remainder portion s≥f the needed lower bound is `m≥c+1+f(ℓ+1)`; choose the first m meeting it and s≥f if still in the interval. The minimum across at most two candidates per quotient region gives m*. The s=f overlap is benign. Arithmetic uses O(r) evaluations, or a simple scan of n candidates when smaller. This is not implementation timing.

## Comparisons and boundary cases

- **Minimum uniform-feasible domain count:** at n=c+rf+1, the mean upper/lower constraints force m*=n. Indeed Lmin(rm)≥frm/n implies m(n−rf)≥n(c+1), hence m≥n. The exact counts are then r(c+f+1) for family-separated sharing and r(c+rf+1) for uniform. Example r=3,c=f=2,n=9: 15 versus 27, saving 12 capsules (44.4% of the uniform comparator). With n=12 for the same parameters the exact counts are 15 versus 21; with n≥15 both admit 15. With fixed r and c, as f grows on this binding domain frontier, the ratio of separated to uniform count tends to 1/r.
- **Earliest agreement when f>0:** m*=a iff n≥ra. If n≥ra the cyclic layout has disjoint family supports and Lmin(ra)=f, yielding m*=a. If n<ra then either floor(ra/n)=1 and the remainder is positive, or floor≥2; in both cases Lmin(ra)>f. Thus the smaller separated count cannot already be reached by the uniform policy with fewer domains. If r>1, f>0, there is a strictly separated infeasibility range a≤n<c+rf+1.
- **r=1:** there are no family disclosures and both optima reduce to c+f+1. For m≤n, Lmin(m)=min(f,m); a legal m necessarily exceeds f, so its least value is a.
- **f=0:** Lmin=0, both need r(c+1), n≥c+1; overlap is immaterial. With c≥n or f≥n, privacy or recovery fails by the stated nonpublic-input model. No r=0 or n=0 situation is claimed.
- A raw single-recipient standard ML-KEM call uses one capsule and has a different security/recovery goal. Capsule counts below only compare the same augmented fault policy; they are not an ML-KEM bandwidth record, throughput benchmark, or evidence of independent failure rates in deployed software.

## Cryptographic interpretation and primary boundaries

The family-separated construction is an oracle-free envelope. For any fixed disclosed r−1 families and endpoint subset, use a hybrid replacing unopened capsule keys in the remaining family by independently uniform masking keys. The assumed quantum-computational KEM key indistinguishability of that remaining family (standard quantum IND-CCA with classical query interface suffices, only the passive part used) bounds the replacement loss by the sum of component advantages, maximally a edges in the chosen layout (up to the conventional factor two for comparison of two message worlds). Keys and encapsulation coins must be generated independently even though offset/share plaintexts are correlated. The ideal XOR masking and threshold argument is then exact. Classical adaptive choices of disclosure sets can be handled by guessing the final survivor family and padded size-c endpoint set with factor at most r·binom(n,c) loss (useful for fixed c), and aborting wrong guesses; no efficient all-c adaptive reduction, chosen-key, or composed active/CCA oracle result is supplied. All independence applies to the stipulated family keys and threat model, not a guarantee that two deployed algorithms share no unknown systemic failure. A final recipient with the recovered key, retained cleartext, malicious service replies, and cross-family implementation compromise are separate.

[FIPS 203](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf) standardizes ML-KEM, not this envelope; [NIST SP 800-227 §4.6](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-227.pdf) already discusses beyond-two component combinations, with different goals. [Benaloh–Leichter](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs754/2001fa/bena88.pdf) subsumes the monotone access policy construction. [NIST IR 8214C](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8214/c/final) targets stronger threshold primitive security. [mKyber](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/Events/2024/fifth-pqc-standardization-conference/documents/papers/how-multi-recipient-kems.pdf) and [malicious-secure lattice mmKEM](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1655) address compact ciphertexts and receiver-corruption notions; our independent-capsule counts do not compare to those models. [NIST IR 8547](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2026/NIST.IR.8547.pdf) distinguishes HQC selection from approved ML-KEM; we do not assume r independently finalized NIST KEM standards or recommend particular combinations. Limited prior check supports only a provisional exact cardinality/cap frontier as contribution, not novelty of secret sharing, composite KEMs or cyclic balancing.
