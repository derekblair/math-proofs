# Sharp capsule trade-off for two KEM families and endpoint outages

An oracle-free theoretical envelope model, not a new KEM, FIPS-compatible threshold operation, active/CCA-secure wrapper, or deployment. The costs below count black-box key encapsulations and masked field payloads, not latency, certification or a performance record. No actual keys, ciphertexts, endpoints or transports were tested.

## Model

There are n distinct endpoint failure domains, and two independently implemented, independently keyed KEM families. Each capsule is labeled by exactly one endpoint and one family, and uses an independent honest invocation of that family. Public metadata does not itself provide any alternative secret channel. A capsule opens via the corresponding receiver secret into an independently encapsulated masking key or, abstractly, an opaque value when the corresponding receiver/family key is unavailable. Access policies can combine component outputs nonlinearly. Honest recovery by an authorized private aggregator must succeed from the public envelope and the available capsules after any f endpoint outages. There is no preexisting sender-to-aggregator secret, service channel, capsule covering multiple endpoints/families, or hidden input to recovery that does not fall under this model. The f-outage guarantee assumes any other participating domain behaves correctly. It does not cover malicious suppression in addition to those f, or an attacker controlling the aggregator.

In the privacy game, an observer receives the public envelope and all private decryption keys of one whole family plus those of up to c endpoint domains across both families. Other KEM implementations/keys and any aggregator holding the recovered secret are outside this compromise. This is a formal failure-domain disclosure model, not a guarantee against arbitrary correlated primitive failures, shared RNG/implementation bugs, copied plaintext caches, or full endpoint processes outside the labeled domains. The observer supplies two equal-length candidate scalars and receives an envelope for one. There is no decryption oracle on the composed envelopes, and no exposure of a recovered challenge value, its seed or intermediate aggregator state. The observer may compute quantumly with these classical public/disclosure interfaces; coherent service calls and active ciphertext injection are not modeled.

The proof requires passive challenge-key indistinguishability against a quantum adversary for whichever KEM family remains undisclosed. Post-quantum KEM IND-CCA with a classical-query interface is a sufficient, stronger component assumption; the envelope itself is not claimed to be CCA secure. Plain Diffie–Hellman is not a post-quantum replacement for either family in this game. All KEM public keys and encapsulation coins are independent of each other and of the sharing coins. Correlated plaintext **shares** are intended and supported by the hybrid. Correlated KEM key generation or encapsulation randomness is not supported, and “one family fails” means disclosure of its keys, not introducing malformed active components or compromise affecting the other family.

## Sharp optimum across arbitrary capsule recovery policies

Let a=c+f+1. The exact minimum is a capsules in each family, total 2a, achievable whenever n≥a; otherwise no design in the stated model suffices. Prove necessity separately for each family; the support condition charges distinct endpoint domains, not a capsule’s byte representation. If that family's support consists of no more than c+f distinct endpoint domains, select up to f of them as the outage set, and up to c more as disclosed endpoints, covering that support. Disclose the other family wholesale. Of all capsules still usable by the prescribed honest recovery after those outages, none is unavailable to the observer: all come from the disclosed family or the disclosed endpoints. By correctness the secret is computable from precisely that authorized recovery view. This contradicts confidentiality even for exact ideal black-box seals. Repetition at an already covered endpoint cannot evade the cut. The counting bound concerns every recovery procedure whose only nonpublic inputs are those surviving standard capsule capabilities. If independent external recovery secrets or interactive labeled threshold KEMs are allowed, it is not a lower bound on those.

**Attainment.** Choose a distinct endpoint support of size a in each family; those support sets can overlap fully, so n≥a is enough. Split s as X+Y with X uniform in a field and Y=s−X. Independently Shamir-share X in family one and Y in family two, each with reconstruction threshold c+1 among its a sites. Seal one share with a fresh independently encapsulated masking key for each pair. For primitive keys of 32 bytes and a field of size 2^256, fixed encodings allow one field share to be XOR-masked by that one KEM key. No masked payload key is reused. At most f outages leave at least c+1 in each family. With one family fully exposed and at most c keys opened in the other, the ideal view reveals a uniform independent offset and at most c shares of its complementary offset. Conditional on the first offset, the second family's ≤c opened Shamir coordinates are independent of s, and all other masked share payloads are uniform if their keys are independent uniform. Thus the ideal view has perfect privacy.

**Quantum-computational proof in the actual oracle-free game.** First fix the disclosed family and endpoint set, at most c. For each unopened capsule of the undisclosed family, replace its real mask key with an independent uniform one while leaving the KEM ciphertext in place. The input public key of that single component is the ordinary challenger key; all other independent keys can be generated locally. The challenger key is never disclosed. The simulated envelope can contain fully correlated share plaintexts because they are chosen by the reduction and masked only afterwards; no assumption of share independence is used. Every hybrid step invokes the base quantum-computational KEM key-indistinguishability assumption and does not need a composite decryption oracle. Thus for a fixed corruption pattern the envelope indistinguishability gap is at most a standard hybrid sum over unopened capsules (at most a components), with up to a factor 2 if bounding both message worlds against a common ideal by probability-difference convention. If the envelope is honestly sent, correctness inherits the relevant component decryption-failure probabilities; a simple union bound over component calls suffices, and robustness against bad replies is not proved.

One can additionally allow the choice of disclosed family and endpoints to depend on the challenge public envelope and earlier disclosures, with **explicit** loss. Guess the final family and a deterministic padded size-c containing set of endpoints (there are at most 2·binom(n,c) choices). Abort with a fair independent output if a requested disclosure is outside that guess or if the completed transcript does not match the padded guess. In the real experiment the probability of guessing that padded set on each fixed realized transcript is exactly its reciprocal, so the guessing test retains that fraction of the distinguishing gap. For the ideal hybrids a fixed guessed pattern exposes only keys available to the reduction, and comparisons on the padded subevent are functions of a secret-independent view. This yields at most the fixed-pattern bound times 2·binom(n,c) (with maximum component advantage over the cases). The reduction is useful asymptotically for fixed c, not a uniform strong adaptive theorem for c growing with n. No malicious chosen-key, CCA-on-envelope, or selective-opening assumption is smuggled into the claim. Any application allowing active inputs, challenge-context decryption or error leakage needs its own authenticated interface and composition proof. A compromised authorized final recipient already has the value, and cannot be protected by this envelope.

## Exact comparison: one global uniform Shamir threshold

Compare the same capsule model when recovery and privacy are governed by one uniform polynomial threshold t across **all individual capsules**, treating distinct capsule labels as distinct nonzero share sites. Origin field is large enough for the tested labels. This is a deliberately restricted baseline, not all access-structure sharing. It is allowed arbitrary duplicated endpoint/family envelopes, unbalanced allocations, or any t.

For family i∈{1,2}, let E_i its capsule count, C_i the sum of its largest c endpoint multiplicities, counting zeros when needed, and M=E_1+E_2. Let L be the sum of the largest f total endpoint multiplicities. The worst surviving count is M−L. If family other than i is disclosed, the worst exposed count is (M−E_i)+C_i. A uniform threshold exists exactly when

    E_i ≥ C_i + L + 1, for i=1,2.

This formula holds for arbitrary integer multiplicities, including parallel capsules at one endpoint-family pair. The threshold may be any integer strictly above all exposed counts and at most M−L. Each family in a feasible layout uses more than c nonempty endpoints, hence C_i≥c; availability needs more than f total nonempty endpoints, hence L≥f. In particular E_i≥a.

**Exact feasibility and costs.**
- If n≥2a, choose disjoint family supports of size a, one capsule per support endpoint. Then C_i=c, L=f and E_i=a; exact minimum 2a.
- If a+f≤n<2a, exact minimum 2(a+f). For the lower bound, suppose L<2f. Then fewer than f endpoints have total multiplicity ≥2. All such endpoints occur among the largest f, so L=f+δ, where δ=M−s and s is the number of nonempty endpoints. The two feasibility inequalities force E_i≥a+δ, hence s=M−δ≥2a+δ and n≥2a, contradiction. Thus L≥2f and each E_i≥a+f. For attainment choose a+f endpoints and place one capsule of each family on each; C_i=c and L=2f.
- If n<a+f, infeasible even with weighted duplication. The sum of the largest c multiplicities is at least c/n times their total, so C_i≥cE_i/n. Similarly L≥fM/n. Summing the two inequalities gives (n−c−2f)M≥2n, thus n≥c+2f+1=a+f (otherwise impossible). Trivial c or f≥n cases are already infeasible from privacy or availability; the averaging statement uses c,f≤n.

So family-separated access saves exactly 2f capsules in the intermediate regime and gives a feasible construction in regimes a≤n<a+f where no uniform threshold works. For n=8,c=f=2, the exact count is 10 versus 14, a 28.6% reduction from this uniform comparator, with family allocations 5+5 versus 7+7. For equal per-family per-capsule costs, this is also the same fractional reduction in the summed capsule bodies plus fixed-length masked shares; headers/signature and transport excluded. More generally costs L_i per family imply the lower and upper allocations above for each positive weighting; lengths are a(L_1+L_2+64) versus (a+f)(L_1+L_2+64) when both share pads are 32 bytes and L_i denote unadorned capsule lengths in bytes. One unaided FIPS ML-KEM recipient uses one capsule, not ten; it does not meet the two-domain failure goal. The claim is optimal count within a precise augmented goal/model, not a speedup against standard ML-KEM's original goal or against general access structures. The bound does not count interactive recovery where states already contain equivalent confidential capabilities.

## Primary context and caveats

[FIPS 203](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf) specifies standard ML-KEM, not this wrapper. [NIST SP 800-227](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-227.pdf) already treats multi-KEM composition and the goal of relying on one secure component; [NIST IR 8214C](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8214/c/final) treats standardized threshold primitives and stronger security requirements. [mKyber](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/Events/2024/fifth-pqc-standardization-conference/documents/papers/how-multi-recipient-kems.pdf) explicitly discusses recipient corruptions and compressed multi-recipient wires; [Lattice-based mmKEM with malicious security](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1655) gets substantial bandwidth savings using a stronger specialized assumption. Those constructions solve other goals and are not beaten by these counts. [Benaloh–Leichter](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs754/2001fa/bena88.pdf) already give general monotone access-structure sharing, which directly subsumes the AND of two family thresholds used here. Secret sharing and combining two KEM components are prior techniques; the candidate is the sharp endpoint/family cut frontier and uniform-baseline separation, not their invention. Different algorithms that both use the same failure domain do not meet the independent-family hypotheses. [NIST IR 8547](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2026/NIST.IR.8547.pdf) says HQC was selected for standardization and is not a claimed second finalized FIPS in this dossier. No adoption recommendation, new primitive, assumption of independent cryptanalytic failures, or absolute priority claim.
