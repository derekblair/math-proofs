# Exact finite spectrum certificates for abelian graph covers

This dossier presents a lattice criterion to decide whether every regular abelian cover of a finite connected simple undirected graph contains a simple cycle of some length in a specified finite set (lengths at least three). It gives a separately kernel-verified application to the Petersen graph. This is a restricted Erdős–Gyárfás result, not a proof of the full conjecture.

## General criterion
Fix a spanning tree and label each remaining directed edge by a distinct basis vector of M = Z^d, opposite orientations by negatives, and tree edges by zero. For a cyclically nonbacktracking closed base walk w of an allowed length, let z_w denote the total voltage and D_w the set of prefix-voltage differences at distinct positions projecting to the same base vertex. Do not count the repeated closing endpoint in these positions. The lift is a simple cycle precisely when z_w lies in the kernel K and no member of D_w does. Such a kernel ranges over all subgroups of M: the quotient gives a regular abelian cover, and tree-gauged voltages generate the deck of a connected cover. Disconnected regular covers can be viewed componentwise or represented by the same labels with additional unused deck elements.

To test whether some cover avoids all allowed lengths, search only spans of subsets of the union of the listed collision vectors. If any bad kernel exists, span only the collision vectors it contains. This preserves all obligations already present and creates no new closing equalities. An exact search adds a collision to the integer span whenever a row closes and currently has no such collision. It branches if several choices are possible. Either every closing row has a collision, identifying an avoiding quotient, or a closing row has no choices, closing that branch. The search is finite; no polynomial running-time bound is claimed.

`SpanCriterion.lean` proves the algebraic finite-span reduction and quotient realization. `GenericVoltage.lean` proves the cover representation and a graph-level simple-cycle lifting theorem. The spanning-tree reduction and completeness of a general all-words enumeration are described in prose; the general lattice search algorithm itself is not formalized.

Finite size bound (paper proof): let L be the longest prescribed length. The tested voltages and collision vectors have Euclidean norm at most L. If the search finds a bad span E of rank r, choose r independent original generators and an r-minor with nonzero absolute value D (D=1 at rank zero). Cramer's rule gives D·(span_Q(E)∩Z^d) ⊆ E. For any tested v outside span_Q(E), some nonzero augmented (r+1)-minor has absolute value at most L^(r+1). Reduce modulo m=D(1+L^(r+1)). Such witnesses cannot enter E+mZ^d, because the augmented minor would be divisible by m. No torsion witness disappears either: if v=e+mz lies in the rational span, z lies in the saturation and mz∈E already. Thus the finite quotient Z^d/(E+mZ^d) preserves all tested incidences, with order at most [L^r(1+L^(r+1))]^d.

## Petersen graph
`PetersenUniversal.all_regular_covers` proves for an arbitrary abelian deck, including infinite or 2-torsion decks, that every regular abelian cover of the Petersen graph has a simple C8 or C16. `PetersenBaseId.identifies` checks the base has outer pentagon, inner pentagram and five spokes. The certificate is 15 explicitly checked walks. Branch obligations are verified by integer combinations in the full edge space, not by trusting an HNF implementation or requiring a tree gauge in the Lean example. Graph prefixes, simple-cycle separations and algebraic branch proofs are kernel-checked with Lean 4.34.1 and Mathlib d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612.

Exoo, [Voltage Graphs, Group Presentations and Cages (2004)](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v11i1n2/pdf/), already gives a 960-vertex abelian lift of girth 16 and a girth-at-most-16 bound on all abelian lifts. Girth at most 16 by itself does not force a power-of-two cycle in the presence of shorter odd cycles. Kelley–Walker [ISIT 2008](https://math.unl.edu/faculty/Kelley/Kelley_Walker_ISIT08.pdf) discuss abelian-forcing reduced walks; the present certificates check simple lift injectivity directly. Absolute priority and minimal size across all sharp covers are not claimed.

The accompanying exact Python checks construct a connected 50-vertex cyclic cover with no C4 or C8 and an explicit C16. They separately exhaust all chord assignments for the abelian groups of order less than five, finding none that avoid C8. The sharpness witness is thus smallest among connected regular abelian Petersen covers in the exact computational check. The example is not claimed to have ordinary girth 16. At this stage, the concrete sharpness and minimal-size checks are computational; the universal upper theorem and its algebraic/graph certificate are Lean-verified.
