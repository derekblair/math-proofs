# A 64 bound for nonnilpotent ribbon groups of derived class two

**Theorem.** Let G be finite of order coprime to 6. Suppose its commutator subgroup D=[G,G] has nilpotency class at most two (equivalently, [D,D] commutes with D). For any nonidentity x,y in G, the one-matching bi-Cayley graph with rims given by x and y contains a simple cycle of length 4, 8, 16, 32, or 64. The rim elements need not generate G. The ambient group need not be nilpotent.

The Lean entry point is [RibbonNonnilDerivedTwo.lean](RibbonNonnilDerivedTwo.lean), theorem `RibbonNonnilDerivedTwo.finite_coprime_six`. A one-matching bi-Cayley graph here has vertices G×{0,1}, with the identity edges between the rims, and the x/x⁻¹ or y/y⁻¹ edges within the respective rim. This is not a claim about all bi-Cayley graphs or the full Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture. The all-odd, possibly nonnilpotent case is also not proved here.

## Proof route

- Work first in the subgroup generated by the two rim elements. If their commutator is nontrivial in the class-two quotient, the quotient separates prefixes and exact commutator identities close one of the 32/64 walks in G itself.
- In the stationary case, the mod-p abelianization of D is a cyclic group-algebra module generated by the image of the port commutator. Its finite commutative quotient algebra supplies a normalized affine chart from each simple residue. The proof handles two distinct residues with two charts, or one nonreduced local algebra using its first-order dual-number chart. If neither exists, the derived group is cyclic, which reduces to the earlier metabelian theorem.
- The finite-field separation computation covers 76 slope branches and the surface 2a+1=0 over arbitrary finite extensions. The finite exceptions reduce to characteristic 11. All polynomial identities, 120 distinct pairs of bad charts and 192 nonzero projective tangent directions are checked by Lean; generators are not trusted. A chart only separates distinct source vertices. All actual closures and the exceptional pairs of prefixes are proved by word identities in G, so the argument does not assume that simple cycles lift through odd covers.

The bound 64 remains sharp on this whole class, since the explicitly checked finite UT₆(F₁₇) class-five example in the [predecessor dossier](../class-two-bicayley/class-five-sharp/README.md) belongs to it. Sharpness specifically within the nonnilpotent subfamily is not claimed.

Burkhart's [odd-order Cayley graph theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05060) concerns ordinary Cayley graphs. The one-matching graph here is the bi-Cayley construction of [Zhou and Feng](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2015.10.004), and need not be vertex-transitive. This dossier does not claim absolute priority.
