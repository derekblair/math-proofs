# Monotone affine permanent projections of powered permanents

**Scope.** The results below concern nonnegative real affine projections of the powered permanent. They do not settle the signed affine exponent or give an algorithm for the ordinary permanent.

An order-N monotone affine projection is the permanent of an N-by-N matrix of affine forms with all coefficients nonnegative real numbers. Let F_(d,q) = sum_i x_i^q. Its minimum order for q >= 2 and d >= 3 is (q-1)d+1. For d=1 or 2 it is q; for q=1 it is 1. Consequently for P_(n,q)(X) = per_n(X^{∘q}) and n >= 3, q >= 2, the minimum order lies between max(qn, (q-1)floor((n+1)^2/4)+1) and n+(q-1)n^2, uniformly Theta(q n^2). For n=1,2 its minimum order is qn. The lower bounds also hold under coefficientwise limits of the same fixed-order nonnegative projections.

## Exact Fermat order

Remove edges unused by every perfect matching. A used entry can contain only one term: after fixing terms in all other entries of a matching containing the edge, swapping the term in this entry changes the total degree by one (impossible), or changes the exponent vector by e_i-e_j, impossible between any two distinct degree-q pure powers. Thus every matching contains exactly q variable edges, all of one color. Fix a color-0 matching M_0, identify each of its matched row-column pairs as a vertex and direct each remaining edge from its row's vertex to its column's vertex. The q variable edges of M_0 give q marked vertices, called anchors. Directed simple cycles in the off-reference arcs correspond to flipping one alternating cycle of M_0.

For each color j > 0, choose a matching M_j. In its symmetric difference with M_0, flipping a component containing any variable edge must remove all q color-0 edges and add all q color-j edges. Indeed, exchanging a positive number of old variable edges without all of them forces either too few color-0 edges or a mix of colors; a component adding any new variable edges forces the matching to be pure color j. So one simple directed cycle C_j contains all anchors and exactly the q color-j arcs. In the union of these chosen cycles, use only their off-reference arcs. No directed simple cycle can meet a nonempty proper subset of anchors: flipping it retains at least one color-0 edge but removes another and adds no color-0 edge. A cycle avoiding all anchors can only have constant arcs, since its flip leaves all q color-0 edges intact.

All C_j visit the anchors in the same cyclic order. Otherwise, linearize after a fixed anchor a_0, take a pair u,v inverted between two cycles, and concatenate paths u to v and v to u that avoid a_0. This closed walk leaves u and returns to u; erase repeated internal vertices to get a simple cycle through u avoiding a_0. The same observation gives gap separation for all anchor-free-interior paths in the union: if the interiors of a_i to a_(i+1) and a_j to a_(j+1) intersect, i != j, splice a prefix of the first to a suffix of the second, then return to a_i through the consecutive anchors from a_(j+1) to a_i along a reference chosen cycle. This closed walk omits the nonempty cyclic interval a_(i+1), ..., a_j and reduces to a simple cycle through a_i. If a_(j+1)=a_i the return leg is empty. The walk visits a_i only at its ends; erasing repeated internal vertices preserves a_i.

Thus we can mix, independently in every gap, the segments of the chosen C_j, obtaining a simple cycle through all anchors. Its flip always produces a degree-q pure power of a nonzero color. Varying one segment holding all others fixed shows its number of colored arcs does not depend on the choice of j. There is at least one positive-count gap, and there cannot be two: choose distinct nonzero colors in two such gaps, since d>=3. Exactly one gap carries all q colored arcs for each j; all other gaps are constant.

In this active gap the internal vertices just after arcs 1 through q-1 of color j are distinct and private to that color among these counted vertices. A shared vertex gives a prefix of the color-j segment with a colored arc and a suffix of the color-k segment with a colored arc, j != k. Remove internal cycles in their concatenation, all constant because they avoid the anchors. This leaves a mixed-color segment, which concatenates with the fixed constant segments into a simple cycle by gap separation, a contradiction. Count q anchors and (d-1)(q-1) distinct internal vertices.

For equality construct q anchors with diagonal entries x_0 and q-1 private internal vertices with diagonal 1 for each other color. For each j>0 use a path from a_0 to a_1 through its q-1 private vertices, all q arcs carrying x_j. Complete the cycle with the common constant path a_1,...,a_(q-1),a_0. Any nontrivial cycle chooses exactly one branch and visits every anchor, so the permanent contains exactly the desired monomials, with coefficient one. For d=1, the degree lower bound q is achieved by diagonal x_0. For d=2 use q anchors, diagonal x_0 and a full directed cycle with arcs x_1; degree proves optimality. For q=1 use the single affine entry sum_i x_i.

## Hall slice and powered permanent

Split the rows of the n-by-n input into a and n-a and the columns into a-1 and n-a+1. Make the lower left zero, upper left and lower right one, and each upper-right entry a different variable. Every permutation uses exactly one upper-right edge, and for each such edge the other rows give (a-1)!(n-a)! allowed choices. The powered permanent becomes this positive constant times F_(a(n-a+1),q). Rescale one row of any putative projected matrix to remove the constant; maximize the number of variables at floor((n+1)^2/4). The independent degree bound is qn. For the upper bound subdivide each original bipartite edge to length 2q-1, assigning its variable to odd-indexed edges and unit constants to even-indexed edges. The alternating path is matched either with both exterior endpoints and q variable edges or with its internal unit edges only. The resulting matrix order is n+(q-1)n^2. For n=2, combine two pure products on a diagonal and one full off-diagonal cycle on 2q vertices; n=1 is one product of q factors. These realize degree exactly.

## Fixed-order closure

For a sequence P_t=per(A_t) at a fixed order with all affine coefficients nonnegative and convergent coefficients, set a=A_t(1). Select a largest product matching of weight M_t <= P_t(1). If M_t=0 then P_t=0 by nonnegativity; such a subsequence can only give the zero limit. Otherwise permute it onto the diagonal and rescale each row so its diagonal entry evaluated at 1 is one. Denote the evaluated rescaled entries by b_ij. Every directed simple cycle product is at most one, else switching that cycle gives a heavier matching. Let s_i be the largest product over simple directed paths from i, including the empty path of weight one. For b_ij>0, prepend i->j to an optimal path from j and erase cycles of weight at most one; b_ij s_j <= s_i. This also holds for b_ij=0. Conjugate the rescaled matrix by these positive potentials, scaling row i by 1/s_i and column j by s_j; the permanent is unchanged and all evaluated entries are <=1. Because coefficients are nonnegative, all individual coefficients are bounded by one. Restore the permanent by multiplying one row by M_t; all coefficients remain uniformly bounded since P_t(1) converges. A convergent subsequence of matrix coefficients gives the required exact nonnegative projection by continuity. This concerns fixed-order real nonnegative Euclidean closure, not Zariski closure or signed representations.

## Reproduction and context

Run `node monotone-powered-permanent-replay.js`. The replay checks 243 scalar constructions, 112 Hall specializations, 1,953,125 labeled order-three candidates for the three quadratic powers (none realizes them), and 125 exact rational normalizations. Compile `MonotonePoweredPermanentSlots.lean` with `lake env lean -s 65536 /absolute/path/to/MonotonePoweredPermanentSlots.lean` under Lean 4.34.1 with Mathlib at `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`. Lean checks the abstract slot invariance and concentration lemmas; it does not check the graph reduction, exact Fermat theorem, Hall slice, or fixed-order closure.

[Grochow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.08417) develops monotone projection lower bounds via Newton extension complexity. Here the powered permanent's Newton polytope is only a dilation, and the lower bound uses degree and cycle-color purity. [Chaugule and Limaye](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/021/) study closures of monotone complexity classes; the closure here is for the fixed-order nonnegative matrix parameterization. [Sheshadri, Proposition A.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40484v1#A3) gives the same-order disjoint-path Fermat construction for determinants and lower bounds for signed determinant and Pfaffian models. The graph of disjoint source-sink paths also gives the monotone permanent upper bound by closing the paths into cycles. The result here is the sharp lower bound for monotone affine permanent, its powered-permanent consequence, and fixed-order closure. The edge-subdivision upper bound for the powered permanent is also analyzed in [the affine projection dossier](https://github.com/derekblair/math-proofs/pull/101). No priority claim is made.
