# Literal dyadic motifs for a chain of common-gain digons with three-edge separators

Take k digons in series, with three-edge paths connecting the right endpoint of each digon to the left endpoint of the next. Lift by an abelian voltage group, with all k nontrivial digon voltages equal up to sign to a single element g of odd order n. Every connected component of the resulting subgraph is the same graph on (sheet, column) ∈ C_n × {0,...,4k−3}. There is a vertical edge (x,j)−(x,j+1) at every successive column and an extra edge (x,4i)−(x+1,4i+1) for i=0,...,k−1. To see this, fix a spanning path through each digon using one of its two edges and all separators, and gauge that whole path to zero. Choosing the other digon edge as the spanning edge flips the sign of the sole remaining gain in that digon, independently at each digon; hence all the remaining edges can have gain +g. Lift components are then the cosets of the cyclic subgroup generated by g.

For k=3 the complete equal dyadic motif classification is:
* n=7: a literal theta with 16 edges per arm.
* Every odd n≥13: a literal barbell with two 32-edge loops and a 32-edge connector.
* n=3,5,9,11: no literal equal dyadic barbell or equal-arm dyadic theta at all. There is nevertheless a direct C16 (and C32) for n=5 and a C32 for n=9 and 11. For n=3 there is no dyadic simple cycle at all in the 30-vertex chain.

The no-input Node checker eg-common-gain-long-digon-chain-replay.js verifies the positive charts literally. The C32/path32/C32 is written over the integer sheet group; its maximum absolute difference between distinct support sheets in any one column is 12. Thus projection mod n≥13 cannot identify any two support vertices. Every permitted edge remains an edge mod n.

The negative scans are exhaustive. Equal arm/loop-path lengths L require at least 3L−1 actual vertices; the only lengths to test are 4,8,16,32 after excluding 1 and 2 (degree-three theta endpoints would have to be in columns internal to a digon; two degree-three common neighbors cannot both exist here, and the cycle scan rules out C4 in all exceptions). For the latter exclusion directly, L=2 theta would give C4, L=2 barbell already requires a simple C2, and L=1 neither can occur in a simple graph. For theta search, use sheet translation to normalize one endpoint to sheet 0 and try every column with degree three. Enumerate every simple path of length L from each of its three first edges. Group by last vertex, and check all three first-edge classes for pairwise disjoint interior masks and distinct last edges. Every possible theta is covered. For barbell search when a C_L exists, enumerate every cycle using sheet translation to place a minimum-column vertex on sheet 0, keep one orientation and then restore every sheet translate. Deduplicating vertex support is sound because only support, not the particular cyclic ordering, determines allowable bridge vertices and anchors. Check every disjoint pair and every L-edge simple connector whose interiors avoid the two supports. For n=5,L16 this gives 40 vertices-support classes with 50 disjoint pairs; for n=11,L32 it gives 264 supports with 3,168 disjoint pairs. No eligible connector is found. All other missing barbell sizes are ruled out by cardinality or absence of cycles of the required length.

Additional checked positive charts: four digons suffice for a direct C32 when n=3, a literal 16-arm theta when n=5 or 9, and a literal C32/path32/C32 for n=11. Twelve digons suffice for a literal C32/path32/C32 when n=3, without claiming twelve is minimal. Additional digons preserve any chart already present, by taking the indicated contiguous subchain.

All fields here are only cyclic sheet groups; n need not be prime. This is exact JavaScript replay, not a Lean proof.
