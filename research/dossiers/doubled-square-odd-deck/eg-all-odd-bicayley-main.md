# Erdős–Gyárfás for all finite bi-Cayley graphs of odd-order groups

**Paper theorem.** A nonempty finite simple graph of minimum degree at least three that admits a free action of a finite group of odd order with at most two vertex orbits has a simple cycle whose length is a power of two. For one orbit this is Burkhart’s prior odd-order Cayley theorem. Here the new scope is the full two-orbit class, including nonnilpotent groups. This is not a resolution for general graphs or an end-to-end Lean proof.

## Reduction to connected marked graphs

Write A_g,B_g for the two free orbits. Every within-orbit neighbor set is inverse-closed with no identity, hence has even cardinality in odd order. The number of cross-orbit ports is the same on each side by counting them. If there are at least three, keep exactly three, normalize one by relabeling B to the identity, and discard other edges. The connected components are Haar graphs on left cosets of the subgroup generated by the two normalized port differences. If there are one or two, retain one normalized matching and one inverse pair in each orbit (minimum degree forces each such pair to exist). Their components are two-rail graphs on cosets of a subgroup generated by the two rail marks. With zero cross ports, use Burkhart on one connected Cayley component. It suffices to prove the theorem for the marked odd groups G generated by two elements u,v and with nonidentity marks, with three distinct ports {1,u,v} in the Haar case.

## A minimal nonabelian quotient

By [Feit–Thompson](https://msp.org/pjm/1963/13-3/pjm-v13-n3-s.pdf), every finite odd-order group is solvable. If G is abelian, use the finite nilpotent bi-Cayley theorem in the companion proof. Otherwise choose a nonabelian quotient Q of least order, with marks inherited from G. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of Q contains D=Q′, because its quotient is a proper abelian quotient. Since Q is solvable, D≠1 and D′ is a proper normal subgroup of Q, so D′=1. Also D has no proper nonzero characteristic subgroup; in fact it is a minimal nonzero normal subgroup of Q, hence elementary abelian for some prime p. The action of Q/D on D as an F_p-vector space is irreducible. If D lies in the center, Q is nilpotent of class two.

If D is not central then Z(Q)=1. Pick a lift t with nonidentity action T on D. Because the action factors through the abelian group Q/D, the fixed space of every action matrix is invariant under the whole action. Thus T−1 is invertible. To see that the action is faithful modulo D, take c in the centralizer of D. Its commutator with t lies in D, so multiply c by a suitable d∈D to make it commute with t, using invertibility of T−1. For every h∈Q, conjugating [h,c] by t leaves it unchanged: t h t⁻¹ lies in the same D-coset as h, and c centralizes D. Since [h,c] is in D and T has no nonzero fixed point, the adjusted c is central, hence identity. Thus the centralizer of D equals D. The commuting action algebra is a finite field F_q: each of its nonzero elements acts invertibly, since its kernel is invariant; irreducibility makes D one-dimensional over this field. The faithful abelian quotient embeds in F_q^× and is cyclic. A lift of its generator has order m=|Q/D|, because its m-th power belongs to D and is fixed by its nonidentity action. This splits Q as the Frobenius group F_q^+⋊⟨λ⟩, m odd and λ acting by multiplication.

## Literal seeds and induction

Prove the two-rail and Haar cases simultaneously by strong induction on |G|. In the class-two nilpotent-quotient case, select a further nontrivial marked nilpotent seed exactly as in the companion proof: a prime-rank-two abelian quotient or one of the bounded characteristic-three class-two marked quotients. The chart produces a literal equal dyadic barbell or theta. In the Frobenius-quotient case the marked ports stay distinct (coalescing any two would make Q cyclic). For the Haar graph use the previous cyclic-abelianization motif theorem on the marked metabelian Q, which provides a literal equal dyadic barbell or theta. For the two-rail graph use the Frobenius rail proof: its one-translation, two-nonzero characteristic-three, and two-nonzero characteristic-at-least-five parts give the same kind of literal motif for every marked Frobenius quotient.

Pass back to the original graph using the regular cover induced by the group quotient. In the preimage of the chosen motif, gauge the barbell bridge or first theta arm to identity. The left deck action lets the other two labels act as right multiplications on sheet labels. After suppressing every equal m-edge path, the component is a two-rail graph or Haar multigraph on the subgroup B generated by those two voltages in the kernel. If a rail loop voltage is identity, a literal m-cycle lifts unchanged. If two theta ports coincide, alternate those distinct arm lifts for a literal 2m-cycle. Otherwise the compressed graphs are simple connected marked graphs on the strictly smaller finite odd group B, so the simultaneous induction supplies a simple dyadic cycle. Expanding its edges multiplies its length by dyadic m, with no repeated vertices because different path types, and different lifts of one path type, have disjoint interiors. This does not require the kernel to be abelian or nilpotent.

**Verification boundary.** The proof uses Feit–Thompson and the earlier marked Haar motif theorem, as named. Exact finite-field and quotient charts are independently replayable. The full graph and group induction is a paper proof; some explicit seed templates are checked in Lean. No publication priority claim.
