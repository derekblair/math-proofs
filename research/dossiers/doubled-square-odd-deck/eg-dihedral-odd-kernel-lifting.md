# Odd-kernel lifting after both rail marks project to dihedral reflections

This proof concerns the two-rail marked K-model with actual noninvolutive marks u,v, after their projections are distinct generating reflections in an odd-rotation dihedral quotient. Kernel M0 is odd. It is not the full even solvable theorem.

Replace M0 by the normal closure M of u²,v². It is a nontrivial odd normal subgroup contained in M0. H/M is still finite dihedral on the two generator reflections, with odd rotation order d≥3 (the old rotation order divides d, and M0/M is odd). M is normally generated by those squares, so in at least one nonzero elementary-abelian quotient W=M/(M' M^p), for odd p, one of their images a,b is nonzero. Conjugations U=Ad(u), V=Ad(v) on W are involutions, U fixes a, V fixes b, and R=UV has odd order dividing d. The projected marked K graph has 2d doubled rails alternating with unit bridges as a cycle. On a consecutive chain the transported gains are, up to orientation, a, U b, U V a, U V U b, (UV)²a, etc. By gauging spanning tree edges, the lifted subgraph admits these as literal parallel gains (up to independently reversible signs).

## Both square images nonzero (complete)

If a,b are independent, the first two gains a,U b are independent. For p≥5 the formal length-16-arm pair-of-digons theta arm chart has nonzero same-base guard vectors with coefficients of magnitude at most 4, so it remains literal. For p=3 use the separate two-digon F3² arm-8 chart. If a,U b are collinear, then a,b are collinear since U²=1 and U fixes a; both U,V fix the common line, so transported gains alternate a,b. Scale the first to 1. The general two-digon chart can be oriented to make the second gain either sign, and we can swap the two types by starting one position later. For p>24 none of the distinct rational short slopes coalesce modulo p (cross determinants bounded by 24). The two signs can both remain bad under the fixed chart only at the symmetric values ±1, ±1/2, ±1/3, ±2, ±3. After swapping/scaling, suffice alternating gains 1, 2 or 3. Equal gains use the five-rail odd chain proof (at this p only three rails and a barbell). Alternating ratio2 uses the displayed integer three-digon 8-arm theta of overall coordinate span5, and ratio3 the two-digon 8-arm theta of span7. For p=5,7,11,13,17,19,23, the checker first tests all four sign/swap orientations of the generic chart, then exact finite theta charts for the 19 remaining scalar classes. p=3 nonzero collinear means equal up to sign and uses the already verified five-pair C8/8/C8 barbell. At least five slots are available since d≥3.

## Exactly one square image nonzero, independent consecutive nonzero gains (complete for odd d)

Swap marks to take b=0. After collapsing only the zero-gain duplicate edge at alternating positions, the nonzero digon pairs are separated by a path of length3, with nonzero gains a, R a, R² a dithered along the chain. Let c=R a. If independent, for p≥11 a two-digon bridge-three formal 32-arm theta has coefficient/span at most 8 so specializes injectively. For p=5,7 and c'=R²a independent of the first two, use a rank-three three-digon chart. If dependent, write c'=αa+βc with α≠0. U R U=R^-1 and U a=a give α²=1, β(α+1)=0 by applying U and multiplying by R². Thus c'=a (β=0) or −a+βc. Up to flipping c in the chart by gauge, β ranges over residues 0 through (p−1)/2. Exact numerical charts cover all those p5/p7 types. At p3 the rank-three chart applies; rank two has R action of odd order, so the only allowed distinct two-dimensional cyclic minimal polynomial is (X−1)² (it excludes X²−1 distinct ±1, X²+1 roots order4, and (X+1)²). Hence the gains repeat a,c,−a−c with R of order3. If d>3 (then d≥9), the four-pair chain has a literal exact theta. If d=3, take the whole ring with three nonzero digons separated by bridges of length3. Let z=(uv)^3 in the projected W. It is invariant under R, so z∈span(a−c), and the last gauge bridge can carry any of the three shifts 0, a−c, c−a (global sign immaterial). Each ring profile has an exact literal 16-arm theta.

## Exactly one nonzero, all gains collinear (remaining branch beyond verified small charts)

If R a is collinear with a, then since U a=a and U²=1, V acts by ±1 on that line; the rotation R has odd order, so the sign is +1. Thus all nonzero gains are equal after gauge. This is the separate long-bridge equal-gain problem (at least three nonzero digons; root collaborator obtained a literal C32/32/C32 for all odd p≥13). At p=3,d=3, W is generated by conjugates of a and is one-dimensional; circumference z=(uv)^3 is zero because U z=3a−z=−z and U fixes W. The resulting 36-vertex ring has direct C16 but no literal equal theta of arm2/4/8, nor equal barbell of feasible size (no C4/C8; m16 needs47 vertices). Literal equal motifs alone are not enough in this branch. Full odd-kernel degeneration theorem not complete.

## Why the positive motifs suffice

The chosen chart is already a literal equal theta or equal barbell in the W-quotient lift of the appropriate labeled subgraph. Lift that motif over the remaining odd kernel, gauge voltages, and compress into the odd-group K/H models as in the earlier odd-order bi-Cayley theorem. Degenerate coincident/zero voltages yield immediate dyadic cycles as in that proof. Any ordinary dyadic witness in the compressed odd-group graph expands uniformly by the dyadic rail/arm factor. No regularity of the full H action was assumed other than the original graph model and stated factor properties.
