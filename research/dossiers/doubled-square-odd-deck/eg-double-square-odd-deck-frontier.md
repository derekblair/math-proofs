# Doubled-opposite square over an odd voltage deck: useful solved subclasses

Model the square on base vertices 0,1,2,3, doubled edges 0–1 and 2–3 with gains 1/u and 1/v, unit 1–2, and closing gain δ on 3–0. Voltages generate the connected deck. They may be regarded as independent when this base comes from a double-lift of an equal theta; in the three-port Haar order2·odd case they are more closely related by conjugation. The goal is arbitrary odd G, still open here.

If the deck is abelian the six-orbit paper/certificate theorem already covers it. Stronger local partial: if a quotient of the odd deck admits a further quotient onto a nonabelian odd p-group p≥5 with the images of u,v,δ generating, then there is a lift-stable literal dyadic motif in a projected subgraph, so the original square lift satisfies Erdős–Gyárfás. In the nonabelian p-group take the Frattini quotient (elementary p, rank≥2). Abbreviate images a,b,z. If a,b independent, use the generic two-digon theta (all 24 coefficient guards <p), ignoring closing δ. If a,b nonzero collinear, z independent; unroll the closing square along z, which has order p≥5 modulo the line of a,b. There are 2p consecutive doubled pairs with unit bridges, alternating gains a,b; use the ordinary alternating chiropodal charts from eg-dihedral-odd-kernel-lifting.md (the abelian part means no extra adjoint assumptions for this step). If exactly one of a,b is nonzero, z independent; unroll into p visible doubled pairs with bridges length3, collapsing only one of the coincident labels at each zero-gain pair. Gains all equal; chain3 or4 literals apply for every odd prime p≥5. Both a,b zero is impossible by rank≥2. Lift the literal motif through the remaining odd part using the earlier odd K/H theorem. Thus odd nilpotent decks are covered if they are abelian or have any nonabelian Sylow p≥5. For a nonabelian Sylow3 the independent and both-nonzero collinear cases still work (exact F3^2 Θ and common gain five-pair motifs); one-zero square remains, Frattini quotient becomes the genuine 36-vertex long-bridge ring d3 with no literal equal motif.

The p3 one-zero case might be bypassed using a nonabelian class-two quotient where the collapsed gain, x^3/y^3 or commutator survives. One should NOT assume any nonabelian two-generated 3-group surjects onto order27: e.g. C27⋊C3 with conjugation x→x^{10} is already minimal nonabelian (cyclic center and derived contained in every nontrivial normal). In a minimal class-two quotient, commutator order3 and cyclic center can offer powers of order≥9, but modest p3,d9 weighted-ring obstructions must also be checked.

Literal equal motifs do not exist in all small abelian square profiles. An exact run over p=3,5,7, a normalized to1 and b≠0, last δ arbitrary, found profile exceptions count4,6,4 respectively (these still have dyadic cycles). Thus blanket transfer by choosing one literal quotient motif needs structural input. ## Structural reduction of the remaining odd-deck problem

The cases proved so far reduce general odd doubled-square decks to **cyclic primitive affine quotients**. More precisely, suppose no quotient of the odd deck (D) is a nonabelian prime-power group (those already supply a lift-stable literal motif). If (D) is nilpotent, it is abelian and the six-orbit theorem applies directly. Otherwise choose a nonnilpotent quotient (P) of minimal order. This odd group is solvable by Feit–Thompson. Choose a nonnormal maximal subgroup; its core quotient is still nonnilpotent because a maximal subgroup of a finite nilpotent group is normal. Minimality forces the core to be trivial. In the resulting faithful primitive action, a minimal normal elementary abelian group (W) is transitive (normal orbits form a block system), hence regular (since it is abelian). Therefore (P=W
times Q), where (Qcong P/W) is the point stabilizer, acts faithfully and irreducibly, and is a proper nilpotent quotient by minimality. If (Q) were nonabelian, one of its Sylow factors would supply a nonabelian prime-power quotient of (D), contrary to assumption. Thus (Q) is abelian. The commutative F_p-algebra it generates on the irreducible module is a finite field acting on a one-dimensional module, so (Q) is cyclic. In particular it suffices to settle arbitrary gain profiles in (E_{
m add}
times C_d) for all odd (d), where the cyclic complement acts faithfully and irreducibly. The unrestricted (d=3,5) cases and the translational-gain/moving-closing case for all (d) are now established. This reduction does not resolve composite complements or the other four-orbit topology, and is not Lean-formalized.
