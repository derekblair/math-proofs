# Independent audit of correlated repair padding

**Verdict:** The cancel-refresh identity, adaptive transcript coupling, and exact conditional entropy bound check under the stipulated mathematical and trust model. The optimum is not an unconditional repair-security lower bound and does not apply to hidden repair outputs or full endpoint compromise.

- **Fixed-R entropy.** On a forced first action with the full batch, fix R and vary f_1 over all degree≤t polynomials. Equality of target values permits every translation in the t-dimensional kernel of target evaluation. The weighted t+1-helper coordinate map is invertible, maps that kernel onto the whole sum-zero space, and correctness requires support within that same space. Consequently a single conditional pad law invariant under all these translations must be uniform. The key premise is the actual Markov condition law(P_1 | R,f_1,forced action)=law(P_1 | R,forced action); lack of an API for f_1 is not enough if there is uncounted setup information correlated with f_1. Likewise the conditional polynomial domain must retain every f_1 when R is fixed. The document states both, but make the Markov condition explicit in a theorem formulation. Count setup seeds and every other randomness source, even if they can be shared with the refresh process; conditioned on a forced path, outputs R,P are deterministic functions of the full charged random input, so the data-processing bound applies. The first pad determines F on a forced path since R_1=0 and the nonconstant coefficients survive centering. No implication from privacy only after averaging over R.
- **Adaptive coupling.** For each fixed full history h and policy tape, translating independent uniform F by the nonconstant part of f_1 makes Z uniform given h. Real and simulator locked to this same Z emit exactly the same messages at any common action, hence agree on future actions and outputs by induction. It is not claimed that Z is uniform conditional on an outcome-selected path. Force-path density is correctly multiplied by the observed policy-compatibility indicator, unlike treating incompatible forced paths as zero. The argument can couple ordinary authorized share reads through the same fixed-history oracle, not through access to sampler/pad state.
- **Physical separation and visibility.** The R-aware sampler sees exact protected R and holds its F; it must not receive any real share or required masked message transcript. A holder already knows its recovered share; outsider simulations do not protect against its full buffers or correlated sampler compromise. With a full batch the repaired value is visible by summation, unavoidably. Dynamically selecting helpers or restricting observed messages based on an earlier common view preserves the full-view coupling; selecting indices after seeing hidden inputs or changing when the sum is authorized is a different experiment. No matching t lower bound for partial visibility. Leakage of R can allow accounting for increments and combining known repaired values as evaluations of f_1, so transcript transparency alone has no proactive privacy consequence under such leakage.
- **Stationary corollary.** The sampler trust does not change the distribution of authorized outputs. Hence the earlier reduced-degree independent-refresh interval lower bound still determines D_min in its own sufficiently-large-field regime; separate causal pad lower/upper gives t+2D_min and the correlated construction gives t+D_min. No application beyond those same exposure and refresh assumptions, no physical bandwidth reduction, and no general threshold-KEM or adaptive-target secret-privacy theorem.
