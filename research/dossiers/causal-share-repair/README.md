# Exact entropy for public transcripts of predeclared online Shamir repairs
**Follow-up:** [Adaptive repair targets and optimal online padding](ADAPTIVE_REPAIR.md) extends the padding theorem, not the underlying proactive secrecy theorem. It also proves a separate stationary protected-memory tradeoff. See [independent audit](ADAPTIVE-AUDIT.md).
[Exact fixed-target refresh budget](FIXED_TARGET_SEGMENTS.md): with a known sequence of public repair targets and no other share reads, greedy segments with at most t distinct sites give the sharp additive refresh cost. The joint pad-plus-refresh optimum is t plus the number of segment boundaries. This does not extend to all state-dependent refresh maps or to adaptive underlying secrecy.


Narrow defensive research result. This is a scalar share-repair/refresh mechanism in an ideal bounded-read model, not a KEM, decryption oracle, production holding system, or claim of active security. No real key, network or endpoint was used.

## Model and concrete parties

For E≥1 epochs and t≥1 over F_q, the secret is the constant term of a degree-at-most-t polynomial f_e at epoch e. The nonconstant initial coefficient vector is uniform. Boundary j adds an independent uniform zero-constant polynomial from a fixed public subspace U_j of dimension d_j. All share sites are distinct nonzero field elements. Each repair target a_e is a nonzero share site; targeting zero would disclose the secret in the public repair sum. The schedule of targets and t+1 live helpers h_(e,i), each distinct from the others and from that epoch's target, is fixed and public before provisioning. Helpers can differ between epochs; n≥t+2 permits a repair when one site is absent. There is exactly one scheduled repair per epoch; no claims for arbitrary or adaptively selected outages.

The observed repair traffic is every field message m_(e,i)=lambda_(e,i) f_e(h_(e,i))+p_(e,i), with public Lagrange coefficients lambda, plus fixed schedule metadata. Every lambda is nonzero since the target is not a helper, so the t+1 weighted helper values identify the actual degree≤t polynomial bijectively as abstract linear coordinates. The pad vector at an epoch has sum zero. The repaired holder simply sums the received messages to obtain f_e(a_e); an observer with the full transcript learns exactly the same sum. There is no target-side pad seed, unmasking message, hidden contribution, or confidential recovery link used at repair time. More helpers can be available, but the specified circuit uses exactly t+1 nonzero weighted contributions; redundant wire messages and higher-degree variants are not covered.

An ideal trusted pad sampler, independent of the dealer's actual secret state and of all refresh coins, can precompute one private pad component for every scheduled helper/epoch from a public linear basis. Privately provision those components to the relevant slots offline; this private setup is not in the public repair transcript. The sampler erases its full working seed and values afterward. A helper keeps its designated components in separate protected storage, uses a component once inside its repair operation, then erases it and the local message work buffer. The repaired holder receives no pad material. Storage and private provisioning can require up to E(t+1) scalar slots even when fewer independent coins are sampled; lower entropy does not imply lower total communication or storage. Retained old true shares and refresh coins, pad storage, helper work buffers and private setup traffic are not visible to this adversary. This is a read-only logical-share snapshot model plus repair traffic, **not** a full memory compromise of those endpoints. Access to those components would require a separate design and proof; no assumption of computationally private links by default.

Between repairs a temporal observer may read at most b_e true current shares at epoch e and see all repair messages. The b_e cap applies only to those ordinary snapshot reads. The public repair messages and their sum are an additional observation at a separate phase, even if the target is not eligible for an ordinary snapshot read. If an ordinary read hits the repair target, the value is duplicated, not a second independent exposure. This is one observer with access to both phases, or equivalently observers that pool their views; no separation or non-collusion is assumed. Ordinary reads may be adaptive; the repair schedule is fixed. Exact causal simulation below is pointwise in each true history, so it applies to an observer selecting its later ordinary reads based on earlier simulated transcripts. All communication observed is classical, so a quantum computer postprocessing this same transcript does not overcome an exact distribution equality. No coherent memory interface, runtime tampering, reliable delivery, verifiability or post-quantum private distribution proof is claimed. Neither pads nor refresh maps depend on ciphertext, true shares, or values learned during repair.

## Causal repair-only criterion and sharp pad cost

Let H_e be the vector space of true histories of weighted helper contributions through epoch e (allow the secret to vary). The t+1 helper coordinates in each epoch are an invertible coordinate system. Consequently H_e is isomorphic to the polynomial coefficient of f_1 (including its constant) and the first e−1 increments in U_j. Let Z_e be the direct product of the per-epoch sum-zero message vectors, and K_e=H_e∩Z_e, the valid true-history differences whose entire target-share prefix is zero. Let pi_e denote ordinary truncation to the first e epochs. Then pi_(e−1)K_e ⊆ K_(e−1), possibly strictly. Pad histories P take values in Z_E and are independent of the whole true state; their joint law need not initially be linear or uniform.

**Definition.** Causal repair-only simulation means that for every e and every two valid true histories with equal target values through e, the first e repair message vectors have identical laws. It must hold pointwise even when the two histories differ in future target values. It is equivalent to requiring the law of pi_e P to be invariant under every translation by K_e, since the actual messages are the true contribution vector plus this pad. Equality of prefix laws also yields a sequential simulator: given the target shares as they arrive, use the conditional law obtained from the next prefix distribution, restricted to the simulator's previous messages; positive-probability prefixes agree by the prefix marginal identity. Thus no future share needs to be supplied to the simulator.

**Theorem.** The minimum normalized Shannon entropy H(P)/log_2(q) for causal repair-only simulation is

    t + sum_(j=1)^(E−1) [d_j − rank(eval_(a_(j+1)) restricted to U_j)].

Each displayed rank is either zero or one. The optimum is attained by uniformly sampling a fixed linear subspace W of full pad histories; an arbitrary nonuniform pad law cannot beat it.

Lower bound: write the chain rule for entropy of the pad vectors in chronological order. In epoch 1 the pad law is invariant by K_1 of dimension t: the one target evaluation is a nonzero linear functional on all initial polynomials. In each later epoch e, condition on any attained pad prefix through e−1. Prefix invariance under the subspace of K_e vanishing on that earlier prefix preserves the conditional law on the next component. Every orbit of this translation subspace has size q raised to its dimension and is equiprobable inside that orbit. Hence its conditional entropy is at least that dimension times log_2(q); mixing orbits cannot decrease the bound. The first e−1 true coefficient vectors of an element of that subspace must vanish, since each earlier helper coordinate map is invertible. The only free change is therefore an increment in U_(e−1) with zero at a_e, of dimension d_(e−1)−rank stated above.

Upper bound: start with W=K_E in the whole pad space. Traverse e=E−1 down to 1. At step e, the current pi_eW is exactly pi_e K_(e+1): after step e+1 the projection through e+1 was exactly K_(e+1), and no shorter step has happened yet. Add any linear complement of pi_e K_(e+1) in K_e, lifted to end at e (append zero pad coordinates at later epochs). This forces pi_eW=K_e at this step and preserves containment for already treated longer prefixes. The dimension added equals dim K_e−dim pi_e K_(e+1). Hence dim W equals dim K_E plus the sum of those deficits, equivalently dim K_1+sum_(e≥2)dim ker(pi_(e−1)|K_e), by telescoping. Uniform W has uniform prefix projections, proving the invariance criterion and matching the lower bound. For optional causal generation rather than offline storage, choose a basis consistent with the filtration by first nonzero coordinate and sample each independent coin no later than its first use; this needs a protected sampler or equivalent private provisioning, not a public seed.

**Why simply adding a fake valid history is insufficient for online privacy.** If simulation is permitted to know all target values up front, uniform padding in K_E alone is exact and optimal for *whole transcript* simulation conditioned on the entire trajectory: translation invariance then requires only K_E. It costs t+1+sum d_j−rank(all target evaluations on H_E), but it can expose a future target early. With two different target sites, degree t≥2, and no refresh, pads in K_E arise from one fake polynomial zero at both targets. The observed first helper vector is a coordinate system for the true polynomial plus this fake one. In this abstract model its evaluation at the second target already equals the future target value, even though only the first repair was completed. The first repair's two target-conditioned worlds can therefore have different prefix distributions. Offline cost t−1 is strictly smaller than the true causal minimum t, while the actual secret can still remain private given those two repaired values when t≥2. General causal cost can exceed the offline cost by the sum of the backwards prefix deficits; neither lower bound relies on attacking a deployed system.

## Does the proactive refresh optimum survive?

The repair pad optimum above counts **separate** randomness. Independently, causal repair transparency removes all repair traffic except the repaired target values, which the observer inherently sees. An observed repair does not cost an extra share observation when its target is already among the known current values. It can cost a full additional observed share when distinct; it is never safe to silently exclude the repair output from the exposure budget.

**A fixed protected repair coordinate across all epochs.** Suppose a_e=a throughout. The exact sharp sufficiently-large-field boundary budget condition is, for every contiguous interval I,

    sum_(e in I) b_e ≤ (t−1) + sum_(j internal to I) d_j,

with b_e ordinary-share reads away from a. Sufficiency: choose all refresh subspaces inside the kernel of evaluation at a, so only one target value is ever exposed. Condition on f(a), subtract a fixed zero-constant interpolant for that value, and divide the remainder by 1−X/a. This exactly transforms the remaining state to degree t−1 with same secret and independently uniform initial nonconstant coefficients. The same transformation applies to all zero-at-a refresh directions. Apply the ideal classical interval theorem to this reduced sharing scheme. Values at other sites transform by public invertible scaling. If more than t−1 coins are budgeted at a boundary, surplus coins can be ignored. For exact subspace dimensions assume d_j ≤ t−1 in this construction.

Necessity: conditioning on all observed f_e(a) reveals each increment's value at a as well as the initial evaluation. Subtract fixed representatives of these known evaluations before the same division. The residual refresh directions have dimensions d_j−rank(eval_a|U_j)≤d_j. If the displayed inequality fails, even the budget d_j is insufficient by the reduced classical interval lower bound. Thus a fixed visible repair coordinate consumes one unit of base privacy degree, not necessarily one exposure at every epoch. In a single interval with six consecutive epochs and small refresh d this is a meaningful distinction, not hardware security. Under the optimal choice eval_a U_j=0, the exact causal pad entropy is t+sum d_j versus tE independent per-repair padding. Total pad plus refresh coins are t+2sum d_j instead of tE+sum d_j, in this narrow model.

**Optimizing the joint coin cost in the fixed-target case.** Let m=t−1 and D_min be the smallest sum of effective boundary dimensions satisfying the displayed interval inequalities. The optimal total (pad plus refresh) is exactly t+2D_min: for arbitrary stationary-target refresh directions, write r_j=rank(eval_a|U_j) and set d'_j=d_j−r_j. The exact pad entropy is t+sum d'_j, while refresh costs sum(d'_j+r_j), giving the lower bound. Choose directions vanishing at a to attain r_j=0 and the minimum. Compute D_min by longest paths on epoch indices: put prefix R_r=sum_(j<r)d'_j, add the constraints R_r≥R_l+sum_(e=l)^r b_e−m for l<r and R_(j+1)≥R_j, and start R_1=0. The least feasible prefixes are the longest-path values; single-epoch feasibility is b_e≤m. An oversized d'_j>m may be clipped to m without losing any inequality: split any interval crossing that seam, apply the left and right interval bounds separately, and sum them. Hence there is no conflict with the ambient dimension m. Example: t=5, six epochs, b_e=1, and repeatedly repairing the same endpoint. Two refresh coins suffice at the middle boundary and are necessary from the whole interval (field sufficiently large); coordinated pad entropy is seven and joint cost nine. A separate uniform sum-zero pad per repair alone takes 30 coins, giving joint cost 32 for the same refresh schedule. The saving is in independent field coins, not bandwidth or total stored field elements.

**Fixed two-epoch schedule with distinct nonzero targets.** For t≥2, after observing the two target values the initial-coordinate restrictions have rank two on nonconstant polynomial directions, regardless of the boundary map. The total dimension available to fit a unit-secret shift is at most t+d−2, before ordinary reads. Spend b_1+b_2 further reads greedily exactly as in the classical interval lower bound: while an affine shift is feasible, any member has constant 1 and cannot vanish at all n>t sites; already imposed point equations vanish, so select a fresh one and drop dimension or make feasibility fail. This yields necessary d≥max(0,b_1+b_2+2−t). Sufficiency follows from the same classical two-epoch large-field interval construction with budgets b_i+1, against even all choices of target and ordinary sites. Assume b_i≤t−1. Correlated causal pads work for these distinct targets with exact dimension formula above. No closed-form refresh optimum claimed for a known varying pattern with three or more epochs; exact finite-matrix rank tests still apply.

Classical interval reference argument used here: a view is private iff a unit change of secret can be absorbed by affine mask-coordinate shifts; the greedy rank-drop argument on any violating interval proves necessity. For sufficiency with independent generic boundary columns, write a leak witness as weights on observed site rows; its nonconstant coefficient suffix sums must vanish on those fresh columns. Break at zero suffixes and pick a component with nonzero total constant coefficient, hence at least t+1 distinct sites (here t denotes the applicable degree). The aggregate nonconstant evaluation matrix has rank t, and the dimension of weights satisfying this initial constraint is v−t, where v is component total reads. Each projective weight direction with all inner suffix sums nonzero satisfies all boundary constraints with probability q^(−sum d_j). The interval bound makes the union probability <1/(q−1) per view and component. Union bound over the finite views gives existence for sufficiently large q; no claim of all-field sufficiency.

## Primary comparisons and limits

[Huang–Bruck, Generic Secure Repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00500) treats secure repair, includes target observations among exposed shares, and studies bandwidth with private repair communication and colluding parties. [Laing–Stinson, Repairable Threshold Secret Sharing Schemes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.7190) treats repair information rate and bandwidth. [Proactive Secret Sharing with Constant Communication](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/942) is an important modern comparator for private hardware/communication and mobile adversaries. Zero-sum repair masks, Shamir interpolation, finite linear algebra and proactive refreshing are standard. The narrow candidate is the optimal separate *causal transcript pad entropy* for a fixed sequence of visible repairs, its gap to offline conditioning, and the preservation criterion for stationary visible repair points. Targeted primary-source search did not locate this exact model and formula; priority remains open.

[NIST ML-KEM](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) and [NIST IR 8214C](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8214/c/final) are only comparators. No provisional threshold ML-KEM is offered. It would also need distributed and authenticated pad setup and refresh, secure physical share isolation, active verification and abort handling, real erasure, protected threshold decapsulation and a simulation proof for its actual output interface. The scalar repair primitive does not perform any of those. No unrestricted decapsulation or output exposure is proposed. Exact Neon XHigh was not available in this workspace; no substitute or unpublished external use.

An independent source audit reviewed the proof and scope; that audit is not a full formal verification or a priority check. Synthetic finite-field controls checked 1,000 fixed target schedules plus three exact controls, including that two distinct targets with t=3 and no refresh use two pad coins under offline conditioning and three under causal conditioning. Pinned Lean checks the conditional support-fiber translation and the rank-nullity increment term; it does not prove the full entropy theorem or generic-field existence result.
