# Near-exact nonnegative affine projection order for mixed powered permanents

Let $P(X)=\operatorname{per}((x_{ij}^{q_{ij}})_{i,j=1}^n)$ in independent real variables, with integers $n\ge2$ and $q_{ij}\ge2$. Let $m$ be the least order of a permanent of affine forms with all coefficients nonnegative that equals $P$. Define $w_e=q_e-1$, $Q=\max_e q_e$, and $U=n+\sum_e w_e$. For a reference permutation $R$, let $S=\sum_{e\notin R}w_e$.

**Theorem (written proof).** For every reference $R$, $m\ge S+n$, and independently $\displaystyle m\ge\max_\sigma\sum_i q_{i,\sigma(i)}$ by degree. Optimize the first bound by minimizing the reference weight $\sum_{e\in R}w_e$. Always $m\le U$; when $n\ge3Q+1$, also $m\ge U-Q^2$. For uniform $q=2$, the gap is at most four when $n\ge7$. These lower bounds also hold for fixed-order real nonnegative coefficientwise limits. They do not concern signed projections or unrestricted permanent computation.

## The modular potential and spanning forest

More generally suppose each exponent of variable $x_e$ in every supported monomial of a nonzero polynomial is divisible by an integer $q_e\ge2$. Count only variables that occur in the polynomial and are absent from some fixed reference monomial $M_0$. In any nonnegative affine permanent projection of order $m$, expand each nonzero affine entry as separate parallel weighted edges, one per positively weighted term; label each edge by either its variable or a constant symbol. Every labeled perfect matching contributes a supported monomial by positivity. Retain only supported labeled edges. Choose a labeled perfect matching contributing $M_0$, contract its row-column pairs to vertices, and direct each nonreference edge from the vertex of its row to the vertex of its column. Alternative parallel terms in the same matched cell give loops. Removing the reference edges leaves all remaining arcs on directed simple cycles, by taking the symmetric difference of the reference matching with a matching using the arc. Thus there are no arcs between distinct strongly connected components.

For each counted variable $e$, assign weight 1 to its labeled arcs and 0 to all other arcs in the contracted graph. Switching a directed simple cycle, including a possible singleton loop, gives a valid matching. Since the reference has no $x_e$, the number of $e$ arcs of such a cycle is divisible by $q_e$. The same holds for every directed closed walk by decomposition. In each strongly connected component, define $\phi_e(v)\in\mathbb Z/q_e$ as the weight of a root-to-$v$ directed walk modulo $q_e$. This is independent of choice because a common return walk from $v$ to the root closes both walks. Along any arc, $\phi_e$ stays constant or increases by one, according to its label. Choosing a matching whose monomial uses $x_e$ and taking its symmetric difference with the reference gives some directed simple cycle with positive $e$ weight. Following it visits all $q_e$ residue values of $\phi_e$ in that component.

Choose one spanning tree of the undirected version of each strongly connected component, retaining each tree edge's original directed label. If a counted variable sees all $q_e$ residues in a component, the tree edges changing that coordinate induce a connected graph on the residues. At least $q_e-1$ tree edges are therefore labeled $e$. Each tree edge has at most one counted label, so
$$
m-\#\{\text{components}\}\ge\sum_{e\text{ absent from }M_0}$q_e-1$.
$$
For the powered permanent a reference matching uses $n$ of the $n^2$ variable labels and every other label occurs. Each later argument keeps these potentials and charges the forest excess.

## Good labels and first-discovery charge

Let $C$ be the number of strongly connected components and $T$ the chosen spanning forest, with each tree edge retaining its arc label. Set $s=m-C-S$. A counted (nonreference) label $e$ is **good** if it occurs exactly $q_e-1$ times in $T$, otherwise **bad**. At most $s$ labels are bad.

All good tree edges of a label occur in one component where its coordinate runs through all residues. They form a tree on those residues under projection: the projected edges are connected and there are exactly $q_e-1$, so edge preimages are unique. Therefore a path in $T$ between vertices that agree in that coordinate cannot use the label: its changes would form a nonempty closed trail in a tree, with no repeated edge. Outside that component the label has no forest edges.

Contract every edge of $T$ that does not carry a good label, obtaining a quotient forest $T'$. The contracted edge set $F$ has size $s+\sum_{\text{bad }e}(q_e-1)$. For an original arc its tree path cannot use a good label other than its own, because every other good coordinate is unchanged. Thus after contraction the path for a good arc uses only edges of that label; for any other arc it is trivial.

Consider a closed witness using no bad label, in one strongly connected component, and let $V_z$ be the fiber of its vector $z$ of bad coordinates within that component. Let $D$ count the identifications of these vertices under the contraction, namely the sum of $\max(|H\cap V_z|-1,0)$ over components $H$ of $F$. Then $D\le s$. To see this, root the tree of the witness component in $V_z$, and each other tree arbitrarily. For each bad label choose a tree covering all residues and traverse it parent before child. Each first discovery of a residue other than its root residue uses an edge with that label. This selects $q_e-1$ distinct edges for each bad label, and selections for distinct labels are disjoint. All selected edges belong to $F$. In a component $H$ meeting $V_z$, the child end of each selected edge differs from $z$ in its discovered coordinate, and those ends are distinct. Consequently it has at most $|H|-|H\cap V_z|=|E(F[H])|-(|H\cap V_z|-1)$ selected edges. Other components contribute at most their edge counts. Summing gives $\sum_{\text{bad }e}(q_e-1)\le |F|-D$, and hence $D\le s$.

## The all-orders additive bound

Relabel the original columns so that the reference permutation is diagonal. If $s\ge n-1$, then $m=C+S+s\ge S+n$. Otherwise choose a uniform directed Hamilton cycle on the original $n$ row indices. A particular off-diagonal label lies on the cycle with probability $1/(n-1)$, so the expected number of bad edges is less than one. There is a cycle with only good labels, including the directed two-cycle at $n=2$.

Take a labeled perfect matching in the projection realizing its monomial and compare it with the reference. Any simple switch cycle that adds a new label gives a supported permutation on its own. Its nonreference labels are a nonempty subset of those on the chosen directed cycle. No proper nonempty subset of a simple directed cycle can be completed by original diagonal edges to an original permutation: a chosen edge forces the next outgoing edge around that cycle. Moreover the supported exponents require each new label's full multiplicity. Hence exactly one simple switch cycle contributes all the new labels, with their full powers. The same reasoning applies to a directed cycle on a subset, fixing the remaining rows.

Write $L$ for the sum of the powers of the $n$ chosen good labels. This switch cycle has $L$ distinct good-arc tails. Their images lie in a connected subtree of $T'$ formed from tree paths of all its arcs. That subtree uses only the chosen labels and has at most $\sum(q_e-1)=L-n$ edges. Its at most $L-n+1$ vertices identify at least $n-1$ of the distinct tails. Since all tails lie in one bad-coordinate fiber, $n-1\le D\le s$, a contradiction. Thus $m\ge S+n$.

## At most $Q^2$ below subdivision order

Let $Q_0=\sum_{e\in R}q_e$, so $U=S+Q_0$. If $s\ge Q_0-Q^2-1$, then $m\ge U-Q^2$. Otherwise on the original row indices declare an undirected pair forbidden if either direction is bad. At most $s<Q_0-Q^2-1\le Q(n-Q)-1$ pairs are forbidden.

[Woodall's cycle theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-24.4.739), also [restated as Theorem 2](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/452/download), gives a cycle of length $n-Q$ when $n\ge2Q+3$ and the number of missing pairs is less than $(Q+1)(n-Q-2)$. The latter threshold is at least $Q(n-Q)-1$ when $n\ge3Q+1$, as their difference is $n-3Q-1$. Thus there is such a cycle using no forbidden pair. Its two directed traversals use disjoint good labels and extend to permutations by leaving all other rows fixed.

Each traversal gives one simple switch cycle as above. Each removes every reference variable occurrence on the visited rows: those variables are absent from the target matching, and a switch component with no new label could only realize the reference permutation, so cannot remove any of those occurrences (the target matching adds none). There are at least $Q_0-Q^2$ such reference edges and their contracted vertices are distinct. Thus both switch cycles contain these anchors, belong to the same component, and have the same bad-coordinate fiber by a common anchor. Their induced subtrees in $T'$ use disjoint good labels and therefore share no edge. Inside a forest they intersect in at most one vertex. Every anchor goes to this common vertex, giving $D\ge Q_0-Q^2-1$. The first-discovery charge $D\le s$ contradicts the assumed excess, proving the claimed bound.

## Upper bound and fixed-order closure

For each original edge $e$ of the complete row-column bipartite graph, replace it by an internally disjoint alternating path of length $2q_e-1$, with edge weights $x_e,1,x_e,\ldots,1,x_e$. In a perfect matching the path takes either all its odd-indexed variable edges (using both original endpoints) or all its even-indexed unit edges (using neither). Parity excludes one endpoint, and the two matchings of the remaining path are forced for their respective boundary conditions. The paths in the first state give an original perfect matching. Each original edge creates $q_e-1$ extra vertices on each bipartition side, so the resulting permanent has order $U$ and equals $P$.

For closure at fixed order $m\ge1$, suppose the coefficients of permanents of nonnegative affine matrices converge. Evaluate each matrix at all variables equal to one and choose a largest product matching, of weight $B$ at most the evaluated permanent. If $B=0$ along an infinite subsequence, the limit is zero and has a zero-matrix representation. Otherwise, on the positive subsequence, permute the largest matching to the diagonal and divide each row by its evaluated diagonal. The new evaluated matrix $b$ has diagonal one and every directed simple cycle product at most one, since switching such a cycle cannot improve the matching. Let $s_i$ be the largest weight of a simple directed path from vertex $i$, including the empty path. Prepending an edge $i\to j$ and erasing cycles of weight at most one shows $b_{ij}s_j\le s_i$. Scale row $i$ by $1/s_i$ and column $i$ by $s_i$. This preserves the permanent and bounds each evaluated entry by one, hence each affine coefficient by one. Finally restore the permanent by multiplying one row by $B$. The evaluated permanents converge, so the restored coefficients are uniformly bounded and a convergent subsequence yields an exact nonnegative affine representation of the coefficientwise limit. This is real nonnegative closure, not signed or Zariski closure.

It checks three abstract counting ingredients only, not the graph construction, the Woodall application, the closure proof, or the full theorem.

[Grochow](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v013a018/) derives monotone projection lower bounds from Newton polytope extension complexity. The present argument uses modular exponent information, rather than convex hulls alone. The earlier [Fermat/Hall dossier](https://github.com/derekblair/math-proofs/pull/103) has a different quadratic lower bound and the subdivision and closure ingredients; those ingredients are proved here independently of repository merge state. No priority claim is made.
