# Uniform relative approximation by nonnegative phase-masked determinant squares


Write X² for entrywise squaring, ∘ for the Hadamard product, and log for the natural logarithm. For any S∈C^(n×n) with |S_ij|=1, define its worst input ratio on real matrices X with per(X²)>0:
  R(S)=sup_X |det(S∘X)|² / per(X²).
Uniformly over S, exp(n−o(n))≤R(S)≤n^n/n!=exp(n−O(log n)). Consequently for any fixed ε∈(0,1), every uniform (1±ε) approximation on all real X of per(X²) by Σ_t w_t |det(S_t∘X)|², with w_t≥0 and |S_t,ij|=1, needs exp(n−o(n)) terms. In contrast general polynomial-square upper O(ε^−2 n²log n) holds. No claim about signed mixture or time.

## 1. Worst-input ratio and mixture lower bound

**Upper on the ratio.** First take X entrywise nonzero. Ratio invariant under positive row and column scaling, so use Sinkhorn to make X² doubly stochastic. Hadamard gives |det(S∘X)|²≤product row norm²=1; van der Waerden gives per(X²)≥n!/n^n. For zeros and per>0, approximate X by nonzero real matrices and use continuity. No need to pick a special X.

**Lower on the ratio.** For sufficiently large n, let b be the largest power of two at most (log n)/(10 log log n). Assign rows in successive disjoint groups of size b and find disjoint column groups of size b greedily. For one row group choose its first row i₀. For every unassigned column j, form its b−1 relative phases arg(S_ij/S_i₀j) mod π. Partition [0,π) into intervals of length at most 1/b and product partition into at most (ceil(π b)+1)^(b−1) boxes. When more than (b−1)·number_boxes columns remain, b columns have their phase vectors in a single box. After unimodular row and column gauges (divide columns by first row, then rotate each other row by lower endpoint phase), the b×b selected mask is entrywise of form τ_ij exp(i δ_ij), τ_ij=±1, |δ_ij|≤1/b, and first row exactly 1. Put X on that block equal to τ∘H_b for a real Sylvester Hadamard H_b, and X=0 on cross-blocks. Thus after gauge S∘X on this block differs from H_b entrywise by ≤1/b, so op norm error ≤Frobenius≤1. All singular values ≥√b−1, and contribution to ratio ≥(√b−1)^(2b)/b! since X² is all-one on block. When unable to find another full block, pair the remaining rows/columns singly with X=1 on the assigned pairs, providing factor 1 per remainder. The number of remaining rows is ≤max(b−1,(b−1)·boxes)=o(n) by b≤log n/(10loglog n). After row and column permutations, the matrix is block diagonal, so R(S)≥(((√b−1)^(2b)/b!))^m, where m is the number of full blocks selected. Stirling log of the block ratio is b−O(√b+log b). Since b→∞ and leftover=o(n), log R(S)≥n−o(n); all constants uniform in S.

Given uniform approximation, test any permutation-support 0/1 matrix: every phase determinant squared is 1, so Σw_t≥1−ε. For every t separately choose its adversarial block matrix above; other terms nonnegative, so w_t≤(1+ε) exp(−n+o(n)). Sum gives r≥(1−ε)/(1+ε) exp(n−o(n)); quantify denominator subexponent uniformly. Section 2 below proves the matching sampling upper.

**A finite bound.** for any two rows and ≥3 columns, among their relative phase angles modulo π, two are circular distance≤π/3. Choose three columns arbitrarily, not need more. Greedily partition row pairs and disjoint column pairs while ≥3 columns remain, yielding floor((n−1)/2) blocks. Take local signs so the determinant absolute² equals 2+2|cosθ|≥3, permanent²-entry target2. Gives R(S)≥(3/2)^floor((n−1)/2) without Stirling. Real sign masks with n power of 2 use X=S∘H directly and reach exact upper n^n/n!.

## 2. Matching phase-mask mixture upper at fixed error, via compact normalization and Bernstein
Let B=n^n/n! and choose independent uniform real sign masks S. Expected determinant square equals per(X²), by sign cancellation of distinct permutation monomials. On the compact set T of real X whose squared entries form a doubly stochastic matrix, P=per(X²)≥1/B, and 0≤det(S∘X)^2/P≤B by the earlier ratio bound. This random variable has mean 1 and variance ≤B (Y²≤BY). At any fixed X, the average of k independent masks is within ε/2 of relative target with tail ≤2 exp(−c k ε²/B) by Bernstein for 0<ε<1.

Take a δ-net with representatives in T, δ=ε/(16nB) in Frobenius norm, size ≤(1+2√n/δ)^(n²) because every T point lies in Frobenius ball √n. On the convex set of real matrices with each row norm ≤1, determinant absolute² of any fixed real sign mask and P(X)=per(X²) are 2n-Lipschitz in Frobenius norm: Hadamard bounds determinant and each cofactor by 1; sub-stochastic row sums bound every permanent minor by 1 (expand independent choices and drop collision restriction), so coordinate derivatives each have modulus ≤2. The convexity ensures the whole line segment is in this domain. If at net point Y the error is ≤(ε/2)P(Y), then at X at distance δ the absolute error is ≤(ε/2)(P(X)+2nδ)+4nδ <ε P(X) by P(X)≥1/B. Thus k=O(B ε^−2 n²(n+log(1/ε))) suffices by finite net union bound, giving exp(n+o(n)) terms at fixed ε. Uniformity extends from T to all entrywise nonzero matrices by positive Sinkhorn row/column scaling of X² (all terms covary) and to matrices with zeros by continuity. This completes sharp exponent exp((1+o(1))n) even allowing arbitrary complex unit-phase masks with nonnegative weights; real signs suffice for the upper. Unlike the general polynomial-square sketch upper, here each factor is an evaluable determinant and the count is exponential. This result doesn't rule out pointwise randomized algorithms, signed weights, non-unit amplitudes, non-determinant factors, or other structured designs.


## Context and verification

The expectation identity used above is the squared-determinant estimator of Godsil and Gutman. See [Barvinok, *Polynomial time algorithms to approximate permanents and mixed discriminants within a simply exponential factor*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9704218) and [Costello and Vu, *Concentration of random determinants and permanent estimators*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.1909) for pointwise estimators and their analysis. Pointwise guarantees for a fixed input do not say how many fixed masks can approximate all real inputs at once. The normalization uses [Sinkhorn and Knopp, *Concerning nonnegative matrices and doubly stochastic matrices*](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1967.21.343) and the [van der Waerden permanent bound (Egorychev)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00968054).

This order is specific to unit-entry-modulus masks and nonnegative weights. The polynomial-size positive square upper bound in [the unrestricted polynomial-factor companion](https://github.com/derekblair/math-proofs/pull/107) does not require that each factor be a masked determinant. Neither result is a claim about pointwise randomized approximation or the time needed to approximate a permanent. The exact signed order-four problem in [a separate dossier](https://github.com/derekblair/math-proofs/pull/104) allows signed weights, which the weight extraction here does not cover.
