# Power-of-two cycles in cubic Haar graphs with cyclic Sylow 2-subgroups

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite group with cyclic Sylow 2-subgroups, including odd-order groups. Every connected simple cubic right Haar graph on G has a simple cycle of power-of-two length. More generally, a simple bi-Cayley graph over G has such a cycle whenever each vertex has at least three neighbors in the other orbit. This is a paper deduction using the earlier odd two-orbit theorem and cyclic-abelianization Haar motifs, with new exact quotient charts.

**Companion result.** The ordinary two-rail graph with two noninvolutive marks and the identity matching has the same conclusion when G has cyclic Sylow 2 and noncyclic abelianization. When |G| is twice odd, it also has the conclusion whenever both rail marks are outside the unique odd subgroup, regardless of the abelianization.

## Keep the full 2-part in the quotient

If |G| is even and the Sylow 2-subgroup is cyclic, the sign of the left regular action maps onto C2. Indeed, an element generating a Sylow 2-subgroup permutes G as an odd number of even cycles. The kernel is characteristic, its order has one less factor of 2, and its Sylow 2 stays cyclic. Iterate inside the successive characteristic kernels. This gives a characteristic odd subgroup U, and a Sylow 2-subgroup S maps isomorphically onto G/U. Thus G/U is cyclic of order |G|₂, the full 2-part of |G|. The odd-order theorem of Feit–Thompson makes U solvable, hence G solvable. In particular G' is odd. No transfer theorem is needed.

Recall the motif-lifting implication from the prior odd bi-Cayley proof. An equal theta consists of three internally disjoint paths of one dyadic length m joining the same two endpoints. An equal barbell consists of two disjoint m-cycles and an m-edge connector. If either appears in a quotient graph with a regular odd-deck cover, suppress its equal paths in the lift. A connected component of the compressed lift is a cubic Haar or two-rail graph over a subgroup of the odd deck. Repeated theta voltages and trivial rail voltages give direct dyadic cycles, while the generic cases use the odd bi-Cayley theorem. Expanding the paths multiplies the cycle length by m and preserves simplicity. See [the odd bi-Cayley argument](../doubled-square-odd-deck/eg-all-odd-bicayley-main.md#literal-seeds-and-induction).

## Haar proof

Normalize the three ports to 1,u,v. Connectedness says u,v generate G. For cyclic abelianization, use M=G/G''. If G is nonabelian, M is nonabelian, since otherwise G' would be solvable and perfect. Its three projected ports stay distinct, since a collision would make M cyclic. Its abelianization is cyclic. The existing marked-metabelian classification supplies a literal equal barbell or theta of size at most 64 in this exact quotient. The kernel G'' is odd and the lifting implication applies. If G is abelian and cyclic, the Haar graph is a dihedral Cayley graph and the earlier dihedral theorem applies. See [the needed marked metabelian result](BottomMotifs.md) and the [Dihedral Cayley theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00010-017-0518-3)

If the abelianization is noncyclic, one odd Sylow factor of it is noncyclic, since its 2-primary factor is cyclic. A two-generated finite abelian group with that property surjects onto Q=C_d × C_p², for odd prime p and d=|G|₂. The full d-factor is retained and the residual kernel is odd. Treat the odd d=1 case by the earlier theorem. Rebase the three ports at a majority parity port, so that the two normalized nonidentity ports have odd and even 2-primary images, respectively. Their C_p² projections remain an ordered basis, by generation. Normalize the first 2-primary coordinate to 1 by multiplying by a unit. The resulting marked coordinates are (1,1,0) and (b,0,1), where b is even modulo d. The quotient graph is a regular cover target with distinct ports.

Use integer-coordinate Haar steps on the two layers, with difference (0,0), (1,0), or (0,1) from layer 0 to layer 1. Project (layer,x,y) to (layer,x+by mod d,x mod p,y mod p). The standalone replay verifies the following finite charts and their validity as integer path words where required.

- For p≥7 the integral theta of arm length 16 has widths 5 and 4 in x,y, so its support stays distinct after projecting just the two odd coordinates. For p=5 the same chart is injective after projecting to C2×C5² with b even, which suffices at every d.
- For p=3 and d=2 the finite marked quotient has a literal theta of arm length 8.
- For p=3 and d divisible by 4, the integral theta of arm length 16 works when b≡2 mod 4. A separate integral equal barbell with each of its three lengths 16 works when b≡0 mod 4. Injectivity is checked after reduction to C4×C3². The integer path endpoints and both loop closures agree before reduction, so the charts lift for every bigger cyclic 2-factor in its corresponding residue class.

Thus every noncyclic-abelianization case has an odd-kernel quotient with a usable motif. For the bi-Cayley consequence, retain any three cross ports and discard the other edges. Rescale one port to identity. Each connected retained component is a cubic Haar graph over the subgroup generated by the two normalized differences. Its Sylow 2-subgroups are cyclic because they are isomorphic to subgroups of a cyclic Sylow of G.

## Companion two-rail cases

For noncyclic abelianization, use the same quotient Q and ordered basis, swapping the rails if needed so the first mark is odd. The second 2-primary image b may have either parity. Distinct inverse signs survive in Q because both p-coordinates have odd order p. The replay has integral equal-arm-8 theta charts for p=3 with each parity of b, injective already on C2×C3². At all p≥5 one chart works for either parity and all d by widths 3 and 3. Apply odd-deck lifting. This does not require the arbitrary odd cover theorem for the four-vertex doubled square or looped path.

Now assume |G| is twice odd and both marks are outside U. If G has a nonabelian odd quotient, take a minimal nonabelian odd quotient Q₀. The ordered marks generate Q₀, so neither vanishes. The earlier odd proof supplies a literal motif in the marked two-rail graph of such a minimal quotient, or in a further noncyclic marked seed quotient used in that proof. Keep the 2-part by projecting G onto the product of the odd seed quotient and C2. The map is onto because its projections are onto and the factor orders are coprime. Each rail edge toggles the new sheet, while a matching edge toggles the rail layer. Thus the change in sheet plus rail layer equals path length modulo 2. Even barbell loops close, and all equal theta arms from the same start land in one sheet over the same endpoint. The motif survives intact, and the remaining deck is odd.

If G is nonabelian with no nonabelian odd quotient, take a minimal nonabelian quotient Q₁. It is even. Solvable minimal nonabelian quotients are class-two nilpotent or faithful irreducible affine groups E_add ⋊ C_m, by the earlier classification. The class-two nilpotent branch would have a nonabelian odd Sylow factor, so is excluded here. In the affine case the projected marks have even phase orders. If both are involutions, apply the odd-kernel dihedral two-rail result. If one is an involution and the other is not, the other phase order is 6 or at least 10. These are exactly covered by the existing small or large involution motifs. If neither is an involution, the existing mixed-even affine motifs apply, with the equal-phase companion when appropriate. Every projection kernel is odd, so these motifs suffice. The abelian G case follows from the known abelian result. These companion arguments invoke additional chart classifications not reproduced in this dossier.
