# Linear-time minimal strong subgraphs and dynamic transitive reduction with two fixed feedback anchors

Nov 26, 2026. Restricted algorithms result; no unrestricted record or absolute priority claim. **Inclusion-minimal** means no output arc can be removed without losing required reachability; it is not the NP-hard minimum-cardinality optimization.

Primary baselines: [Han–Kelsen–Ramachandran–Tarjan, §6.2](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~vlr/papers/minspan.pdf) gives O(m+n log n) for the general static minimal strongly connected spanning subgraph problem and proves that the older minimum-augmentation iterative framework can take logarithmically many rounds. [Goranci–Karczmarz–Momeni–Parotsidis, arXiv:2504.18161](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18161), Theorems 1.1–1.2, gives deterministic amortized O(m) for dynamic DAG reductions and O(m+n log n) for unrestricted dynamic digraph reductions, with the same vertex-centered insertion batches and arbitrary deletion batches considered here. Its intra-SCC step explicitly uses the O(m+n log n) general minimal strong subgraph baseline. [Garey–Tarjan (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(78)90015-7) already reports all singleton directed feedback vertex sets in linear time. [Lokshtanov–Ramanujan–Saurabh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04347) already finds a directed feedback vertex set of size ≤k, if one exists, in fixed-k deterministic linear time. The new candidate is the degree-safe two-anchor pruning algorithm and its dynamic lift, not feedback-set detection. Focused primary-source search did not locate this exact inclusion-minimal specialization; priority remains open to review.

## A. Static inclusion-minimal strong spanning subgraph: O(m+n) given or finding two feedback vertices

Remove loops; they cannot be required under reflexive reachability. A strongly connected graph with more than one vertex has indegree/outdegree at least one. If F is a directed feedback vertex set, any spanning subgraph with this positive degree property has every vertex reachable **from** at least one vertex of F and able to reach at least one vertex of F: follow predecessor/successor arcs until F is reached, since an infinite continued walk outside F would contain a forbidden cycle. Thus such a subgraph is strongly connected iff its vertices of F are mutually reachable. For |F|=1 this reduces to local degrees. Scan arcs once, deleting whenever tail outdegree and head indegree are both >1. Feasibility persists and every kept edge was blocked by a degree that never increases, proving inclusion minimality. Singleton input is trivial.

For F={a,b}, find any simple spanning-subset paths P:a→b and Q:b→a. Because their internal vertices lie in the same DAG, their common internal vertices occur in the same order along both paths. If the list is nonempty, replace Q's segment from the first to the last common vertex by P's segment. The paths then share exactly one contiguous internal segment (possibly a single vertex). Their union backbone B is strongly connected on the two anchors; it has at most one vertex with more than one incoming backbone arc (the first common internal vertex), and at most one with more than one outgoing backbone arc (the last). Root degrees are one in each direction. When P and Q have no common internal vertex, both exceptional degree counts are zero.

Protect B initially. Scan all nonbackbone arcs and remove any whose tail has outdegree≥2 and head indegree≥2. The resulting H still connects the anchors and has all degrees positive, so is strong. Every remaining nonbackbone arc is now essential by at least one held incidence of degree one; it remains so after any further deletions preserving feasibility.

Freeze all surviving nonbackbone arcs. At each vertex lacking a frozen incoming or outgoing arc, freeze the unique backbone arc of that type if unique; this is essential by degree in H. For any of the at most two exceptional degrees lacking a frozen arc, freeze just one of its backbone choices arbitrarily, recording it as a potentially redundant lock. The frozen set now alone guarantees all positive degrees. Give cost zero to its arcs and cost one to all remaining optional backbone arcs. Find an a→b and b→a path pair of minimum total **union cost** in H. Keep the entire frozen set and precisely the optional arcs occurring in that union. This graph is strong. Every kept optional arc is essential: if it were removed with strong connectivity preserved, there would be a feasible path pair with union containing fewer optional arcs, contradicting optimality. Lastly try deleting the ≤2 recorded potentially redundant locks by ordinary linear strong-connectivity checks. Every retained lock is essential; an edge already essential remains essential during deletions.

### Minimum union of two directed paths in a split-anchor DAG in linear time

Split a,b into source and sink copies a+,a−,b+,b−, routing the tail of every outgoing arc to the source copy and head of every incoming arc to the sink copy. The new directed graph is a DAG. Feasible simple a→b and b→a paths correspond to paths a+→b− and b+→a−; their arcs correspond one-to-one to original arcs, preserving union cost. The helper below applies to arbitrary nonnegative costs in a DAG with distinct pure sources s1,s2 and distinct pure sinks t1,t2, demands s1→t1,s2→t2.

Let d be directed distance (infinity for absent paths). The optimal union cost is
```
min( d(s1,t1)+d(s2,t2),
     min_{u,v} [d(s1,u)+d(s2,u)+d(u,v)+d(v,t1)+d(v,t2)] ).
```
For any finite candidate, choose witness paths for all its summands and concatenate as specified. Acyclicity prevents a concatenation from revisiting a vertex (apart from the segment joins). Union cost is at most the sum because costs are nonnegative, so every candidate is at least the optimal union cost.

Conversely choose optimal paths. If they have no common vertex then their costs add, and the first candidate is at most their union cost. If they have a common vertex, let u,v be their first and last common vertices in topological order. The two prefixes and suffixes have no shared arc; topological order keeps every prefix edge strictly before every suffix and forbids overlap with edges between u,v. The union of the two u→v subpaths includes at least one u→v route and has cost at least d(u,v). Hence the second candidate indexed by u,v is at most optimal union cost. This also covers u=v, with empty middle path. So the formula is exact.

Compute the four source-forward and sink-backward distances by topological dynamic programming. Seed one additional forward pass at every u with d(s1,u)+d(s2,u), and take the minimum over v after adding the two sink distances. Store predecessors (seeding ties can choose either witness) to reconstruct the two witness paths or the shared five-segment union. Time and space O(m+n). No shortest-path matrix is formed.

All scans of the static solver are linear. Use the cited fixed-k DFVS detection if the two-anchor certificate is not supplied. In the comparison to the general static baseline, graph structure is restricted but no certificate is required.

## B. Fixed global feedback set, fully dynamic exact transitive reduction: O(n+m) deterministic amortized

Assume a fixed supplied F with |F|≤2 remains a feedback vertex set at all times, on a fixed vertex set. Initially empty, as in the cited dynamic baseline; vertex-centered insertions and arbitrary deletion batches preserve the promise. Keep a single published Theorem 1.1 dynamic transitive reduction of the persistent induced DAG D=G−F. Input updates project to one supported batch update in D (or no change). At each update, also compute the SCC decomposition and condensation C of current G in O(n+m), grouping intercomponent arcs by dense SCC-index pair with radix sorting. A condensation edge XY is necessary iff there is no X→Y path of length ≥2 in C. Exactly one original edge suffices for a necessary ordered pair.

For each component A containing an element of F, compute its descendants and ancestors in C, including A. By an extra condensation-arc scan, store for every Y whether an edge V→Y with V≠A has V among A's descendants, and symmetrically for every X whether an arc X→V with V≠A has V among A's ancestors. This costs O(|F|(m+n)).
- If A is neither X nor Y, a genuine indirect path via A exists exactly when X is an ancestor and Y is a descendant of A. Concatenations are valid paths in the DAG.
- If A=X, the first extra array tests a genuine indirect path by its final edge V→Y with V≠X; if A=Y, the second array works symmetrically.
- If X or Y contains F, these endpoint tests alone determine whether the condensation edge is necessary, regardless of where the internal paths run in the original graph. In particular, no same-endpoint alias is mistaken for a true intermediate SCC.
- Otherwise X,Y must both be singleton vertices x,y outside F. An original alternate path avoiding F is exactly an alternate x→y path in D, so it is detected by the absence of xy from the exact maintained DAG reduction (after deduplicating graph arcs). Every other genuine alternate uses some F vertex and is detected by the middle-component case above. Conversely any alternate path in D has a genuine intermediate SCC, since both endpoint SCCs are singleton and D is acyclic. 

Include one original representative edge per necessary condensation pair; inside each SCC use Part A with F∩SCC. The union preserves all reachability and is inclusion-minimal: essential internal edges cannot be bypassed by a path leaving and reentering their SCC, since the condensation is acyclic; representative edges correspond to mandatory condensation links. Store a dense per-ordered-pair bit for membership in D’s explicit reduction (within the underlying O(n²)-space allowance) so the singleton-edge queries take deterministic constant time. Set the bits by traversing the current explicit DAG reduction; clear the same entries after the scan. The dense array needs one-time initialization, not an O(n²) clearing pass per update. The wrapper inherits O(n²) total space. Work external to the one maintained DAG reduction is O(n+m) worst-case per update, yielding O(n+m) deterministic amortized in its benchmark model (charge batches using the baseline convention, plus time reading supplied updates). This removes the log on sparse graphs **under the fixed-global-feedback-set promise**, not for changing witnesses, merely SCC-local certificates or unrestricted graphs. Graphs with fixed two-anchor feedback can still have arbitrarily many vertices in one SCC; the subclass is not bounded SCC size. The dynamic wrapper also works with a larger fixed F in O(|F|(n+m)) additional scans if its SCC intersections have size ≤2, with this additional structural promise, but this is not a gain when |F| is large.

## Verification and caveats

`two_anchor.py` checked 8,004 strongly connected input certificates against independent full reachability and literal single-edge deletion tests, and 2,204 weighted path-union DP cases against full enumeration. The prototype uses convenient Python sets and sorts; the stated asymptotic algorithm uses flat lists, dense graph arrays, Boolean per-arc activity flags, and topological relaxations. `dynamic_lift.py` compares complete reachability plus every edge's necessity at each step of vertex-centered insertion/batch-deletion streams; it deliberately recomputes the DAG oracle by brute force and is a correctness control, not an implementation of the published dynamic routine.

Completed controls: the dynamic lift passed 17,600 intermediate states, covering 23,741 inserted and 21,857 deleted arcs. Lean `TwoAnchor.degree_anchor_connected` checks the central positive-degree + anchor-connectivity implication via ranked forward and backward walks; `permanent_block` and `optional_essential` check monotonicity/count consequences. Standard axioms only. The entire two-path union theorem and dynamic graph complexity are written proofs and independently finite-tested, not fully Lean-formalized.
