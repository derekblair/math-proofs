# Dyadic cycles in cubic Haar graphs of class six

## All finite groups through class six

[HaarClass6Finite.any_ports](HaarClass6Finite.lean) verifies that every cubic Haar graph over a finite group of nilpotency class at most six has a simple power-of-two cycle of length at most 64 times the Sylow 2-subgroup order, for any three distinct ports. [HaarClass6Odd.any_ports](HaarClass6Odd.lean) gives 64 as an upper bound when the group order is odd. If the normalized ports generate an exact-class-six subgroup in the odd case, a simple 64-cycle always exists. No restriction on divisibility by three remains.

For odd order, reduce to the subgroup generated by the normalized ports. If its class is lower, use the preceding class-five theorem. If either projected third commutator does not cube to one, use the original parallel-loop word. If both cube to one, the third-layer image is either rank two, where the quartic comparison resolves it, or cyclic order three. In the cyclic case the full third subgroup is abelian in class six. A spliced word and two permutations or rotations cover all 36 projected quartic parameter cases; the exact words close in the original group and the projected tables prove injectivity. Every case, including subgroup restriction, the two-primary voltage lift, and collapsed ports, has been checked in Lean. The unrestricted Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture remains open.

## Prime-to-three precursor


[HaarClass6FiniteNoThree.any_ports](HaarClass6FiniteNoThree.lean) proves that if a finite group G has nilpotency class at most six and 3 does not divide its order, then any three distinct Haar ports force a simple power-of-two cycle of length at most 64 times the order of a Sylow 2-subgroup. For odd-order G this gives a simple dyadic cycle of length at most 64. If the normalized ports generate a subgroup of exact class six, the odd-order proof gives the 64-cycle directly. This precursor does not use either of the order-three branches.

[HaarClass6HighGirth.any_ports](HaarClass6HighGirth.lean) also handles arbitrary group order, including infinite groups: if a cubic three-port Haar graph over a group of class at most six has no even simple cycle of length 4 through 32, it has a simple 64-cycle.

The new 64-port word uses alternating walks A and B of lengths 12 and 20, followed by their reverse walks. Their values agree as B = A² modulo the fourth lower-central subgroup. The error term therefore centralizes A in class six, so the full 64-step walk closes even without a girth premise. The formal class-three projection tables and odd order force all same-side vertices to be distinct when the projected third commutator V does not cube to one. A port swap treats U instead. The high-girth result uses the same closed word, with cyclic nonbacktracking checked separately. The even-order finite extension uses the existing Sylow-2 voltage lift. This is not the full Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture.

Burkhart's [odd-order Cayley theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05060v1) is about ordinary Cayley graphs, not cubic three-port Haar graphs. Exoo and Jajcay's [nilpotent voltage bound](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195669810001757) is a girth bound, not a dyadic-cycle conclusion. No absolute priority is asserted.

## Girth at most 40, with no group-order hypothesis

[HaarClass6GirthUpper.any_ports](HaarClass6GirthUpper.lean) gives an even simple cycle of length at most 40 for every group of class at most six, including infinite groups. The 40-step closed word is a cyclically reduced Wicks-form commutator: its two 12-step values agree modulo γ₄ and lie in γ₃. In class six, γ₄ commutes with γ₃. The short closed walk therefore contains a simple cycle. This is an ordinary girth bound, not a claim that the cycle of length at most 40 is dyadic or that 40 is optimal. The cited general Exoo–Jajcay bound specializes to 98 on the three-edge dipole in class six.

## A class-six case with order-three commutators

[HaarClass6RankTwoActual.cycle](HaarClass6RankTwoActual.lean) applies when the two normalized ports generate a group of exact class six and the two projected third commutators have no nontrivial multiplicative dependence modulo three, with the first commutator cubing to one in the class-three quotient. In particular this covers the rank-two elementary abelian third layer of order nine. Three explicit words close in the original group. Their remaining collisions are separated in the fourth layer by comparisons of three commutators. The rank-one case uses a separate spliced-word certificate as described above.

## No nilpotency-class cutoff when the third layer subgroup is abelian

[HaarAbelianThirdFinite.any_ports](HaarAbelianThirdFinite.lean) extends the prime-to-three result to any finite nilpotent group whose subgroup γ₃ is abelian. [The metabelian corollary](HaarAbelianThirdFinite.lean) follows because γ₃ is contained in the derived subgroup. The bound is 64 for odd order and 64 times the Sylow 2-subgroup order in general. Nilpotence rules out stabilization at a nontrivial γ₃; abelianness of γ₃ makes the two 64-word constituent loops commute regardless of the class. This precursor excludes orders divisible by three; the next theorem removes that restriction.

## All finite nilpotent groups with abelian γ₃, including all finite nilpotent metabelian groups

[HaarAbelianThirdAllFinite.any_ports](HaarAbelianThirdAllFinite.lean) removes the prime-to-three restriction from the unbounded-class result. There is no fixed class bound. For any finite nilpotent group with abelian third lower-central subgroup and any three distinct ports, there is a simple dyadic cycle of length at most 64 times the Sylow 2-subgroup order. For odd order the bound is 64. [HaarAbelianThirdAllFinite.metabelian](HaarAbelianThirdAllFinite.lean) specializes this to all finite nilpotent metabelian groups.

To pass beyond class six, the two short loops and the rank-one splices close using ambient abelianness of γ₃, not the former class-six commutation argument. If the nilpotent descending series stabilized at a nontrivial subgroup it would never reach the identity, which gives the required nontrivial projected layers. Hence the same exact rank certificates separate the chosen vertices for arbitrary class. The finite even-order case again uses the Sylow 2 quotient and the voltage lift. This statement does not cover arbitrary metabelian groups that are not nilpotent.
