# Exponential lower bounds for positive permanent square length
Let B_n have vertex set S_n and adjacent permutations differing by exactly one nontrivial cycle. For all n≥1, any real orthogonal representation by nonzero vectors has dimension at least Ω(1.971^n), and at least 2^(floor((n−1)/2)−1) explicitly. Consequently the same lower bounds hold for number of real squares in P_n=per((x_ij^2)); for complex modulus squares divide by two (real/imag). No restriction on supports/sparsity or original degrees. To see the support restriction, give exponents a generic separating linear weight. The maximal monomial among all summands contributes its positive squared leading coefficient to the maximal weight of the sum, so it cannot cancel. This puts every summand support in half the target Newton polytope, which is the Birkhoff polytope. Its only lattice points are permutation matrices. Gram diagonal is one, and an edge has Gστ=0 because product mσmτ has only one unordered factorization when their union differs on one alternating cycle.

## Matrix transfer (all details)
Take any real nonzero entrywise nonnegative PSD matrix K indexed by S_n, zero on edges of B_n. Define S=sum K>0, T=tr K>0, N=n!, d=S/(NT)≤1 (the Rayleigh quotient of the all-ones vector is at most the top eigenvalue, which is at most T). Regard pair (σ,τ) as probability Kστ/S, relative g=στ^-1. Let F_k(g)=(fix(g))_k = number of ordered distinct k-tuples fixed by g. For each ordered domain tuple I, split by ordered image J; K_{I,J} is a principal block with ambient permutation dimension (n−k)! after row/column permutation to put common assignments last. Trace sums over J=T for each I; omit zero-trace blocks (PSD implies zero). Averaging d(K_{I,J}) over I uniform and J weighted by tr(K_{I,J})/T gives exactly d(K) E F_k: pair agrees on precisely F_k tuples and (n)_k(n−k)!=n!. Thus if E F_k>c^k, some block gains >c^k density. Zero pattern inherited and support in one parity preserved because relative permutations fix the assigned positions and row/column relabeling multiplies every parity by one common sign.

**Initial parity restriction.** K gives a real Gram representation Kστ=<wσ,wτ>; writing u_± as sums of w over each parity, diagonal block sums add to ||u_+||²+||u_-||²≥||u_++u_-||²/2=S/2. Choose a principal parity block, then pad rows and columns for other parity with zeros, keeping N indexing. Trace-weighted average of densities obtains ≥d/2. For even n, reduce by one using E F_1≥1: perm character = trivial plus standard, both positive-definite kernels; for any unitary ρ, matrix χρ(στ^-1)=Tr(ρ(σ)ρ(τ)^*) is a PSD Gram, so E χρ≥0 by Frobenius PSD pairing. Parity preservation after relabeling holds regardless of k; thereafter take even k only to preserve odd order.

## Asymptotic transfer from an external LP certificate
Centralize pair-difference probability to φ. Set xλ=E χλ≥0, x_trivial=x_sign=1 and xλ=xλ^T since differences even. Young permutation character on ordered k-tuples equals F_k, so at even-moment terminal E F_k≤c^k all Young constraints hold exactly. Writing φ(g) for the probability of a particular element after centralization, character orthogonality gives φ(g)=(1/m!) Σλ χλ(g)xλ (characters are real). φ vanishes at all single cycles. Thus x with Ψ_ℓ=0 and M=0 is feasible for Coregliano–Jeronimo LP I at any valid ℓ₀ (Def 4.4). [Coregliano and Jeronimo](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.05841), §5.4 Table 1 and the proof of Theorem 1.3, certify a positive LP III optimum at c=1.971, ℓ₀=74, k₀=469, m₀=1086. Their Propositions 4.22 and 4.17 transfer this to positivity of LP I at c=1.971 for all sufficiently large odd m; take the threshold greater than ℓ₀+1. This is stronger input than just their upper bound on independence, which alone does not imply the PSD transfer. Peel strict failures for even k until terminal odd m<n₀; after initial decrease n→n'∈{n−1,n}, d(K)/2 · c^(n'−m) ≤ d_terminal≤1, hence d(K)≤2c^(n₀+1)c^(−n). No need to reduce exponent by choosing c<c₀ when allowing an absolute threshold-dependent constant. Equivalently, Schrijver's theta prime satisfies θ′(B_n)=O(n!/1.971^n), using the trace-one, entrywise nonnegative PSD formulation with zeros on edges.

If G is a unit Gram of orthogonal representation in rank r, K=G∘G is nonnegative PSD, T=N, S=||G||_F²≥N²/r (Cauchy on eigenvalues). Therefore d(K)≥1/r. For complex Hermitian Gram use G∘conj G; dimension bound separately applies to complex graph orthogonal representations as well (not needed for P_n claim). No lower bound on general signed SOS, determinant circuits, or permanent computation time.

## Standalone explicit √2 proof, no LP solver
Use c=√2 in same transfer. If odd terminal m≥3, identity of Fourier character at an m-cycle says 0=Σ_{j=0}^{m−1} (−1)^j x_j where x_j=E χ_(m−j,1^j)≥0, x_0=x_(m−1)=1, x_j=x_(m−1−j); coefficient zero for nonhooks by Murnaghan–Nakayama. Half the sum of odd-j entries (paired by transposition, middle counted at most once after dividing by 2) is at most Σ_{odd 1≤j≤(m−1)/2} x_j. We show this <1, contradiction, regardless of other positive even contributions.
For hook leg j, Young hook permutation module on ordered k-tuples contains hook with multiplicity C(k,j) when j≤k≤m−1, by branching from S_(m−k): the skew from a first-row strip has j vertical and k−j horizontal boxes, interleavings arbitrary. Together with the trivial coefficient 1 and positivity, terminal k=2 yields x1≤1/2. If m≥7, k=6 yields 1+6x1+20x3≤8, so x1+x3≤(7/10)(1/2)+7/20=7/10. If m=3 or 5, only j=1 in the half-range, already <1. For odd j≥5 in half-range, k=2j yields x_j≤t_j:=2^j/C(2j,j). Successive odd t ratio t_(j+2)/t_j=(j+1)(j+2)/((2j+1)(2j+3))≤42/143<3/10 (each factor decreases in j; explicit polynomial cross-multiply alternative). t5=8/63, so sum tail ≤(8/63)/(7/10)=80/441<3/10. Total <1. Therefore peel to m=1, d(K)≤2·2^(−floor((n−1)/2)), gives explicit lower bound r≥2^(floor((n−1)/2)−1). The n=1,2 formula is harmless weaker than 1.



## Finite exact certificates and reproducibility

The included Node.js replay independently evaluates characters by Murnaghan–Nakayama and a Frobenius coefficient formula. Here is how its rational certificates give finite bounds. For a matrix K as above, define a_μ as the sum of K entries over ordered pairs of conjugacy class μ divided by T. The identity class has a_μ=1; single-cycle classes have zero mass. Pairing K against the character kernels yields d_λ + Σ_μ χ_λ(μ)a_μ ≥ 0, where the sum excludes the identity and d_λ=χ_λ(1). For nonnegative dual weights y_λ with Σ_λ y_λχ_λ(μ)≤−1 in every eligible nonidentity class, sum these inequalities to get S/T≤1+Σ_λ y_λ d_λ. The Gram-square transfer therefore gives rank at least the ceiling of n! divided by that bound. These are lower bounds, not exact square lengths.
