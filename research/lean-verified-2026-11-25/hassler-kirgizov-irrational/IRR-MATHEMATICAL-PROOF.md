# Irrational limiting popularity (Hassler–Kirgizov, §5 question 3)
This is a distinct posed open question, not another conjecture and not the already-delivered 5.1. Primary https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02442, §5 q.3; authors' peer-reviewed version also asks it, https://www.kirgizov.link/publications/emerging-avoidance.pdf. Exact title plus irrational + Fibonacci/2143/tilings searches found no prior solution. Recheck math-proofs and indexed papers before delivery.
 
Forbid all consecutive length-4 patterns except 1234,2134,1324,1243,2143 (the patterns with no inverted nonadjacent positions). For n>=4, for every i,j at distances 2 and 3 one can find a consecutive window covering them, so they are in increasing order. Transitivity implies all pairs at distance >=2 are in increasing order (every integer >=2 is a sum of 2s and 3s). Descents at consecutive i cannot overlap and each swaps consecutive values of the underlying identity: every other pair is forced ascending, and ranks uniquely specify the permutation. The avoidance class is thus in bijection with monomer–dimer tilings of [n]. Conversely every such tiling's permutation has valid windows.

The class size is F_{n+1} for F_0=0,F_1=1, by a recursion on the first tile. A 2143 occurrence at position i is precisely two dimers occupying i,i+1 and i+2,i+3 (zero-based), forcing breaks before and after the block. Independent prefix/suffix tilings give F_{i+1} F_{n-3-i} choices. With z=n−4, total occurrences = sum_{i=0}^z F_{i+1}F_{z-i+1}. The exact density (divide by n times |class|) tends to 1/(phi^3 sqrt(5)) = 1−(2/5)sqrt(5), irrational. Binet's formula proves this: write r=psi/phi, whose absolute value is <1. The sum becomes phi^(z+2)/5 * ((z+1)(1+r^(z+2)) − 2 sum_{i=0}^z r^(i+1)); the finite geometric sum stays bounded while the first term grows linearly. Normalize by (z+4)F_{z+5}. The frequency is about 0.10557.
