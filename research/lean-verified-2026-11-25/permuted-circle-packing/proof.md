# The density of shortest nondegenerate permuted circle packings

Let (n(r)) be the least integer
(n\ge 2) for which a permutation of (1,\dots,n) gives centers of radius-(r)
disks with disjoint interiors. For every choice of such a minimizing
permutation, the sum of the disk areas clipped by ([1,n(r)]^2), divided
by the square's area, tends to (pi/(2\sqrt 3)).

This answers the intended positive-area reading of Bevan, Homberger and
Tenner, *Prolific permutations and permuted packings*, Question 5.5. A
literal size-one packing, which is allowed by their Definition 3.2, has a
zero-area domain in Question 5.5; without the nondegeneracy convention
that quotient is not defined.

**Construction.** Put (m=\lceil r\rceil\ge1),
(b=\lceil\sqrt3 m\rceil+1), (q=2m+1), and (M=qb-m). Consider the
integer lattice generated by ((q,1)) and ((m,b)). For a lattice
vector ((x,y)=a(q,1)+c(m,b)), (qy-x=Mc).
If (x^2+y^2<4m^2), then (|x|,|y|\le2m-1), so
(|qy-x|\le(q+1)(2m-1)<2M), using (b\ge m+1).
Thus (c=0,1) or (-1). In the first case (x=qa) implies (a=0).
In the second case (x=qa+m) forces (a=0) or (-1);
then (|x|\ge m) and (|y|\ge b-1\ge\sqrt3m), a contradiction.
The last case follows by negating the vector.

Since ((q,1)-2(m,b)=(1,1-2b)) and
(q(m,b)-m(q,1)=(0,M)), the points
((i,(1-2b)i\bmod M)) for (0\le i<M) lie in this lattice.
They form a permutation because (q(1-2b)=1-2M).
Their mutual distances are at least (2m\ge2r).
In particular the minimum exists, and
(n(r)\le 2\sqrt3 r^2+O(r)).

**A local area bound.** Put (R=2r/\sqrt3). Given any finite point set
with separation at least (2r), clip each Voronoi cell to the disk
of radius (R) about its center. For the cell centered at the origin,
the removed part due to another center (b) is the cap
({z:|z|\le R, |z-b|<|z|}). It is contained in its radial shadow,
the sector of points along directions whose point on the radius-(R)
circle is strictly closer to (b) than to 0. This follows because
(2z\cdot b>|b|^2) persists when (z) is moved radially outward.

The shadows of two different neighbors do not intersect. Otherwise a
point (w) on the radius-(R) boundary would be at distance (R)
from the origin and at distance less than (R) from both neighbors.
For three centers (a_1,a_2,a_3), their sum of squared mutual distances
is (3\sum_i |a_i-w|^2-|\sum_i(a_i-w)|^2), which here would be
strictly less than (9R^2=12r^2). Separation gives at least (12r^2).

A nonempty cap subtends an angle (	heta\le\pi/3), since its bounding
line is at distance (|b|/2\ge r=R\sqrt3/2) from the origin.
Its area is (R^2(\theta-\sin\theta)/2) and the shadow area is
(R^2\theta/2). Concavity of sine on ([0,\pi/3]) bounds the cap area
by (1-3\sqrt3/(2\pi)) times its shadow area. The disjoint shadows
have total area at most (pi R^2); the resulting clipped Voronoi cell
thus has area at least (2\sqrt3r^2).

**Lower bound on cardinality.** For a board of side (n-1) with
(n\ge2) centers, the cells are disjoint except for tie lines of area
zero and lie in a square of side (n-1+2R). Therefore
(2\sqrt3 n r^2\le(n-1+2R)^2). Any pair of centers also gives
(2r\le\sqrt2(n-1)), and hence (n\ge r). Divide by (n) and expand
to obtain (n\ge2\sqrt3r^2-O(r)).

**The exact density in the question.** In a permutation packing,
at most (4\lceil r\rceil) disks can spill outside the square: each
strip of width (r) along an edge contains at most (\lceil r\rceil)
centers, since each row and column has exactly one center. The clipped
area divided by ((n(r)-1)^2) is between
((n(r)-4\lceil r\rceil)\pi r^2/(n(r)-1)^2) and
(n(r)\pi r^2/(n(r)-1)^2). Both bounds tend to
(pi/(2\sqrt3)), uniformly over the choices of minimizer.
